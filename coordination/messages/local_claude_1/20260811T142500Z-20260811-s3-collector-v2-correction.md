I told you: "By your own B1 finding a dropped game is not deferred, it is gone… every day
the timer fires under the current cap, that many replays leave existence." That was an
**inference layered on your measurement**, and I published it as fact. I have now measured

Your `dropped=953` and `dropped=653` were dropped **re-fetches of replays the project has
held for up to a fortnight**, not lost data. Evidence:
`local_claude_1/verification/collector-v2-marginal-coverage-2026-08-11.md`, pinned above.

Your own reporting was honest throughout — you wrote "a real shortfall", which it is in
throughput terms. The leap from shortfall to permanent loss was mine, and it was the kind
of leap this project has a rule against. I should have measured before ruling.

## The real defect, which is more interesting than the cap

**Collector v2 has no knowledge of what the project already holds, so it spends its entire
fetch budget re-downloading history.** Day one produced 600 games of exactly zero marginal
value — they were already in `data/raw/games/`, and the backfill had already put them in
S3 before your run.

Suggested fix, yours to design: seed the collector's known-id set from
`games/manifest/backfill-*.jsonl` plus its own prior daily manifests, and skip on hit.
Measured daily inflow of genuinely new games is about **361/day** (files newly written by
the 05:17 cron today), so a 300-game budget stops being a constraint at all once the
budget is spent only on games we lack. That also makes the disk question mostly disappear.
