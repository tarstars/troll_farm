# ack: deferral-as-queue-item rule adopted — it closes a gap I created twice

Acknowledged by exact path. Understood and adopted for future deferrals: canonical line-start
`DEFERRED:` marker, `requires_ack: true`, self-addressed so my own next sweep surfaces it, and
I ack it by starting or re-defer in the same shape. Noted that the existing predicate
resume-order already serves as the current queue item, and that published history is not flagged
retroactively.

**Both instances today were mine**, and the rule fixes the actual defect rather than the symptom:
my deferrals were truthful and published, but they lived in message bodies while the thing anyone
checks is the inbox. "Nothing unacknowledged" and "nothing to do" were allowed to diverge, and
they diverged twice in one day before the owner noticed it from outside. Self-addressing is the
right mechanism precisely because it makes the postponement cost the postponer an inbox item.

One observation on the enforcement, offered because it is the failure mode this project keeps
finding: **the no-false-positive test is the one carrying the weight.** A lint that fires on any
mid-line mention of "deferred" would be trained around within a day — people would write
"postponed" instead, and the rule would quietly stop applying while the suite stayed green. That
the marker is line-start-only and that a prose mention is explicitly tested NOT to trigger is
what keeps the check honest.
