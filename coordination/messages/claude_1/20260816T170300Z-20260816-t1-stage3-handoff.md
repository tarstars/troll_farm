# handoff: T-1 stage 3 (idle-yield) — 0 FIXED / 34 and ZERO rows changed. The feature cannot reach its target population.

## For the owner, in plain terms

The second of the three troll-coordination fixes is built: an idle troll now steps aside when
another troll wants the square it is standing on.

**It changed nothing at all** — not one of the 34 situations moved, on either measure.

The reason is worth more than the result. Our idle troll usually is not standing on the *place the
other troll is going*. It is standing *in the corridor on the way there*. My rule only looks at
destinations, so it never notices a troll blocking the route — which is precisely the situation
the feature was meant for.

## The measurement

Artifact `853dc8b2`, pushed and remote-verified. **0 FIXED / 34**, and a row-by-row diff against
stage 2 shows **0 rows changed** on either clause. The two detector-silent rows are the same
OSC-008 and OSC-012 that stage 2 produced.

## Why — a design fault, not a tuning problem

My `wanted` set is built from the **MOVE targets** of the other units' chosen commands. A MOVE
target is a **goal** cell. The frozen library's dominant mechanism is **M1, "blocker on route"** —
the idle troll sits on the **path**, not on the goal. So the contested test never fires for the
population the feature exists to serve.

**This is the same conceptual error `codex_1` caught in the viewer as V1:** *a command target is
not the next cell.* I accepted that finding, wrote it into the viewer, and then built a planner
feature on the opposite assumption.

## The consequence, which is the part that needs a ruling

Detecting a route-blocker requires knowing the **path**. Computing the path inside the bot means
mirroring `engine.rs::next_cell` — **the exact mirror I refused to build for the viewer**, on the
grounds that a mirror disagreeing with the authority is worse than none. `next_cell` returns the
target only when `d <= speed` and otherwise steps part-way, and its result also depends on
simultaneous resolution against the opponent.

So idle-yield as specified in the charter has two honest futures, and neither is mine to pick:

- **(i)** accept a path mirror inside the candidate bot, with the divergence risk stated and
  tested against the authority; or
- **(ii)** drop yield and let **swap** (stage 4) carry the corridor cases — which the prediction
  registry already expects for the working-blocker group, where yield would interrupt the worker
  anyway.

I lean **(ii)**: it needs no mirror, and the registry's own reasoning already assigns those cases
to swap. But this changes what T-1 delivers, so it is your call or the owner's.
