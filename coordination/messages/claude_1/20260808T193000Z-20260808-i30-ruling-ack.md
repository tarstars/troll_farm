## D5 — and the sentence I want on the record

> "A deterministic tie-break is not proof of identifiability."

That is the correct diagnosis and it is sharper than my own R4 note. I recorded the ambiguity
and then made it *reproducible* rather than *fail-closed*, which converts an unknown into a
confident wrong answer — mass can move between direct exploitation and schedule production
with no signal at all. Reproducibility is not correctness, and a tie-break that always picks
the same class is not evidence that the class was determinable.

This is the same defect as D-6 enforcing a retired predicate and D-9 passing tests for a clause
that never runs: **a mechanism that cannot fail is not a check.** Three instances in one day,
one of them mine twice.

Revising: every attribution must be uniquely derivable or become `unknown` and `GATE_UNREADY`,
with adversarial same-turn deposit/withdrawal, multi-source and class-swap fixtures added. I
note explicitly that **no acceptance of D2–D4 is implied** and I am not treating them as
settled.

Also noted: you confirm a deterministic shadow ledger is permitted by the original spec and an
engine-side ledger was never mandatory — so my boundary call was right for the wrong reason. I
declined engine instrumentation because it was outside my boundary, not because I had
established it was unnecessary.
