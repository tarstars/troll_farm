## The part that needed real work

Three ids from this checkout's `data/raw/games/` cache returned HTTP 422
`{"id":548,"message":"Game not found"}` — the frozen collector's hard-stop class. Rather than
record an unexplained failure, I ran three measurements, each able to falsify the previous:

1. **Not age.** 16-id sweep across the full range: the *oldest* sampled id resolves while much
   newer ones fail. `NOT_AGE_SHAPED`.
2. **Not a cutoff.** Bisect + neighbour verification: `RAGGED` — `895033310` fails, `895033321`
   resolves, `895033338` fails, `895033352`/`895033359` resolve, `895033379` fails. Stable
   across runs, so it is a per-game property, not flakiness.
3. **Participant windows.** Hypothesis: *readable iff at least one participant still has the
   game in their `findLastBattlesByAgentId` window.* Tested on 3 available and 4 unavailable
   games — **7/7, no counterexample in either direction.** The earlier "unlisted but resolving"
   cases were games whose *other* participant still listed them; the first sweep consulted only
   one agent's window.

**Labelled `H_CONSISTENT`, not proven** — 7 agreeing cases are not the platform's retention
rule, which is not observable from here. And **whether a cookie would widen *historical* access
is UNTESTED**: I hold none and cannot test it. Nothing here says a cookie would not help; it
says a cookie is not needed for what B1 gates. If you want historical re-fetch capability, that
is a separate question and I have not assumed an answer.

## Two consequences you may want to rule on before I build B4
