## What is actually new, in one paragraph

`M` is gone, so the rule cannot be a score multiplier — a multiplier with no margin is a
**restriction of the troll's candidate list** to the candidates carrying its kept goal. That is the
shape my own r1 §4 argued against on the two-unit joint path, and the objection does not go away
because the charter changed. So r4's content is the **mechanics that make a restriction safe**, not
the rule sentence, which is now one line. The rule text (§1) contains no numeral except the zero in
"non-empty".

## The five things I am asking you to rule on

1. **`DONE_ON_CHOP` (§3.2) — a finding against the charter's own wording.** The charter's release
   list says *done — chopped … there*. If a `CHOP` at `c` discharges a `Tree(c)` goal, the mover
   releases on the very turn it arrives and chops, re-picks from scratch the next turn, and that is
   the champion's behaviour and the loop's mechanism (the goals stay with the cells). The loop proof
   would hold for exactly one turn past the exchange. **I propose a `Tree` goal has no `done`
   predicate and completes only as *gone* — the tree felled.** A chop is progress *toward*, not
   completion *of*. Both readings are specified; if you rule the charter's literal way I will say in
   the packet that I do not have the loop proof, rather than build to one I cannot show.
2. **R4(c), both fallback forms (§4.2, §4.3).** `best_pair` stays `None` when every restricted
   cross pair is rejected — reachably, when both units are restricted to `PICK` candidates for the
   same item with `inventory[item] < 2` (`stock_compatible`, `:931`). The champion then falls
   through to the greedy path (`:973`) where `.unwrap_or_else(Self::wait)` (`:989`) hands a troll
   `WAIT`. **This is the parking failure mode, and the bonus form made it structurally
   impossible.** My replacement: re-run `select` on the unrestricted lists for that turn (two-unit
   form), or a two-phase greedy where a unit parked by the restriction is re-decided with its full
   list (`>= 3` form); no goal released, both counted as `xn=`/`xp=`/`xg=`. **If this is wrong, the
   rule is wrong** — it is the replacement for the guarantee the correction removed.
3. **Valid-but-not-live goals are preserved, not erased (§2).** The planner declining to offer a
   goal for a turn is not the goal becoming invalid, and erasing on it re-creates the loop (the
   exchange turn *is* a not-live turn). The price is named rather than discovered: a chopper whose
   carry fills goes not-live, banks unrestricted, and then **walks all the way back** even if a
   better tree stands by the shack. Under the bonus that cost was bounded by `M`; under absolute
   keep it is unbounded, and it is the single largest own-score risk of the no-margin form. The
   alternative is written as a switch (`ERASE_WHEN_NOT_LIVE`) so you can take it in one word; I am
   not proposing to measure both, which would be a second candidate rather than a knob.
4. **v6: `k=` becomes three-valued and `m=` is deleted (§5).** `k=2` restricted and emitted its
   goal, `k=1` valid-but-not-live, `k=0` no goal — two-valued would hide exactly the case item 3 is
   about. There is no margin constant left to stamp a wire with, which is itself the version's
   signature. Mutual refusal is asserted **three ways** against v4 and v5.
5. **Plan-keeping needs no new machinery (§7)** — and this is a prediction, not a claim. The
   champion's regeneration `PICK` already carries `target: Target::Cell(unit.cell)` (`:1795-1798`,
   `:1964-1967`) — the **plant site**, not the pick; the `PLANT` for that cell carries the same
   target (`:1916-1927`); the bank clause's `DROP` carries `Target::Bank(cell)` (`:590-610`), a
   different one. So R4(b) alone forbids Candidate 0's `PICK`↔`DROP` two-cycle, with no clause
   about regeneration and no reference to Candidate 0's fallback. **`m061` at G-2 is the test, and
   if the cycle is still there this paragraph is wrong.**
