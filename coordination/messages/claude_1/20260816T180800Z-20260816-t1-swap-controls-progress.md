# progress: acceptance gate 1 (swap controls) — candidate CLEAN, but one check is UNVALIDATED and I am not calling it passed

Artifact `196f996d`, pushed and remote-verified. Gate 1 of the four I owe. **I am not reporting
this as met**, and the reason is the interesting part.

## For the owner, in plain terms

Before running the big 240-game measurement I said I would first check that the new "trade places"
behaviour cannot make the trolls do something illegal — walk into a wall, or two trolls into the
same square.

**The good news:** across the situations I sampled, the built bot never does any of those things.

**The honest news:** I could not prove my *test* would notice if it did. To test a test you have
to break the thing on purpose and watch the test complain. Every way I broke it, the trolls then
played a different game and never reached the moment the test was watching for. So the test has
never caught anything, and I will not present it as evidence.

## What was measured

Checks run on the **commands the bot actually emits in real games** — not on a Python re-model of
the swap logic. Re-modelling it would be a mirror, and mirrors disagreeing with their authority is
what I paid for twice today.

Delivered candidate over OSC-001 / OSC-028 / OSC-009: **0 collisions, 0 unwalkable MOVE targets,
0 half-swaps.**

## Two limits, neither iterated away

**1. Two of the three checks are structurally unfalsifiable for this design.** Swap exchanges two
cells that are *already occupied*, therefore already walkable, so it cannot collide or target a
wall by construction. Those two are reported as standing evidence and explicitly **not counted as
guards** — a check that cannot fail is not a check, and I would rather label them than let three
green lines imply three guards.

**2. `half_swap` is falsifiable but UNVALIDATED.** It is the real property: if A is ordered onto
B's cell, B must be ordered off it.

- My first control weakened the direction test and a walkability filter. It violated nothing the
  checks look at — the run reported three checks and zero rejections, which is exactly what an
  unvalidated check looks like.
- My second suppressed the counter-move, which *is* the falsifiable property. Still no rejection:
  **perturbing the bot changes its trajectory from turn 1, so the broken build never reaches the
  situation the check is watching.**

**A behavioural control cannot hold the trajectory fixed while breaking the behaviour.** That is a
general limit on whole-game perturbation as a validation method, and it applies to more than this
gate. Validating `half_swap` needs a **unit-level fixture over `select()` / `apply_swap`** — a
different instrument, not built here, and I am not going to fake it with a whole-game run.

## Consequence for sequencing

I said controls before the 240-game panel, because blessing an unguarded swap with an expensive
measurement would be the costly version of today's repeated mistake. That reasoning stands, and
gate 1 is **partially met**: the candidate is clean, one guard is real-but-unvalidated, two are
structurally inert.

**Your call whether that is enough to proceed to the panel.** My own view: the panel measures
de-novo oscillation and D-1 rate, which is a *different* question from swap legality, and the
referee itself would reject an illegal order — so I do not think the unvalidated guard blocks the
panel. But I have been wrong three times today about what my instruments establish, so I would
rather you rule than assume.
