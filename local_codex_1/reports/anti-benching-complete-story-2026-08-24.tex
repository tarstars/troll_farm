From the original problem to the causal-split conclusion}

\vspace{9mm}
{\large\color{muted}
A complete account of what we stated, what we changed, what the gates measured,
why the attempted cure worsened the locked result, and what the evidence actually permits next}

\vspace{13mm}
\begin{BlueBox}
{\large\bfseries\color{ink}The answer in one paragraph}

The original defect was real: the joint pair selector could discard one troll's available work,
leaving that troll idle for as many as 194 consecutive turns. The first cure, P1+P2, removed the
visible benching mechanism but usually did not restore progress. Route diagnosis then found useful
replant \code{PICK} candidates being built and thrown away. Revision r2 preserved those candidates,
but also introduced persistent cross-turn commitments and a duplicate-candidate path. On the
frozen 240-game cost panel, blocking rose from \textbf{35 to 115}; five orchard-inertness failures
alone were decisive. That result is valid and r2 is rejected. The later review corrected one
overstatement: the data do \emph{not} prove that persistent commitment caused all or most of the
damage, and 73 P4 labels are not 73 directly caused stall windows. The clean conclusion is:
\textbf{the option is structurally isolatable, but its value is unproved}. A future experiment, if
authorized, must add only the ephemeral current-turn option to the exact baseline candidate list,
without duplicate candidates or new commitment memory, and must stop at the first structural or
progress falsifier.
\end{BlueBox}

\section{Executive decision}

This report covers one continuous investigation. It begins with the anti-benching rule, follows
the first selector repair and the route diagnosis, reconstructs r2 and its gates, and ends with the
review-corrected causal conclusion and the exact boundary of a possible next experiment.

\begin{RedBox}
\textbf{Operational decision.} Revision r2 is \stopmark. It produced 115 blocking games against 35
for the exact P1+P2 base on the same locked 240-game panel: 80 de-novo blocks, zero healed blocks,
five new P3 failures, and 73 new P4 labels. G-e progress testing was correctly not run after the
first hard G-d falsifier. Nothing in the later review reopens r2.
\end{RedBox}

\begin{GreenBox}
\textbf{Scientific correction.} The measured result is reproducible, but the broad causal story
was stronger than the evidence. R2 bundled at least three mechanisms. There was no no-commitment
arm, no Delta-B census over the full panel, and no per-game selector decomposition. Moreover, P4
uses future trajectory information, so some labels describe temporal reclassification rather than
a stall caused inside the reported earlier interval. The precise verdict is therefore
\code{RESULT\_VALID\_BUT\_CAUSAL\_CLAIM\_UNPROVEN}.
\end{GreenBox}

\section{What we stated initially}

\subsection{The rule and the failure shape}

The original statement was not ``make both units move.'' It was stronger and more useful:

\begin{BlueBox}
\textbf{Anti-benching rule R-2.} When a troll has oracle-verified available work, the team's joint
pair selection must not discard that work and leave the troll idle. Employment means real progress,
not merely replacing \code{WAIT} with a harmless action.
\end{BlueBox}

The target was the 24-case \term{GOAL\_SPLIT} class. The recurring shape was that each local route
could be individually reasonable while their joint pairing was not. One unit wanted to move into
the cell occupied by its teammate, while the selected partner command ordered that teammate to
\code{WAIT}; the referee then dropped the move. In the worst observed case this repeated for
\textbf{194 turns in a 200-turn game}.

The field audit showed the defect was sparse but real: across 160 real games, trolls had work
available but received nothing on \textbf{615 of 84,928 troll-turns (0.72\%)}. That audit motivated
the work; it did not imply that every later fixture turn or every restored candidate was one of
those 615 turns.

\subsection{Initial success criterion}

The investigation deliberately separated three questions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Mechanism:} does the selector stop constructing self-impossible or needlessly idle
        pairs?
  \item \textbf{Progress:} does the previously benched troll actually bank, harvest, build, route,
        or otherwise advance useful work?
  \item \textbf{Cost:} does the cure preserve named invariants and avoid new liveness failures on
        the locked panel?
\end{enumerate}

This separation became decisive. A command can silence the benching detector without helping the
game. Conversely, a later action can alter a trajectory-level liveness label without causing the
earlier interval that the label reports.

\section{Phase 1 and Phase 2: the first cure}

\subsection{What P1 and P2 changed}

The first repair was intentionally narrow and applied as one patch to two bases.

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{20mm}p{42mm}Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Part} & \textbf{Rule} & \textbf{Intended effect} \\
\midrule
P1 & Refuse a pair where a unit moves onto a cell occupied by a teammate whom the same pair orders to \code{WAIT}. & Remove self-impossible pairs that the referee will invalidate. \\
P2 & On an exact pair-score tie, prefer the pair with fewer \code{WAIT} commands. & Avoid arbitrary map-order tie resolution when an equally scored active pair exists. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

P3 and P4 were explicitly not part of that build. The 235 non-deadlock turns identified by the
diagnosis remained outside the initial fix. The patch was behavior-changing, so qualification
required not just local examples but a named-cost gate and a separate platform decision.

\subsection{The repair was mechanically real}

The cure did what its code said. On the selected fixtures, benched turns fell to zero. On broader
panel accounting, blocking totals also fell:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Base} & \textbf{Before P1+P2} & \textbf{After P1+P2} & \textbf{Change} \\
\midrule
cure-C & 53 & 33 & $-20$ \\
door-1 & 43 & 35 & $-8$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

That is why it would be wrong to say the first cure ``did nothing.'' It changed the intended
selection mechanism. The failure was at the next semantic layer: activity was not the same as
productive progress.

\subsection{Why detector success was not cure success}

Under the fixed progress grader, cure-C moved only \textbf{3 to 4} and door-1 remained
\textbf{8 to 8}. Only OSC-034 became fixed on cure-C. OSC-004, OSC-013, and OSC-017 became
detector-quiet while retaining \code{progress\_restored = false}.

Two additional named costs appeared on door-1:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{P3 absolute regression:} \code{m004}, seat 0, violated the locked
        orchard-inertness comparison.
  \item \textbf{P4 and horizon cost:} \code{m021} acquired a new P4 / \code{r5-horizon}
        property violation, even though that game was already blocked for another reason.
\end{itemize}

\begin{AmberBox}
\textbf{First central lesson.} ``The troll is no longer benched'' is an intermediate mechanism
metric. It is not the project outcome. A cure must produce actual banking or employment and then
survive the named-cost gate.
\end{AmberBox}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Phase 3a: route diagnosis}

\subsection{The lossy fallback}

The route probe found that the parent candidate list was not genuinely empty. It typically had a
seeded \code{WAIT}, and in eligible late states the regeneration path formed valuable-looking
replant \code{PICK} candidates. But the fallback logic behaved conceptually as follows:

\begin{verbatim}
build ordinary candidates into out
form safe-regeneration PICK candidates in out
if idle_regeneration and chop candidates are empty:
    return fresh [WAIT]          # earlier out is discarded
\end{verbatim}

The open design question was therefore \term{extend versus replace}: should the fallback return a
fresh wait-only list, or extend the exact parent fallback with the candidates that had just been
formed?

\subsection{OSC-013: the clearest trace}

Across 170 idle turns in OSC-013, the route split cleanly:

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{30mm}p{28mm}Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Turns} & \textbf{Count} & \textbf{Observed route state} \\
\midrule
31--99 & 69 & Only \code{WAIT}; there was no discarded replant option to recover. \\
100--200 & 101 & Two replant \code{PICK} candidates were present in \code{out}, with scores 7500 and 7499, but the fallback returned \code{WAIT}. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

This was strong evidence of a missing option. It was not yet evidence that selecting the option
would create durable progress. It also did not show that a persistent intention to complete the
replant transaction was safe.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Phase 3b: what r2 actually bundled}

Revision r2 implemented the intuitive ``extend, do not replace'' idea, but its behavioral surface
was larger than that phrase suggests.

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{27mm}Y p{36mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Mechanism} & \textbf{What it did} & \textbf{Status before G-d} \\
\midrule
Delta-A & Preserved the replant \code{PICK} candidates that the parent fallback discarded. & Natural formation and selection observed. \\
Persistent commitment & When a new \code{PICK} was selected, recorded regeneration state and routed the unit toward endgame candidates on later turns. & Source path identified; no no-commitment control. \\
Delta-B & In carried, shack-adjacent fallback states, returning the whole accumulated vector and appending bank candidates could produce element-identical duplicates. & Derived from source; zero fixture ticks; unmeasured on full panel. \\
Joint selector exposure & The unchanged pair selector saw a larger candidate cross-product and could choose a different command for either unit. & Selection occurred; alternatives not decomposed per game. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

The source structure matters. Candidate formation occurs in \code{main\_candidates}; joint choice
occurs in \code{MoisanBot::select}; cross-turn memory is inserted by
\code{remember\_selected\_regeneration}; later command routing reads that memory. Thus a small
candidate-list edit can propagate into later turns through a separate memory mechanism.

\begin{BlueBox}
\textbf{Why the bundle matters.} The r2 experiment did not compare ``baseline'' with ``the exact
same bot plus one ephemeral option.'' It compared the baseline with a system in which the option
could be selected, remembered, and followed across turns, while another duplicate-candidate path
also existed. The experiment can reject that system. It cannot identify every component's causal
share without additional arms or traces.
\end{BlueBox}

\section{The locked G-d result}

\subsection{The decisive measurement}

The full named-cost panel contained 240 locked games. Against the exact door-1 P1+P2 base, r2
produced:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule

\begin{RedBox}
\textbf{Why the result worsened.} In the exact operational sense, r2 broadened the behavior of the
bot in states covered by the locked safety rules. It changed commands in five maps that had to be
inert and generated many trajectories classified as newly blocking. Since it healed no blocked
game, the net locked result moved from 35 to 115. This is sufficient to reject r2 even before we
know which bundled internal mechanism caused each change.
\end{RedBox}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Why the first causal explanation was too strong}

The early explanation was plausible: the newly selected \code{PICK} creates a persistent
regeneration commitment; that commitment keeps routing the troll and causes long liveness
regressions. Source inspection supports the possibility. The panel supports broad downstream
behavioral change. But plausibility plus correlation is not a component-level causal estimate.

\subsection{Missing counterfactuals}

The experiment did not include:

\begin{itemize}
  \item an r2 arm with Delta-A but no new commitment memory;
  \item a full-panel census and same-state selector fork for Delta-B;
  \item per-changed-game candidate vectors, rejected pair alternatives, and scores;
  \item commitment timelines joined to first divergence and later progress events; or
  \item a measurement that partitions P4 windows by whether they occur before, cross, or after the
        first command divergence.
\end{itemize}

Without those counterfactuals, ``persistent commitment caused most of the 73'' remains a hypothesis.

\subsection{P4 looks backward from the future}

The frozen P4 implementation computes remaining work and a live horizon from the later trajectory,
then scans backward and trims windows relative to the terminal suffix. That definition is valid as
a gate, but it is future-dependent.

The smallest concrete counterexample is \code{m035}, seat 0:

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{42mm}Y@{}}
\toprule
Candidate-only P4 window & turns 33--99 \\
\code{live\_end} & 99 \\
\code{terminal\_from} & 106 \\
First command divergence & turn 100 \\
Commands and world through turn 99 & identical between exact base and r2 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

A deterministic referee cannot make a command at turn 100 cause different commands or world state
on turns 33--99. Instead, the later reactivation changes where the final terminal suffix begins,
which retroactively changes the earlier interval's P4 classification. The same temporal shape is
seen in \code{m065} and \code{m114}.

\begin{AmberBox}
\textbf{Correct interpretation.} ``73 new P4 labels'' is a valid frozen-gate output. It is not a
causal inventory of 73 stalls that r2 produced inside the named intervals. Some subset may still
be genuine post-divergence harm; the existing package does not partition it.
\end{AmberBox}

This correction does not rescue r2. The five direct P3 command divergences remain, and the exact
35-to-115 gate result remains. It only prevents the project from learning the wrong mechanism from
a correctly failed candidate.

\section{Causal ledger}

The final review separated four epistemic levels: observed, deduced from source or definitions,
hypothesized, and missing evidence.

\subsection{Delta-A: preserved replant option}

\textbf{Observed.} Two score-7500/7499 candidates were discarded on 101 consecutive OSC-013 turns;
the fixture library reproduced formation and selection; the verified real subset reproduced 339
restored-and-selected turns. The five P3 games show turn-100 command impact.

\textbf{Deduced.} The option has non-zero natural reach and can survive joint selection.

\textbf{Hypothesis.} An isolated one-turn option may create useful banking or employment at an
acceptable cost.

\textbf{Missing.} The progress gate, closed-loop value, full-corpus reach, score, and qualification.

\subsection{Persistent regeneration commitment}

\textbf{Observed.} The tuned r2 path records selected \code{PICK}s and later routes remembered
units; source separates memory insertion from formation and selection.

\textbf{Deduced.} A selected Delta-A action can have a larger cross-turn behavioral surface than
its local candidate diff suggests. The new option can be excluded from memory without changing
baseline \code{PICK} handling.

\textbf{Hypothesis.} Persistent commitment caused most post-turn-100 regressions.

\textbf{Missing.} A no-commitment arm and field-level memory timelines.

\subsection{Delta-B: duplicate bank candidates}

\textbf{Observed.} Source derivation shows duplicated, element-identical bank candidates in a
carried, shack-adjacent fallback state. The fixture library reached zero such ticks.

\textbf{Deduced.} Delta-B and Delta-A are mutually exclusive in one unit state
(\code{carried > 0} versus \code{carried == 0}), but a Delta-A trajectory could reach Delta-B later.

\textbf{Hypothesis.} Exact duplicates are selector-inert—or they alter an ordering or tie path.

\textbf{Missing.} A natural-state census and same-state selection comparison on the 240-game run.

\subsection{Joint pair selection}

\textbf{Observed.} The selector applies target, stock, self-blocking, score, and fewer-wait checks
across the candidate cross-product. Added candidates were sometimes selected.

\textbf{Deduced.} Adding one unit option can change either selected command without changing the
selector's source.

\textbf{Hypothesis.} Some options complement a partner command; others displace a better pair.

\textbf{Missing.} Per-game full vectors, pair scores, rejected alternatives, and resolved commands.

\subsection{P3 and P4}

\textbf{P3 observed and decisive.} Five orchard-eligible streams differ from the exact parent.
This is direct, not inferential.

\textbf{P4 observed but causally ambiguous.} Seventy-three labels are reproduced exactly under the
frozen definition. At least one named window precedes the first command divergence, so the total is
not a direct stall count.

\section{The final causal-split conclusion}

\begin{GreenBox}
{\large\bfseries\color{good}Conclusion: ISOLATABLE}

The missing replant option can be separated structurally from both new persistent commitment and
duplicate bank candidates. Candidate formation, joint selection, and commitment insertion occupy
distinct functions. A future design can construct the exact parent's returned list first, append
only the explicitly discarded replant candidates, pass the result through the unchanged selector,
and exclude any selected added candidate from commitment memory.

\textbf{This is an isolatable design, not a validated cure.}
\end{GreenBox}

\subsection{Exact option-only candidate-list contract}

Let $B[u]$ be the exact ordered candidate vector returned by the parent for unit $u$ on the same
recorded decision state. Let $D[u]$ contain only the eligible, deduplicated replant \code{PICK}
candidates. The candidate vector under test must be:

\[
C[u] =
\begin{cases}
B[u] \mathbin{\|} D[u], & \text{when every exact precondition holds},\\
B[u], & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\]

where $\mathbin{\|}$ means ordered append. No element of $B$ may be removed, reordered, rescored,
retargeted, or duplicated. Nothing other than $D$ may be added.

\subsection{Exact same-state preconditions}

Delta-A may form for a unit only when all conditions hold:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item command routing uses the parent's ordinary \code{main\_candidates} branch—not an early,
        endgame, or remembered-regeneration branch;
  \item the dedicated option-only flag is enabled and the frozen initial layout is not
        orchard-eligible;
  \item idle regeneration is active and chop candidates are empty;
  \item carried inventory is zero and free capacity is positive;
  \item turn is at least 100, there are at most two plants, and at least two own units exist;
  \item the unit is adjacent to its own shack and no plant occupies its cell; and
  \item the bank contains at least one fruit kind.
\end{enumerate}

For each bank fruit in unchanged inventory order, $D$ uses the existing identity:
\code{PICK <unit> <kind>}, score $7500 - \text{priority}$, target the unit's current cell. Any
element already identical in $B$ is removed from $D$.

\subsection{What must remain exact}

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{37mm}Y@{}}
\toprule

Later turns may differ because the referee executed a selected \code{PICK} and the world now holds
different cargo. They may not differ because the experimental candidate inserted a hidden target,

\subsection{Stage-level stop rule}

If the isolated option has natural selection but no useful progress, retire the replant cure family

success, timeout, cancellation, and ownership. Do not restore the generic commitment implicitly.
