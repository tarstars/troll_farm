\section*{1. Evidence boundary}

The package contains 153 current-bot games: \textbf{95 wins, 2 ties and 56 losses}, with
68 games in seat 0 and 85 in seat 1. Seven sealed-tagged games were excluded. The direct
strong-opponent evidence is only game \texttt{897780884} against rank-13 Astrobytes; the
larger top-20 corpus is observational context, not a direct treatment comparison.

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l r Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Evidence slice} & \textbf{Size} & \textbf{Permitted interpretation} \\
\midrule
Current-new open games & 153 & Current behavior, associations and recurrent failure modes \\
Full 300-turn current games & 96 & Phase-by-phase score decomposition \\
Direct current vs top-20 & 1 & Exact postmortem only; not broad evidence \\
Top-20-source open games & 2,684 & Benchmark context; not causal value \\
Sealed-tagged games & 7 & Excluded and unopened \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

No report used sealed data, performed a build or simulation, created a candidate, or
mutated the Arena.

\section*{2. The recurrent loss pattern}

The blind replication isolated ten catastrophic games, defined before mechanism analysis
as final margin at most $-100$:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{10/153 games} (6.54\%; Wilson 95\% interval 3.59--11.61\%);
  \item total margin \textbf{$-1{,}674$}, or $-167.4$ per catastrophe;
  \item every catastrophic opponent finished with 3--4 workers while the resident stayed
        at exactly two;
  \item the resident was still ahead at turn 150 in 9/10, but ahead at turn 250 in only
        1/10 and at the end in 0/10.
\end{itemize}

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l *{6}{>{\centering\arraybackslash}X}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Checkpoint} & t50 & t100 & t150 & t200 & t250 & Final \\
\midrule
Resident ahead, catastrophes & 9/10 & 10/10 & 9/10 & 6/10 & 1/10 & 0/10 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

This is a late crossover under opponent scaling, not a recurrent opening collapse. It is
still observational: final roster is endogenous, the resident has no workforce variation,
and opponent scaling bundles many policy differences.

\section*{3. What changes across the game}

Claude's non-blind replication independently decomposed points per 50-turn window over all
96 full games. Against opponents that finish with at least three workers, resident scoring
does not decline; opponent scoring accelerates sharply.

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}l r r r r@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Window} & \textbf{Our points} & \textbf{Their points} & \textbf{Net} & \textbf{Opponent roster group} \\
\midrule
t50--100  & 36.92 & 8.64   & +28.28 & $\geq 3$ workers \\
t100--150 & 37.64 & 16.47  & +21.17 & $\geq 3$ workers \\
t150--200 & 39.75 & 46.06  & $-6.31$ & $\geq 3$ workers \\
t200--250 & 40.83 & 79.81  & $-38.97$ & $\geq 3$ workers \\
t250--300 & 42.56 & 102.19 & $-59.64$ & $\geq 3$ workers \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

For the 60 full games against opponents finishing with at most two workers, the resident's
net remains positive in every window. For the 36 games against 3--4-worker opponents, the
net turns negative in t150--200 and deteriorates afterward. The reconciled interpretation
is deliberately narrow:

\begin{quote}\raggedright
\textbf{Opponent workforce divergence can be established by turn 150; the score crossover
and largest terminal damage occur later.}
\end{quote}

The decomposition rejects the simple story that our own output collapses. It does not, by
itself, prove every marginal own-economy improvement worthless; those families remain
closed by their separate controlled experiments and displacement accounting.

\section*{4. Direct game: Astrobytes, \texttt{897780884}}

The resident lost 333--403. It trained once at turn 11; Astrobytes trained at turns 1, 56
and 105 and finished with four workers. The resident led through turn 200, then crossed
behind during the late acceleration. The direct trajectory supports the chronology but
cannot identify a treatment effect.

The early WAIT sequence at turns 4--8 is followed by harvesting the same tree and matches
the documented ripening-wait behavior. Across the 36 scaled-opponent full games, resident
WAIT count correlates only $-0.046$ with margin. Cross-review therefore rejected a generic
WAIT legality/precedence audit as a ranked improvement.

Tree lineage, first feller, wood recipient, and counterfactual legality are unavailable for
the wider cohort in this package. Claims depending on those fields remain unavailable.

\section*{7. H3a: exact next decision}

The intended three arms remain:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item unchanged resident control;
  \item exact eligible opponent-crop priority always on;
  \item the identical priority activated, and then kept active, after public state first
        shows at least three opponent workers.
\end{enumerate}

The existing reconstruction self-test passes, but it proves source reconstruction only.
Before any 6,144-task value panel, a new host-authorized read-only task must materialize the
required trajectories and test the trigger gate: sufficiently early activation in the ten
catastrophes, low false activation on matched wins, and eligible ETA-$\leq 6$ decisions after
activation. If that preflight fails, stop H3a without tuning it. If it passes, build the
conditioned source and exact value runner under a new frozen protocol; the frozen protocol's
value gates govern.

A purely illustrative 20\% recovery of the ten-catastrophe negative mass would be
$1{,}674 \times 0.20 / 153 = +2.19$ mean margin per current game. This is a scenario, not a
prediction and not an Arena-rating conversion.

\section*{8. Data and provenance corrections}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Do not compute \texttt{planted\_ok / plant\_cmd}: over top-20 sides the aggregate
        numerator is 86,023 and the denominator 81,280. The counters are not documented as
        a subset relation, so a success rate can exceed 100\% and is unsound.
  \item For retrospective ``scaled by turn 150'' counts, require a successfully landed
        extra worker:
        \begin{center}\small
        \texttt{second\_train\_turn <= 151}\\[-1mm]
        \texttt{AND roster\_final >= 3}
        \end{center}
        Game \texttt{897782434} contains a failed TRAIN and must be excluded.
  \item Do not use the earlier unexplained ``1,268'' count; no coherent package predicate
        reproduced it.
  \item The rubric's SHA-256 and Git blob hash are different hash types over the same
        content, not a provenance failure.
\end{itemize}
