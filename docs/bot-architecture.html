tass — a Troll Farm bot

CodinGame Spring Challenge 2026 · Silver league rank 1 / 681 (score 23.95 peak) · version v1.1.5-nolock · 2026-07-02

1. The game in one paragraph

Two players farm a point-symmetric grid (8–11 rows, width = 2×height) for 300 turns. Trolls harvest fruit from trees (1 pt each), chop trees for wood (4 pts each — the dominant currency), mine iron, plant new trees, and train more trolls. Training the n-th troll costs n + stat² of a resource per stat: PLUM→speed, LEMON→carry, APPLE→harvest, IRON→chop. A felled tree yields min(size, free carry) wood and is gone. Trees adjacent to water grow dramatically faster (APPLE cooldown 9→2, PLUM 8→3, BANANA 6→4). Only banked resources score; carried goods are worth nothing at the buzzer.

2. The opponent: Boss 4, decoded

Promotion to Gold requires out-rating Boss 4, which sits above the #1 human player in Silver — so the real bar is "be the best bot of 681". We reverse-engineered the boss from full debug dumps (cgauto/last_console.txt, per-turn @TFD/@TFSUM state) and arena replays. Its fixed script:

  • t≈2: trains a cheap (1,1,1,2) utility troll; the starter plants a base LEMON orchard and mines iron.
  • Harvesting is strictly local: its starter never left radius 5 of its shack in 300 turns (measured); the utility troll averages 4.7.
  • Hoards to lemon 18, then at t≈150 trains one (2,4,2,2) power-chopper — carry 4 means 16 pts per felled size-4 tree.
  • That chopper then CHOPs every single turn, raiding deep into our half; boss wood is ≈0 before t≈190, then ~1 wood / 5 turns.
  • Never trains a 4th troll (ends games with 20+ lemon unspent).

This script is modelled as a sparring partner in rust/src/strategies/script_boss.rs (scriptboss), alongside the older greedy-expansion model silver_boss.rs. Every change must hold on both models — the rule that caught several would-be regressions (§6).

3. The core idea: a task-market scheduler with denial camping

Instead of fixed per-troll roles, every turn is an auction. For each (troll, task) pair we compute a marginal rate — points per turn — and greedily assign the best pairs under exclusivity. Strategy emerges from the prices, not from if-else role code.

Implemented twice, kept in sync: rust/src/main.rs :: decide_sched() (the single-file CodinGame submission) and rust/src/strategies/sched_bot.rs (the simulator mirror with env-var tuning knobs). Task types (enum SchedTask):

3.1 Denial camping — the discovery that beats the boss

The fell rate deliberately ignores wood yield and prices a tree by closeness to the enemy shack (the classic metric d + 3·manh(tree, oppShack) from the earlier heuristic bot, re-expressed as a rate). Because that rate (≈0.8) is below a full load's bank rate but above everything else, a chopper that fills up far from home keeps felling at zero extraction — permanently starving the opponent's tree supply — and only banks when passing near home or at the endgame. Wood is worth 4 pts, but a tree denied to the boss costs it up to 16 pts + future fruit. Measured: this "camper" beats the boss-script model at 87.5% where the yield-driven "cycler" variant (SB_FELL_FREE=1) manages 67%. From 80 turns before the end the gate flips and fells require free carry — denial value decays to zero at the buzzer (need_free = turns_rem ≤ 80).

3.2 Training plan

Greedy, inherited from the proven heuristic ancestor: build up to two fast cheap choppers (2,2,0,2) as soon as affordable (speed wins the race to contested trees; carry 2 keeps them cheap — copying the boss's expensive carry-4 chopper measurably drains the economy), otherwise the cheapest affordable harvester of (2,2,2,0) / (1,2,2,0) / (1,1,1,0), capped at 4 trolls. Constants MB_CHOPPER, MB_HARVESTERS in main.rs.

4. The economy: wood printing and water crops

Fruit is seed capital; wood is income. Surplus fruit is planted near the base, grown fast beside water, and felled young — a self-sustaining "wood printer" worth ~8 pts per cycle. Decoded from the replays of the bots that were beating us (aRi: 67 wood = 268 of its 275 points; logiqub: 76 wood = 304 of 308).
  • Species follows the spot (from logiqub's crop system): on a water-adjacent cell plant APPLE (grows a size per 2 ticks, and its high health makes it unprofitable for the enemy to steal) or PLUM; on dry cells plant BANANA (fastest dry grower, and worthless for training — pure surplus). Plum/apple are training currency, so they are only spent above a reserve of 8 banked units (SB_CROP_RES).
  • PICK-based feeding: harvesters PICK a seed back out of the bank at the shack when a plantable base spot exists — the printer never starves waiting for a lucky carry.
  • The plum orchard (max 2, water-adjacent) keeps the troll-training pipeline funded; orchard duty outranks printing for a plum-carrying troll.

6. Methodology: how changes are validated

  1. Fast local benchmark — bench A B N plays N seeds × both seats in parallel (rust/src/bin/bench.rs) on a referee-faithful simulator (rust/src/game/engine.rs, validated against real replays).
  2. Two-model rule: a change is accepted only if it helps (or holds) against both silverboss and scriptboss. Anchor-calibrated: the models correctly order historical bots by their real win rates where a single model inverted them.
  3. Real batches before defaults for risky classes. Three transfer-failure modes were measured, where the sim said "better" and the arena said "worse":
    1. Overfitting one boss model (v1.0.4: 90.5% sim, 33% real).  2. Troll-spec/economy changes interacting with map-economy calibration (v1.0.9 "mower": best-ever on both models, 25% real).  3. Opponent-conditional triggers under-represented by both models (v1.1.3 raid gate: +1.7pp on models, −5 rating points in the arena).
  4. Arena replay decoding (pure HTTP, no browser): findLastBattlesByAgentId → gameResult/findByGameId exposes both players' raw command streams, per-turn inventories and summaries. This pipeline found every major leak and decoded the elite engines. Submissions are verified by a new agentId on the leaderboard, never by the submitter's exit code.
