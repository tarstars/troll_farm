# D101 — Production persistence, not suppression, is the observational architecture gap

**Status:** `diagnosis`  
**Review state:** `proposed`  
**Decision date:** 2026-07-18  
**Scope:** Observed replay behavior of the resident and top-three agents; architecture diagnosis, not intervention value.

## Question

What distinguishes the resident from the top cohort at the architecture level?

## Decision

The resident already suppresses competitively; the observed gap is persistent self-production during and after scaling.

## Decisive evidence

- **self_crop_reap_game_coverage**: top-3 93.3% vs resident 10.3%  
  Population: top-three and resident decoded public games  
  Evidence: `docs/CONSTRAINTS.md` (lines 113-116) [observational_audit]
- **created_crop_reap_rate**: top-3 24.16% vs resident 0.94%  
  Population: created-crop generations in decoded public games  
  Evidence: `docs/CONSTRAINTS.md` (lines 113-116) [observational_audit]
- **late_suppression_share**: 78% of top-3 suppression chops occur at workforce ≥3  
  Population: top-three decoded suppression chops  
  Evidence: `docs/CONSTRAINTS.md` (lines 113-116) [observational_audit]

## Attempts

- D101 production/suppression decomposition

## What this does not prove

- Production persistence causally raises ladder score.
- Scaling is necessary for every strong two-worker policy.
- A production patch on the current scheduler will transfer.

## Limitations and counterevidence

- Cohort behavior is observational and confounded by policy quality, opponents, and game state.
- The diagnosis does not isolate the minimum causal mechanism.


## Relations

- `supports` → `external:TERMINAL-SYNTHESIS`
- `constrains` → `external:PRODUCTION-PROPOSALS`

## Reopening conditions

- A controlled intervention that preserves denial while adding renewable production passes a frozen causal panel.
