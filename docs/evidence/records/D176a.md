# D176a — Oscillation mechanism succeeds, value is immaterial, and two gates were mis-specified

**Status:** `closed`  
**Review state:** `proposed`  
**Decision date:** 2026-07-30  
**Scope:** D176a exact-resident preference tie-break on the frozen 2,048-task panel; gate-design postmortem included.

## Question

Does a bounded preference tie-break eliminate long oscillation and deliver decision-relevant value?

## Decision

The mechanism largely succeeds and all value gates pass, but the gain is immaterial. Two mechanism gates were invalidly specified against the wrong population/failure mode.

## Decisive evidence

- **long_oscillation_rate**: 8.50% → 2.88%  
  Population: D176a 2,048-task paired panel

- **de_novo**: 0 de-novo oscillations  
  Population: D176a 2,048-task paired panel  
  Evidence: `docs/CONSTRAINTS.md` (lines 855-860) [panel_causal]
- **overall_value**: +0.045 margin; CI [−0.024,+0.114]  
  Population: D176a 2,048-task paired panel  
  Evidence: `data/analysis/live-agent-6553250/d176a-oscillation-breaker-successor-result.json` (/value/overall_mean_margin_delta) [panel_causal]
- **worst_case_gate_population**: real-corpus reference 133 turns vs panel control 247 turns  
  Population: real resident corpus compared with D176a synthetic panel control  
  Evidence: `docs/CONSTRAINTS.md` (lines 899-906) [observational_audit]

## Attempts

- D171a hard-forbid predecessor
- D176a bounded preference successor

## What this does not prove

- Oscillation is not a fidelity defect.
- Every preference tie-break is valueless.
- The invalid mechanism gates invalidate the measured +0.045 value result.

## Limitations and counterevidence

- The immateriality conclusion is panel value, not an Arena result.
- The verdict uses the frozen decision rule even though two mechanism sub-gates were post-hoc diagnosed as mis-specified.
