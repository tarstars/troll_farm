## 1. Decision

The accepted table is not one dance mechanism. It is three observably different failures:

| owner scope | accepted population | strongest fact | design owner |
|---|---:|---|---|
| P1 | 10 episodes / 430 turns | exactly one stationary **working** blocker on every blocker tick | joint assignment / active-cell ownership |
| P2 | 15 episodes / 434 turns | no blocker, fixed target, target occupied on 218 turns | target occupancy / stable approach |
| P3 | 37 episodes / 1,598 turns | no blocker, target and source change; 29 episodes preserve route, commitment boundary, and blocker set | semantic intent persistence |

`[READ]` These counts and predicates are the accepted owner scopes in
`docs/EVIDENCE-DANCE-2026-08-24.md`, sections "P1", "P2", and "P3". The accepted instrument
uses the post-final-resolver selected target/source record and a six-turn minimum dance span. The
160-game synthetic panel is exact, one-process, and passed G0-G4 after the phase-order repair.

`[INFERRED]` A single cure cannot be justified from these facts. P1 is a composition error between
work assignment and occupancy resolution. P2 preserves semantic intent but loses destination
stability around an occupied target. P3 loses semantic intent itself. A patch that adds all three
forms of state at once would make every changed game causally ambiguous and reproduce the
repository's documented "two correct doors make a wall" failure at a larger scale.

### Recommended order

1. **P1: active-work cell lease at joint selection — first build.** Strongest causal evidence,
   smallest state, narrowest predicate, and earliest falsifier.
2. **P2: fixed-target occupancy lease with one stable staging destination.** More state and an
   unresolved occupant-identity question; design after P1 is measured alone.
3. **P3: target-source intent lease for the 29 stable-context episodes.** Largest apparent reach,
   but broadest behavioral commitment and weakest causal specificity; do last, or retire if P1/P2
   capture the useful share.

A full shared planner remains a plausible long-term architecture, but it is deliberately not a
first build. The first experiment should buy information, not merely remove detector rows.

## 2. What the champion currently composes

### 2.1 Candidate selection

`[READ]` In the pinned champion, `Candidate` carries `command`, `score`, and semantic `Target`.
`MoisanBot::compatible` rejects equal semantic target cells and
`MoisanBot::stock_compatible` protects duplicate `PICK` inventory. `MoisanBot::select` then
maximizes the sum of two candidates' scores with a strict `score > best_score`. It receives no
unit positions, occupancy lease, projected landing, route memory, or persistent target owner.
Source: the pinned champion, functions `compatible`, `stock_compatible`, and `select`.

`[READ]` Candidate lists are regenerated from the current `GameState` on every turn by
`early_candidates`, `endgame_candidates`, and `main_candidates`. `YamoBot` has specialized
`regeneration_commitments`, but no general per-unit target/source lease and no shared target owner.
Source: the pinned champion, `struct YamoBot` and `commands`.

### 2.2 Conflict resolution

`[READ]` `resolve_move_conflicts_with_priority_and_forbidden` parses selected `MOVE`s, computes a
projected landing with `next_cell`, reserves cells of own units that are not moving, and accepts a
landing only when it is not reserved or forbidden. When the landing is blocked, it chooses one
orthogonal detour cell that is currently unoccupied/unreserved and minimizes distance to the
original target; otherwise it emits `WAIT`. The detour ranking uses the original target and current
state only. It stores no waypoint or reason for the detour across turns. Source: the pinned champion,
`resolve_move_conflicts_with_priority_and_forbidden`.

`[READ]` The command pipeline has more than one composition boundary: ordinary candidate selection,
then conflict resolution, then a route/source reuse merge with another selection, then a final
priority/forbidden conflict resolution and door-clear pass. Source: the pinned champion,
`YamoBot::commands`.

`[INFERRED]` The final resolver is a legality owner, not an intent owner. It can prevent two units
from landing on one cell this turn, but it cannot decide whether the mover should retain the target,
which unit owns ongoing work, or whether a detour should persist. Fixing only the resolver would
therefore risk replacing an oscillation with repeated `WAIT` while preserving the upstream reason
that selects the impossible or unstable assignment.

### 2.3 What the accepted "blocker" means

`[READ]` `build_oscillation_library.measure_blocker` calls a peer the blocker when it occupies one
cell for the whole dance window and that cell is orthogonally adjacent to one of the dancer's two
cells. Its own documentation says this is the configuration in which the shipped resolver's detour
branch can be forced. `dance_facts.mech` then distinguishes a working blocker from an idle blocker
using the peer's actually emitted non-`WAIT` commands. Source:
`origin/main@801af9f8b3541351afa9e321f555e854c6e13228:claude_1/banana-restoration-r2/build_oscillation_library.py`,
`measure_blocker`, and `agent/claude_1@4c92432f:claude_1/dance1/dance_facts.py`, `mech`.

This definition is narrower than "some teammate existed nearby" and is sufficient to design a
narrow P1 predicate.

## 3. P1 mechanism and cure: active work must own its current cell

### 3.1 Mechanism

`[READ]` The accepted P1 predicate is
`FOLLOWER_WORKING && blockers >= 1 && blocker_working_count == blockers`. It selects 10 episodes
and 430 turns. Every blocker tick has exactly one working blocker; there are no unowned blocker
ticks and only three distinct blocker unit IDs across the population. Source:
`docs/EVIDENCE-DANCE-2026-08-24.md`, P1 tables.

`[READ]` The selector compares semantic target equality and stock only. It does not ask whether the
mover's projected landing is the current cell of the teammate selected to perform stationary work.
The resolver asks that question later, after scoring has already chosen the pair. When the landing
is reserved by the working unit, the resolver chooses a fresh one-step detour toward the unchanged
target. Source: champion functions `select` and
`resolve_move_conflicts_with_priority_and_forbidden`.

`[INFERRED]` P1 is the following loop:

1. the joint scorer chooses both a mover's target and a teammate's productive stationary command;
2. the selected pair is score/target-compatible but physically incompatible at the mover's next
   landing;
3. the resolver correctly preserves the working teammate and diverts the mover one step;
4. next turn, candidate scoring has forgotten the conflict and chooses the same assignment shape;
5. the fresh detour ranking sends the mover back across the same two cells.

The working command and the occupancy repair can each be locally correct while their composition is
not. This is the exact architecture failure described in
`docs/DISCOVERY-two-correct-doors-make-a-wall-2026-08-17.md`.

### 3.2 Proposed rule: one-turn `ActiveWorkLease`

`[PROPOSAL]` Add one ephemeral ownership object to the **joint candidate-composition layer**, not to
the final resolver:

```text
ActiveWorkLease {
    owner_unit: i32,
    owner_cell: Cell,
    source: candidate source/route identity,
    target: Candidate::target,
    verb: CHOP | HARVEST | PLANT | PICK | DROP | MINE,
}
```

A candidate creates a lease only when all of the following are true:

```text
1. its command is a productive non-MOVE, non-WAIT verb from the set above;
2. it was generated for the unit's current state by the existing generator;
3. its command leaves the unit on its current cell for this turn;
4. the candidate is part of the pair being evaluated, not merely present in the list.
```

For every evaluated pair `(a, b)`, compute each `MOVE` candidate's projected landing with the same
`next_cell` rule used by the resolver. Reject the pair before score comparison when:

```text
projected_landing(mover) == ActiveWorkLease.owner_cell
and the owner candidate does not vacate that cell.
```

Also reject a direct semantic target on the leased cell when the target cannot be completed without
displacing the owner this turn. Do not merely subtract a tuning penalty: the pair is physically
self-inconsistent and should be outside the feasible set.

The rule must be owned by one helper used at **both** candidate-composition sites in
`YamoBot::commands`:

- ordinary multi-unit selection; and
- the later source/reuse merge where fixed source commands and newly selected commands are combined.

Fixed source commands are inputs to the same lease set. The final resolver remains unchanged as a
last legality check. It must not independently reimplement the lease policy.

### 3.3 What this preserves

`[PROPOSAL]`

- The working unit's existing command, score, target, route, and specialized commitment are
  preserved.
- The mover keeps the existing scorer and candidate list; only a pair proven self-inconsistent is
  removed.
- No cross-turn state is introduced in the first build. The lease is reconstructed from the pair on
  every turn.
- P2 and P3 have no working blocker by definition, so this predicate should be inert on their
  accepted rows.
- Existing same-target, stock, priority, forbidden-cell, and door-clear semantics remain in force.

### 3.4 Composition risks

`[INFERRED]`

1. **Detector silence without progress.** If every useful mover candidate conflicts, selection may
   choose `WAIT`; the P1 D-1 row disappears while the mover remains unemployed.
2. **Choke monopolization.** A productive worker can own an articulation cell for many turns and
   starve a second unit even though moving the worker would improve total value.
3. **Second-boundary bypass.** Applying the rule only to the first `select` call lets route/source
   reuse recreate the conflict later.
4. **False productivity.** Treating every non-`MOVE` as work would reserve cells for ineffective or
   impossible commands. The lease must use the existing generated candidate and the exact productive
   verb set, not the final string `!= WAIT` alone.
5. **Migration to another mechanism.** The mover may start changing targets (P3) or waiting beside a
   fixed occupied target (P2).

### 3.5 Exact predicted evidence movement

`[PREDICTION]` On the accepted 160-game corpus, the first build targets exactly the 10 rows selected
by the owner P1 predicate, keyed by `(map_id, seat, unit, turn_start, turn_end)`, and their 430
covered turns.

Required outcome:

```text
owner P1 rows                         10 -> 0
owner P1 covered turns               430 -> 0
WORKING_BLOCKER context rows          those exact 10 disappear
P2 rows / turns                       exactly 15 / 434, unchanged
P3 rows / turns                       exactly 37 / 1,598, unchanged

```

The targeted rows must **disappear**, not move to `FIXED_TARGET_NO_BLOCKER`, P3 `UNCLASSIFIED`, an
idle-blocker class, or a different D-1 window. Every changed game must additionally show real
productive continuation: at least one accepted progress event attributable to the previously
blocked work/mover path, not only absence of D-1.

### 3.6 Earliest falsifiers and kill rules

`[KILL]` Stop the candidate on the first of:

- any of the exact 10 P1 rows remains;
- any accepted P2 or P3 row, key, target/source sequence, route, or commitment sequence changes;
- a targeted P1 row becomes another dance class or a new D-1 episode;
- a new idle/no-progress/P4 symptom appears in any changed game;
- the working unit loses or delays its productive command without compensating progress;
- the rule fires outside a pair with an exact projected landing on the selected working unit's cell;
- either candidate-composition boundary can bypass the same contract;
- G0 identity/parity, G1 controls, or G2 row join fails.

This is the recommended first build because its falsifier can be observed before introducing a
persistent memory policy.

## 4. P2 mechanism and cure: keep the target, stabilize the approach

### 4.1 Mechanism

`[READ]` P2 has 15 episodes and 434 turns, no blocker, zero target changes, 218 turns with the target
cell occupied, and only seven distinct target cells. Source: the accepted P2 table in the dossier.

`[READ]` The current selector can continue choosing a high-scoring candidate for the same semantic
target because `compatible` does not price occupancy over time. The resolver sees only this turn's
landing/reservations and chooses a new detour or `WAIT`; it does not remember the previous detour.
Source: champion `select` and `resolve_move_conflicts_with_priority_and_forbidden`.

`[INFERRED]` P2 is not goal indecision. The target is stable; the approach is not. Replanning a
one-step detour from alternating positions around the same occupied target can create a two-cell
cycle even though semantic intent is perfectly preserved. The accepted table does not, by itself,
prove whether the occupant is always own, opponent, or changes identity, so an own-target ownership
patch is not yet justified for all 15 rows.

### 4.2 Proposed rule: `OccupiedTargetApproach`

`[PROPOSAL]` This is a separate later build with per-unit state:

```text
OccupiedTargetApproach {
    unit: i32,
    source: source/route identity,
    target: Target,
    occupant: optional (player, unit_id),
    staging_cell: optional Cell,
    started_turn: i32,
    context_epoch: (route, commitment state, blocker set),
}
```

Trigger only after the same `(source, target)` is selected on consecutive turns and the target cell
is occupied in both observed states. Keep the semantic target unchanged. Choose one deterministic
unoccupied staging cell adjacent to the target, using a stable key independent of the dancer's
current side:

```text
(distance from staging cell to own shack,
 distance from current unit to staging cell,
 staging cell coordinates)
```

Once selected, retain that staging cell while the target's occupant and context epoch are unchanged.
Resume the true target when it clears. Invalidate immediately when the target disappears, the source
route changes, a commitment boundary changes, the occupant changes, the staging cell becomes
illegal, or a working-cell lease conflicts.

If no legal staging cell exists, suppress this target for the current selection rather than asking
the resolver to invent a fresh detour. The existing scorer then chooses another candidate or `WAIT`;
the measurement must reject a `WAIT`-only apparent cure.

Before implementation, the 15 accepted rows must be read-only split by occupant player and occupant
identity stability. Opponent occupancy must not be silently treated as own-unit ownership: reaching a
tree occupied by an opponent may still be correct.

### 4.3 Preservation and risks

`[PROPOSAL]` Preserve `(source, target)` and route/commitment context; change only the intermediate
destination. Do not add a score bonus or alter target ranking.

`[INFERRED]` Main risks:

- a stale staging lease becomes a new fixed-target stall;
- two units choose the same staging cell;
- suppressing an occupied target creates P3 target churn;
- an opponent occupying the target is strategically useful contact, not a reason to abandon it;
- a target can be permanently occupied, requiring a separate ownership or abandonment decision.

### 4.4 Expected table and kill rules

`[PREDICTION]`

```text
owner P2 rows                         15 -> 0
owner P2 covered turns               434 -> 0
target-cell-occupied turns in P2     218 -> 0 within a surviving dance window
P1 rows / turns                       exactly 10 / 430, unchanged
P3 rows / turns                       exactly 37 / 1,598, unchanged
```

The 15 rows must disappear rather than become P3 target churn or a no-target/waiting class.

`[KILL]` Stop on any P1/P3 change, any target change in a formerly P2 row, repeated staging-cell
changes under unchanged occupancy, a new no-progress window, or a case where the occupant identity
needed by the rule is absent from accepted evidence.

## 5. P3 mechanism and cure: preserve target-source intent only in a stable context

### 5.1 Mechanism

`[READ]` P3 contains 37 episodes and 1,598 turns. It has no blocker; target and source change in all
37 episodes. Sixteen episodes change destination, while 21 do not. Eight cross a commitment
boundary; 29 do not. The accepted disruption table says 29 episodes / 1,374 turns preserve route,
commitment boundary, and blocker set despite target/source churn. Source: dossier P3 and disruption
composition tables.

`[READ]` `main_candidates`, `early_candidates`, and `endgame_candidates` rebuild candidates and
scores from each current state. `select` chooses the current maximum and retains neither the
previous target nor the previous source. `YamoBot` stores only specialized regeneration
commitments, not a general semantic goal. Source: champion candidate generators, `select`,
`YamoBot`, and `commands`.

`[INFERRED]` The 29 stable-axis episodes are the strongest goal-hysteresis candidates: route,
commitment boundary, and blocker set do not explain the changes, so repeated current-state scoring
can switch among near-competing target/source pairs. This is still an inference. The accepted owner
P3 scope intentionally includes all target-changing no-blocker episodes and does not claim that
"target churn" is one cause. The eight boundary-changing episodes must not be folded into a first
lease experiment.

### 5.2 Proposed rule: stable-context `IntentLease`

`[PROPOSAL]` Later, and only for the 29 stable-axis rows, add:

```text
IntentLease {
    unit: i32,
    source: candidate source/route identity,
    target: Target,
    acquired_turn: i32,
    context_epoch: (route, commitment state, blocker set),
    last_progress_turn: i32,
}
```

Acquire the lease when a real non-`None` candidate is selected in a stable context. On subsequent
turns, keep the same `(source, target)` candidate if it is still generated and legal. Do not switch
merely because another legal candidate has a higher instantaneous score. Release on exactly one of:

```text
- the leased candidate is no longer generated or becomes illegal;
- its target disappears or is completed;
- accepted progress completes or materially advances the action;
- route changes;
- commitment state changes;
- blocker set changes;
- an ActiveWorkLease or occupied-target contract makes it infeasible.
```

The first version has no free numeric score margin. That makes the intervention falsifiable: it is a
semantic lease, not a tuning change. The eight P3 rows with commitment-boundary change remain out of
scope and must stay byte-identical.

### 5.3 Preservation, risks, expected table

`[PROPOSAL]` Preserve the route and existing specialized commitment state. The lease sits above
candidate scoring and below joint feasibility; it supplies one preferred candidate, while P1/P2
contracts can still reject it as physically infeasible.

`[INFERRED]` Main risks are stale bad goals, missed opponent opportunities, ownership conflicts,
commitment leakage, and turning target churn into a fixed-target stall.

`[PREDICTION]`

```text
stable-axis P3 rows / turns           29 / 1,374 -> 0
boundary-changing P3 rows / turns      8 /   224, unchanged
P1 rows / turns                       exactly 10 / 430, unchanged
P2 rows / turns                       exactly 15 / 434, unchanged
```

`[KILL]` Stop if a targeted row becomes P2, any lease survives a route/commitment/blocker-set
change, a new P1 blocker appears, a formerly stable target remains unproductive, or score/liveness
is used as a substitute for actual completed work.

## 6. Why not one unified patch now

`[READ]` The repository already records that composition failures occur at joins between locally
reasonable subsystems. The architecture discussion recommends explicit shared ownership rather than
scattered predicates, while also noting that a structural invariant can remove a bad state without
restoring progress. Sources:
`docs/DISCOVERY-two-correct-doors-make-a-wall-2026-08-17.md` and
`docs/DISCUSSION-architecture-over-score-2026-08-22.md`.

`[INFERRED]` A combined patch would simultaneously:

- change joint feasibility for P1;
- add destination state for P2;
- add semantic target/source state for P3;
- alter interactions with specialized regeneration commitments;
- change final resolver inputs and possibly route/source reuse.

A green aggregate would not identify which rule helped. A red aggregate would not identify which
rule harmed. Trying to cure every revealed problem in one patch is therefore the wrong objective.
The correct architecture direction may ultimately be a transactional work/target ownership layer,
but the evidence should be accumulated through isolated, composable contracts.

## 9. Final recommendation

**First build:** P1-only active-work ownership at the joint candidate-composition boundary.

It is the best expected benefit per unit risk because the accepted evidence gives a complete
mechanism signature on every targeted tick: one stationary working blocker, no unowned case, and a
known resolver detour geometry. It requires no persistent cross-turn memory and is definitionally
inert on P2/P3.

**Do not build P2 or P3 with it.** P2 needs occupant-identity and stable-approach design. P3 needs a
semantic lease and should initially exclude the eight commitment-boundary rows. Each has a different
composition contract and a different earliest falsifier.

The goal is not to make the detector quiet. The goal is to make one ownership contract explicit,
preserve productive work, and demonstrate that the team actually progresses without creating a
new dance under another name.
