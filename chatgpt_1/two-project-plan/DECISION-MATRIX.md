## Which project's evidence is stronger for which question?

**Ladder variability:** the main project's five recent identical-source submissions are the cleaner estimate of local rollout variability. The neighbour's mixed-policy 23-reading summary is a historical performance band, not a comparable variance estimator. The old main 1.501 within-family estimate and the neighbour's identical-source V368 spread both warn that the recent 0.815 estimate is not universal. [S2, S5, S9]

**Whether our native port failed and why:** the main project's reproduced port post-mortem and real-player comparison are stronger than the old fruit-first diagnosis. They identify the stock-as-cap and missing-transition defects. They do not estimate a causal ten-point improvement from a repaired implementation because native and champion opponents lack common support. [S11]

**Whether points-per-trip/productive fallback alone solves the game:** the neighbour's source-matched adaptive real-agent experiments are more directly relevant than either project's proxy score gains. They show a real zero-wood defect, its repair, and the lack of a subsequent win improvement. The tiny pilot samples are strong counterexamples to universal claims, not precise population estimates. [S12, S13]

**Whether the most recent standalone dispatch line failed:** the neighbour has the latest direct implementation evidence, but this snapshot contains WORKSTATE summaries rather than the underlying September 7 primary reports. Treat the reported negative screens as a serious anti-duplication warning and request the manifests before spending on a new build; do not promote the summary into an independently rerun result. [S3]

**Mechanics:** neither project gets a blanket 'exact' badge. Prefer exact authoritative transition/command comparisons with the affected source and supported domain. The neighbour found replay ordering and CHOP context defects; main documents terrain/cost, state-transaction, map-generation and frozen-replay traps. A proof for one domain cannot bless another runner. [S8, S12, S13]

**Neural selection:** the main programme's preregistered locked-panel verdict is stronger evidence about its own recipe than a general claim that neural learning should eventually win. The neighbour's rejected selector is a different experiment and does not independently replicate or falsify whole-policy self-play. [S15, S4]

**Independent validation infrastructure:** combine the main project's publication/reproduction discipline and sealed-bank lifecycle with the neighbour's exact-identity adaptive-opponent collector and export audits. Neither alone is sufficient: a sealed but unrealistic zoo is wrong-target evidence, and a real-agent pilot repeatedly reused for selection is no longer confirmation. [S9, S10, S12, S13]
