# Two projects: what to do now

**7 September 2026 — chatgpt_1, task A-1**

## Leave the current bot alone

The shared account holds the neighbour's **V439**, not our old champion. Its September 5 completed rollout was **23.43, rank 28**; the September 7 snapshot shows **23.35, still rank 28**. Our last champion rollout was **19.23, rank 60**. V439 is the sensible incumbent, but these separate rollouts do not prove a transferable four-point improvement.

## The next job is not “write an orchard bot from scratch”

Both projects have already built standalone controllers. The neighbour's September 7 dispatch versions lost their tests; a later funding experiment was still active in the snapshot. Calling another allocator “standalone” does not make it a new idea. Read those results before commissioning a duplicate.

There is a more specific opening. The native top-player reconstruction shows that our failed port **mistook seven trees standing at one moment for a limit on how many trees to grow during the game**. It also stopped harvesting when it switched to wood. The real bot replaces trees and keeps fruit and wood work going together. Changing the switch time never repaired that missing cycle.

That is a reason for **one bounded, conditional attempt to recover the missing behaviour**, not a promise of ten rating points. Strong players can do productive renewal with two workers; other strong players use more. Our own census already shows putibuzu buying a second worker that can harvest. “Every good second worker has harvest zero” was an overgeneralization.

## Stop paying for the wrong evidence

More fruit, more workers, more trees and even more final own score have repeatedly produced fewer wins. A legal crop cycle must also pay for the actions it displaces and survive an opponent who takes its fruit or wood.

Use one chain: prove the mechanism; reproduce the exact program independently; test one candidate against actual opponents; open one untouched confirmation set; only then request a ladder comparison. The existing sealed set already has 512 map blocks. Its standard two-bot comparison costs **1,024 games**, not five times that. Small rating claims are expensive: four rollouts per bot still give only roughly one-point resolution under the optimistic recent noise estimate.

## Three decisions

**UNIFY** — one coordinator and one account queue for both projects. Keep their code histories; do not merge every experimental bot.

**TURNOVER** — authorize one bounded offline audit/design/prototype, but only if the missing mechanism is genuinely different from the neighbour's completed attempts. No parallel generic rewrite or new neural tuning campaign.

**HOLD** — keep V439 and the platform freeze. Actual-opponent tests and ladder replacement need a separate approval with the exact candidate and game budget.

The detailed plan sets deadlines, named roles, stopping rules and a maximum conditional unranked budget of 1,092 games. None of those games was run for this analysis. Some historical mature timestamps and top-ten boundaries are missing; the ledger marks them missing rather than inventing them.
