## 5. The common mechanism — with quantitative limits

### 5.1 The diagnosis is productivity and renewal, not worker count alone

In the V440 comparison, own felling is concentrated at size one: **1,702 of 1,931 own-tree fellings, 88.14%**. V440 performs 166.8 chops per game for roughly 46 wood, whereas delineate performs 169.8 for roughly 98. The recorded wood-per-chop values are 0.28 versus 0.58. Delineate plants 40.6 times against V440's 12.3, and its wood by successive 100-turn periods is 7.6, 31.9, 58.6 versus 14.8, 16.5, 14.7. That is an investment/production trajectory, not simply an extra worker. The near equality of total chop counts makes chop utilization, maturity and bankable capacity relevant. These are cross-policy descriptive cohorts, not a controlled intervention. [S5]

The rank-seven target offers a useful counterexample to 'double wood'. Putibuzu's corresponding score is 246 versus V440's 215, with fruit 61 versus 31 and wood points about 185 versus 184. Most of this particular raw-score gap is fruit, not a twofold wood increase. Putibuzu's later profile also has a high replant rate and about two workers. A proposed wood-only policy cannot claim to implement every strong two-worker economy. [S5, S6]

The later field profile gives putibuzu **114/116 two-worker games (98.28%)** and tonigineer **121/122 (99.18%)**, with own-cell replant fractions around 83% and 86%. Delineate's two-worker *subset* also produces well, but selecting games by final worker count is outcome-conditioned: it is not a counterfactual experiment in forbidding worker three. Use those traces to find mechanisms, not to estimate the causal value of a global roster cap. [S6]

### 5.2 Resolve the worker-talent disagreement rather than voting on it

The main census's 'wood-troll club' describes yaichi, Stounate, skotz and yamo. In the SAME report, putibuzu's trained second worker has harvesting in 100% of the sample; 2/2/2/2 occurs 69 times. The September profile again has 2/2/2/2 as its most frequent current vector, 49 cases. Different dates and windows change counts; neither source supports 'putibuzu's second worker cannot harvest'. The board and handover generalized the first cohort too far. Fix that statement upstream at integration. [S6, S7]

### 5.3 A mature tree is not free production

A size-four tree yields four wood, worth 16 points before collection and banking. At chop power two, a mature banana takes three chop actions, versus six for plum/lemon and ten for apple. But compared with a size-one banana, the wood-per-chop improvement is **2.67 times**, not four: maturity also raises health. Growth, one seed, the planting turn, carrying capacity, contested wood allocation and the return trip all matter. Banana growth is 24 turns on plain terrain versus 16 near water; apple is 36 versus 8. Species preference is state-dependent, not simply 'bananas always'. [S5, S7, S8]

The production object should be a **completed resource transaction**: seed obtained from an attainable source; seed survives banking/training decisions; planting succeeds; growth and possible harvest produce the next seed; wood is actually collected; cargo reaches the bank; a free site receives a replacement where profitable. A tree count or nominal standing 16 points is an intermediate state, not realized value. The opponent may harvest or cut the crop, and harvesting theft is not captured by a chop-only stop latch. [S8, S14]

### 5.4 The native port identifies a concrete missing transition system

At turn 100, the real native bot and the port both have **6.60 wood points**, but native plants are **9.44 versus 4.01**. At turn 150, native has **49.32 wood points and 15.13 plants**, port **26.05 and 7.34**. At turn 200 the figures are **154.72/21.53** versus **61.65/10.34**. End values are **334.16 wood points and 31.89 plants** versus **99.95 and 13.01**. These are useful diagnostic trajectories from different samples, not map-independent acceptance thresholds or a paired effect estimate. [S11]

The native controller continues harvesting during the later wood phase and replaces/thins crops. The port's seven standing trees became a lifetime production cap; its modes were mutually exclusive where the native behaviour has asynchronous flags/jobs. Recover the T/second-flag behaviour, admission and replacement, own-mature thinning, target choice and worker funding together. 'Move the Produce→Deforest turn' repaired none of that. The native first wood is around turn 97 and its later switch around 153; early investment itself is not disproven. [S11]

### 5.5 Why the neighbour's new allocator also failed

On one real putibuzu block V543/V564 train a 2/1/1/3 second worker at turn two, then issue **zero CHOP for the entire game**, ending with zero wood. They repeatedly harvest available fruit while a missing lemon component makes the third-worker bill unattainable. The chopper has power three but only one carrying slot. A role hierarchy can be locally consistent and globally unproductive. [S12]

The next_bot repair actually removes this zero-wood failure, reserves shared stock and compares collectible trip returns. It still does not win more on fresh blocks. In the V368/V468 counterexample, our own wood changes only 28→29 while timely plum denial prevents the opponent's second hire and collapses its wood from 61 to 9. This demonstrates a strategic interaction excluded by own-output optimization, not a general instruction to maximize denial. The eventual isolated denial confirmation tied on wins. [S4, S12]

Thus the joint hypothesis is narrower than 'add an orchard': **maintain renewable productive capacity while allocating scarce actions/cargo/seed capital between banking, replacement, investment and timely interference.** Its truth as a design hypothesis is plausible; its profitable implementation is not yet established.

## 6. Evaluation: four different questions need four different gates

### Mechanics and deployment

A fixture proves a specified action/state property. Export replay equality proves the tested programs agree on the supplied states. Neither proves profitable closed-loop play. A compile/runtime measurement must use the exact source, actual Rust toolchain and agreed limits. Do not advertise the policy-flat repair as meeting its original 20% compile-cost improvement criterion: that target was missed, and a later ≤1.10 ratio contract was used. Scope changes can be legitimate when explicitly reauthorized; they cannot retrospectively become the original pass. [S3]

The two projects have found real semantic gaps: MOVE destination normalization, terrain versus league training-cost assumptions, initial plant ordering, and growth cancellation during CHOP. Historical frozen action replay also diverges after a candidate changes the world. A runner is exact on validated domains and cases, not automatically every counterfactual. The reported original/compact equality across real replays is useful and must remain separate from adaptation and strategy. [S8, S12, S13]

### Local competitive screening

Compare identical initial states, seats and opponent policies, then let opponents respond to each changed world. Use final **W + 0.5D** per game as primary outcome; report own score, opponent score, margin, resource transfers, no-progress episodes and deployment failures as diagnostics. Aggregate correlated seat/opponent cases by the independent map. Never call 192 evaluations on eight maps 192 independent maps. Never exclude a timeout because it obscures the policy's promise. [S12, S13]

The main four-policy zoo and neighbour twelve-family generator are cheap diagnostics with demonstrated selection failures. A duel with our own clear-cutter favors related styles; orchard6 losing 324/400 such games did not establish its relative ladder position. Conversely, a fresh-map proxy win remains a proxy result. Neither instrument should be the final promotion authority. [S9, S12]

### The sealed bank: reuse it, but state its scope

The existing instrument at commit `46c39d9848636436bf55e011b8768b7bf3c85885` contains active 512-map bank 001 and standby bank 002, with five locked real-agent identities. **One opponent is assigned to each map/player-zero block**. A two-arm comparison costs **1,024 games**, not 512×5×2. Adding both seats would cost 2,048 and requires an explicitly verified runner extension; do not pretend that extension has already been delivered. No seeds or keys were accessed for this analysis. Before use the independent reviewer must verify the current opening/retirement receipts; the build-time zero-open report is not a fresh inspection. [S10]

The local calibration's arm SD 40.203456 yields a normal half-width of **4.9249 local margin points** at 512 maps. Using its measured paired-difference SD 47.930793 yields **4.1518**. These are local score-margin calculations, not real-agent match-point power. Even under the same paired SD, an approximate two-sided 5%/80%-power detectable effect is **5.9346**, larger than the confidence half-width. Reusing '±5' as both precision and powered effect would be incorrect. [`audit.py`; S10]

For a paired match-point difference in [-1,1], a conservative variance bound gives a normal-approximation half-width around **8.66 percentage points** at 512 independent blocks. A distribution-free Hoeffding 95% bound is **12.00 points**, assuming independent blocks. Actual paired variance may be far smaller, but it must be measured rather than borrowed from margin. Timestamped opponent identities must remain fixed within a gate; an updated boundary-player mix can be proposed and frozen BEFORE opening, not selected after seeing maps. [Reasoning and calculations; S10, S13]

### Ladder confirmation and the cost of small claims

For independent equal-size arm means under a fixed known SD, the illustrative half-width is `1.96 * SD * sqrt(2/n)`. The recent five-run SD gives:

| Rollouts per bot | Total games at 160/rollout | Recent-SD half-width | Older pooled-SD sensitivity |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 320 | 2.260 | 4.161 |
| 4 | 1,280 | 1.130 | 2.080 |
| 6 | 1,920 | 0.923 | 1.699 |
| 21 | 6,720 | 0.493 | 0.908 |

The older pooled within-family SD is 1.501. With it, about 18 rollouts per bot are needed for a one-point half-width and 70 for a half-point under the same formula. These are **sensitivity calculations**, not valid empirical coverage guarantees for a drifting ladder with five recent observations. They omit small-sample SD uncertainty, correlations and schedule effects. The board's 'nothing under 2.2' is what one approximate comparison buys, not a universal evidential wall; a gap above 2.2 is not automatically conclusive either. [S2, S9; `audit.py`]

The efficient combined pipeline is therefore mechanics → limited local falsification → frozen adaptive real-agent pilot → one sealed real-agent comparison → explicitly authorized interleaved mature ladder block. Its exact costs and stop rules are in [PLAN-2026-09-07.md](PLAN-2026-09-07.md). No positive margin-to-rating conversion appears anywhere in that plan.
