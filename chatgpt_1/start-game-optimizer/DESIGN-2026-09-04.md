# Proper start-game optimizer — design

Date: 2026-09-04  
Task: `20260904-start-game-optimizer-design`  
Status: design only; no build or platform action

## Decision in one paragraph

Build a **receding-horizon, event-driven dynamic programme with best-first branch-and-bound**, not another optimizer for the turn a troll is trained. It compares each candidate opening with the unchanged champion on the same finite, contested world and maximizes the expected **final score-margin improvement at game end**, with own-score improvement reported separately. `PLANT` is a first-class searched action beside harvesting, chopping, mining and training. Existing and planted trees form a finite wood ledger; no forecast may multiply a rate by the turns remaining without also proving that enough trees and worker time exist. A deterministic champion plan is always the incumbent and fallback. The online search returns the best replay-valid plan found by its deadline; the offline version may certify an optimum only inside its published model, scenario set and macro-action vocabulary.

This design deliberately allows the answer to be **do not plant** and **do not train another troll**. It does not force the owner's orchard hypothesis or the roster hypothesis into the result.

## 1. Objective: paired final value, not roster time

The previous objective — minimize the third troll's arrival turn — is disproved as a strength objective. Stage 2A reached three trolls about 23 game turns before its field and lost 4.13 rating points. The later submitted optimizer reached its third troll at median game turn 25, roughly 71 turns before its field, and still scored about 19 points a game less than the champion. Earlier is therefore a diagnostic, not value.

Let:

- `s` be the live state at a planning event;
- `C` be the frozen champion continuation policy;
- `pi` be a candidate sequence of opening macro-actions followed by `C` after hand-back;
- `omega` be one fixed opponent/raid scenario;
- `M(pi, omega) = score_us(300) - score_opp(300)`, using the terminal score if the referee ends the game earlier.

The paired value of a candidate is

```text
DeltaMargin(pi, omega) = M(pi -> C, omega) - M(C, omega)
DeltaOwn(pi, omega)    = score_us(pi -> C, omega) - score_us(C, omega)
```

Candidate and baseline use the same map, draw, seat, opponent scenario and random seed. The primary search objective is the mean `DeltaMargin` over the fixed scenario set. The first tie-break is the lower fifth of the `DeltaMargin` distribution; the second is mean `DeltaOwn`; the third is fewer irreversible commitments. A plan is not allowed to override the champion when its conservative estimated advantage is below one wood unit, four points.

Why margin rather than raw own score: the ladder rewards wins, and `PLANT`, `HARVEST` and `CHOP` alter what the opponent can later score. The project's instrument audit also found paired score margin materially more informative than the old win indicator. Own score remains mandatory in every report and a plan with a negative conservative `DeltaOwn` is flagged; it is not silently hidden by a denial gain.

The optimizer must report, but never optimize directly for:

- second- and third-troll arrival turns;
- number of plants;
- standing wood;
- bank at intermediate turns.

Those are explanatory columns. The target is paired game-end points under an explicit continuation.

## 2. Continuation and baseline

`C` is the current champion's ordinary policy, frozen at the implementation pin. Its required memory is represented explicitly and advanced by the same simulated views as the candidate. The live bot keeps an always-current **shadow champion**. When the optimizer hands back, it stops overriding the shadow; there is no controller restart with guessed state.

For hypothetical leaves, the compact continuation-relevant state travels with the search node: focus species, regeneration commitments, carried seed commitments, current targets and any other field that changes later decisions. Strings and map geometry do not travel with nodes; they are represented by small IDs into immutable tables.

The unchanged champion itself is the value control. An "optimizer-off" build may be used as a regression check only if it produces byte-identical command streams; it is not a substitute for a mechanically sound control.

## 3. Published action vocabulary

The search branches on **joint macro-actions** at decision events. Every macro expands into ordinary commands and is replayed command by command through the maintained referee. Event-driven means fewer branch points, not approximate mechanics.

| Game verb | Treatment in the optimizer |
|---|---|
| `MOVE` | The target is searched. The route is fixed to a deterministic shortest path because units do not block one another. Equal shortest paths use one canonical tie-break. |
| `HARVEST` | Search tree, amount/stop event, and whether the cargo is for banking, a bill or a seed. Quantities include one item, exact remaining need and fill-to-capacity. Waiting for the next fruit is an explicit event choice. |
| `PICK` | Chosen as the resource-reservation step of a planting plan. After the actual `PICK`, the held seed and intended cells are in state and may be repaired if the cell is taken. |
| `PLANT` | **Fully searched:** species, cell, troll and timing. It competes directly with harvest, chop, mine and train value. `NO_PLANT` is always legal. |
| `DROP` | Search bank door and timing. Partial loads are allowed when banking now changes a train or planting decision. |
| `CHOP` | Search tree and stop event. A normal macro fells and banks the tree; a partial chop is generated only when an exogenous tree event, contest or planning horizon occurs before completion. |
| `MINE` | Search mining cell and amount: exact deficit, one action, or fill-to-capacity. Iron is not treated as depleting. |
| `TRAIN` | Search the full legal talent tuple and exact turn, including the choice never to train. There is no fixed `2/3/0/c` menu. |
| `WAIT` | Search only until the next relevant event: fruit, growth, delivery, training affordability or opponent/tree change. Arbitrary turn-by-turn waiting is dominated. |

All own trolls are assigned jointly. Resource stock, trees, planting cells and targets are reserved during combination so two trolls cannot spend the same seed, claim the same fruit or unknowingly duplicate a tree job.

### Planting cells

The exact offline vocabulary contains every reachable, legal, non-door cell. A cell may be removed only by a recorded dominance proof: for the same species another cell is no worse for every relevant own travel, water cooldown, expected opponent arrival and door interference, and strictly better in at least one. The online beam may cap the surviving frontier, but then it must call its answer "best found", not optimal.

## 4. Search state and algorithm

A state contains every fact that changes future transitions:

- game turn and current-turn training lock;
- banked inventory and score;
- each own troll's talents, position, cargo and active macro-job;
- every relevant tree's identity, species, cell, size, health, fruits, cooldown and provenance;
- held seeds, reserved stock and intended planting cells;
- trees planted by the candidate and their exact growth schedule;
- observable opponent roster/positions and scenario weights;
- the compact shadow-champion continuation state.

Geometry, distance maps, species constants and scenario definitions are immutable shared tables.

The previous minimum-time A* becomes a maximum-reward best-first search:

```text
incumbent = exact champion continuation
queue     = initial state, ordered by optimistic final paired value

while queue not empty and before deadline:
    state = pop largest upper bound
    discard if upper_bound(state) <= incumbent_value
    generate legal joint macro-actions
    advance exactly to the next decision event
    apply Pareto/dominance pruning
    when a state reaches the opening horizon:
        run the fixed continuation to game end
        replace incumbent if paired final value improves
```

For an equal structural key, state A dominates B when A is no later, has at least as much bank/cargo/secured score, has no additional irreversible seed or job obligation, and has a no-worse tree ledger. Tree state, positions, ownership, scenario facts and continuation memory remain in the key; they may not be hidden in a monotone resource vector.

The online implementation is two-stage:

1. **Generator:** a fast expected-contestation model expands many states and preserves a Pareto frontier of complete opening plans.
2. **Exact re-ranker:** the best frontier plans are replayed under the fixed opponent scenarios through the exact referee and frozen continuation. Only replay-valid plans may replace the incumbent.

The offline oracle may run the generator to exhaustion and report a score interval `[best feasible, best remaining upper bound]`. That certificate is only for the deterministic surrogate and published vocabulary. The stochastic scenario ranking has no global optimality claim.

## 5. Finite, contested forest and the effect of planting

The central correction is a **wood ledger**, not a production rate.

For every existing or explicitly planted tree `i`, the model knows or bounds:

- the release times of each size/fruit state;
- wood units available if felled at a given time;
- service time to reach, fell, carry and bank it for each troll;
- survival in each opponent scenario;
- whether another own job already reserves it.

For a state `s`, the optimistic wood bound is constrained twice:

```text
resource bound = wood mass on surviving existing trees
                 + wood mass on trees explicitly planted by this plan

labour bound   = relaxed worker-time/carry assignment from now to turn 300

convertible wood <= min(resource bound, labour bound)
```

The labour bound may ignore collisions to remain optimistic, but it may not invent trees. A relaxed min-cost assignment matches tree release windows to troll travel/chop/drop capacity. Final wood points are at most four times this convertible mass.

A `PLANT(kind, cell)` transition:

1. spends an actual seed and the troll turns needed to pick and plant;
2. creates the exact species- and water-dependent growth timeline;
3. adds no wood before that timeline releases it;
4. adds only wood that can still be felled and banked by turn 300;
5. is removed or discounted in scenarios where the opponent raids it first.

Fruit regrowth does not create free infinite production. Every harvest, retained seed, replant and maintenance turn is explicit. A rate multiplied by time may be used only as a labour upper bound and is always capped by this finite resource ledger. This directly prevents the tenfold overstatement measured in the wood-charging experiment.

## 6. Opponent model: scenarios, not an idle board

The first implementation should not attempt full minimax. The opponent cannot block routes or exhaust iron; it matters by taking or changing trees, occupying planting cells, harvesting fruit and building its own orchard.

Use a deterministic, map-seeded ensemble with common random numbers for candidate and champion:

1. an early clear-cutter/champion-like opponent;
2. an orchard-building harvester based on the reconstructed top-bot order;
3. a mixed strong opening with early training and planting;
4. a high-raid stress case using the upper measured post-turn-100 rate.

Use at least two fixed seeds per archetype for exact re-ranking. The fast generator uses tree survival hazards calibrated to the same ensemble. The current aggregate anchors are 0.19 raids per 100 near-tree turns before turn 100 and 0.6–1.0 after; opponent orchard volume is about 25.8 plants and 23.5 harvested fruit per game. The orchard-kinetics read should refine these by time and distance.

Every live turn replaces prediction with observation. A missing tree, shortened harvest, occupied plant cell or unexpected opponent roster immediately invalidates the affected macro and re-roots the search. Scenario weights may be updated from observed planting/chopping style, but the first build must also report the equal-weight result so adaptation cannot hide a bad base model.

An idle opponent is retained only as a diagnostic upper bound. It is never part of the value gate.

## 7. Horizon, execution and hand-back

The optimizer always evaluates leaves through turn 300, but it does not search arbitrary opening commands for 300 turns.

The opening-control horizon `H` is selected on development data from `{80, 100, 120, 150}` by a rule fixed now:

> Choose the smallest `H` whose paired final value is within two points of the `H = 150` result on at least 90% of development maps and by which at least 90% of selected planting/training commitments have either paid out or been abandoned.

The orchard-kinetics curve supplies the evidence. A provisional expectation is around turn 120 because top-bot orchards overtake wild trees around turns 40–70 and previous extra trolls arrived around turns 75–108, but 120 is not hard-coded before the curve exists.

On turn 1, the planner constructs a complete incumbent and searches. It executes only the first command of the selected macro. Later turns normally follow the stored macro; a local search runs only when a macro completes, a target changes, or a periodic validation event fires.

Hand-back is **one way** for the game:

- at `H`;
- earlier when no replay-valid plan exceeds the shadow champion by the four-point activation margin;
- when live orchard/forest value leaves the model's calibrated envelope;
- or when repair cannot finish inside its internal deadline.

Once handed back, the optimizer does not retake control. This avoids a new source of controller-level dancing.

## 8. Compute and representation budget

The existing generic Rust planner proved a 182,787-state reduced case in about 378 ms and 84 MiB, but that is not a real-map promise. The build must measure the complete path — parsing, geometry, baseline, search, exact re-ranking, command creation and storage cleanup — on one core.

Initial internal turn-1 allocation:

```text
0–100 ms    parse, distances, non-dominated planting cells, champion incumbent
100–650 ms  bounded DP / branch-and-bound generator
650–850 ms  exact scenario re-ranking of the retained frontier
850 ms      hard internal stop; 150 ms reserved for jitter and output
```

Later planning events have a 35 ms internal stop: at most 5 ms validation/state update, 20 ms repair search and 10 ms re-ranking. Ordinary plan-following turns do not rebuild the search.

Required representation:

- packed scalar state and tree IDs;
- immutable geometry shared once;
- preallocated node arena and heap of node IDs;
- parent ID plus compact macro action, never cloned action vectors;
- incremental fingerprints and bounded Pareto labels;
- storage reused across turns so destruction is not on the timed path;
- deterministic champion incumbent available before the search clock starts.

The future candidate must remain below 100,000 UTF-16 code units. Peak resident memory must be no more than 128 MiB and must retain at least 25% headroom below the platform's recorded limit, whichever condition is stricter.

## 9. What this design needs from `20260904-orchard-kinetics`

Do not duplicate that read. Consume it as a machine-readable calibration table plus its plain-language verdict. Required fields are:

- by species, water status and plant turn: first fruit, size/health timeline and wood available at turns 100/150/200/250/300;
- worker turns for `PICK -> MOVE -> PLANT`, maintenance/harvest, felling and banking by distance and representative troll talents;
- net final points of one planting turn versus one chopping turn;
- survival/raid curves by time and distance from both camps;
- the champion's unaided 9.8-tree contribution on the same scale;
- the best orchard size/timing before raid and labour costs dominate;
- whether any third-troll tuple becomes positive when the orchard, rather than the bare forest, is in the ledger.

The exact referee remains authoritative. These tables seed bounds, candidate ordering and falsification checks; they do not replace replay.

**No-code stop:** if the accepted read cannot find an orchard schedule worth at least eight net final points per game after seed, displaced work and measured raids, positive on at least 60% of development maps, no optimizer build follows.

## 10. Gates for a future build

The 24-map smoke and pinned 200-map panel are development data. They may test mechanics and tune no threshold after the fresh holdout is revealed.

### Gate A — model and vocabulary, before integration

- machine-readable action vocabulary committed;
- toy case where `PLANT` creates later bankable wood and beats immediate chop;
- counterexample where rate-times-remaining would buy a troll but the finite ledger correctly refuses it;
- every generated macro replays exactly through the referee;
- offline search reports its model/vocabulary score gap rather than calling a bounded result optimal.

### Gate B — mechanics and compute on development data

- unchanged champion is the control;
- optimizer-off command stream is byte-identical to the champion;
- candidate and control independently pass 34 fixtures and 24/24 smoke maps with no stall, illegal command, timeout or state divergence;
- every searched verb, including `PLANT`, is exercised by a positive and negative test;
- turn-1 p99 below 850 ms, warm p99 below 40 ms, maxima below 1,000/50 ms on one core;
- source and memory caps pass;
- online search is within four final-margin points of the offline best on at least 80% of development maps, with the full budget-quality curve reported.

### Gate C — forecast calibration on development data

At turns 100/150/200/250/300, predicted added convertible wood is compared with exact replay. Mean absolute error must be at most two wood units per game and the 90th-percentile overstatement must stay below 1.5 times realised wood. The old tenfold error is the failure this gate exists to catch.

### Gate D — sealed fresh holdout

The coordinator selects map hashes and opponent seeds not used in any previous smoke, panel, threshold choice or code inspection. Prefer newly collected maps after the candidate design date. Before revealing them, record only the holdout manifest hash; freeze candidate source, scenario weights, horizon and thresholds. Reveal and run once. A changed candidate requires a new holdout.

Candidate and champion play identical maps, draws, seats and opponent scenarios. Cluster by map. Required result:

- 100% mechanics for both arms;
- paired mean `DeltaMargin` at least +8 points with its 95% lower bound above zero;
- paired `DeltaOwn` lower bound not below zero;
- non-negative mean margin in at least three of the four opponent archetypes;
- forecast calibration remains inside Gate C's bounds.

A candidate that is itself an opponent is never included as a self-play field cell.

### Gate E — external confirmation

Only after the fresh holdout passes: a real-field burst against the held-out Legend agents, then one full interleaved ladder block under the current queue rules. Alternate candidate and identical champion readings to reduce ladder drift. A difference smaller than the identical champion file's measured 1.68-point spread is inconclusive, not a promotion.

## 11. Falsification

This design is wrong, and the line stops, if any of the following occurs:

1. the orchard-kinetics no-code gate fails;
2. the best offline `PLANT`-aware plan cannot beat champion continuation by eight points, or the optimizer almost always selects the champion/no-plant incumbent;
3. the finite-forest forecast has the wrong sign on exact replay, exceeds the 1.5-times overstatement bound, or only works against the idle/soft opponent;
4. the online budget cannot recover within four points of the offline result;
5. either candidate or control fails mechanics before value is read;
6. the sealed holdout misses any Gate D value condition;
7. the gain is concentrated in one opponent archetype and reverses under the high-raid scenario;
8. the search again selects an early weak troll without first creating enough convertible wood to pay for it.

A fresh-holdout failure may inform a new design, but the same holdout is then development data and may not be reused as proof.

## 12. Recommended implementation order after an owner go

1. accept the orchard-kinetics table and apply the no-code gate;
2. add exact `PLANT`/growth/tree-ledger transitions to the offline oracle;
3. prove the two new toy cases and replay every macro;
4. run the offline score search on development maps and publish the action vocabulary and score gaps;
5. specialize the Rust anytime engine, retaining the champion incumbent;
6. pass mechanics, budget and forecast calibration;
7. freeze the candidate and run the sealed holdout once;
8. request external confirmation only after the holdout passes.

The first candidate should be free to remain a two-troll orchard bot. A third troll is an output of the final-value search, not a requirement.

## Design verdict

**ACCEPT FOR A FUTURE BUILD, CONDITIONAL ON THE ORCHARD-KINETICS NO-CODE GATE.** The correct optimizer is a final-value planner over a finite contested forest with `PLANT` inside its action space and the champion as incumbent. It is not another faster way to buy a troll.
