## The findings, ranked

### 1. `CHAMPION-BEHAVIOUR.md` §4 — the substitute train rule does not reproduce the observed distribution

The document says: *"A rule that reproduces the observed distribution: wait until the best
affordable worker has ms + cc + chop >= 5, then buy it; buy unconditionally by turn 35."*

Measured on the same 160 recordings (`train_rule_test.py`): the rule buys on the champion's
turn — and then always the champion's bundle — in **63 of 160 matches (39 %)**. In the other 97
it buys **earlier**, by a median of 10 turns and up to 27, and usually a weaker worker:

| match | the champion bought | the rule buys |
|---|---|---|
| 900571120 | 2/2/0/2 at turn 14 | 2/1/0/2 at turn 1 |
| 900571124 | 1/3/0/2 at turn 32 | 1/2/0/2 at turn 6 |
| 900571125 | 2/2/0/2 at turn 22 | 2/1/0/2 at turn 10 |
| 900571267 | 2/2/0/3 at turn 6 | 2/2/0/2 at turn 1 |

Thresholds 4 and 6 do no better (60 and 59 of 160). `DOMAIN.md` §1.2 records that fielding a
weaker worker goes with losing, so an implementer who follows this sentence builds a different
and probably weaker bot while believing it matches.

**Edit.** State the measured agreement the way §5.2 already does for tree choice: *"the best
simple rule found reproduces the purchase turn in 63 of 160; when it misses it buys earlier, by a
median of 10 turns"*. Then give the implementer the data to fit their own rule: for each of the
160 matches, the shack contents on every turn up to the purchase and what was bought — a small
JSON exported by `spec-work/measure.py` into the package. Two more facts worth one sentence each:
in 159 of 160 the champion bought on the first turn its bundle became affordable (already in the
document — the implementer needs it next to the rule), and because each talent's price draws on
its own resource, *"the largest ms + cc + chop"* is simply *"each talent at the largest value its
own resource affords"* — the best bundle was unique in all 160 purchases.

### 2. `harness/referee.py` + `RULES.md` §10 — a tree planted this turn can be chopped this turn in the harness; on the platform it cannot

Match 900572315, turns 258 and 262: seat 0's troll 2 plants a BANANA, then an APPLE, on cell
(10,7) while seat 1's troll 3, standing on the same cell, prints `CHOP 3`. The platform: the
sapling is untouched (BANANA size 1 health 3; APPLE size 1 health 11). `referee.py`: the banana
is killed and removed; the apple is at health 9. This is **the only rule disagreement** found in
39,176 replayed turns (the other differences are all the random tie-break and one timeout, below).

`RULES.md` §10 has the right conclusion for the wrong reason: *"PLANT happens before CHOP, so a
seed planted on a cell this turn cannot be chopped this turn"* — if PLANT really ran first, the
chop would land on the seed.

**Edit.** `referee.py`: take the set of cells that hold a tree **before** applying this turn's
PLANTs and let CHOP act only on those. `RULES.md` §10: *"CHOP acts only on trees that existed
before this turn's PLANTs; a seed planted this turn cannot be chopped this turn."*

### 3. `RULES.md` §12, `CONSTRAINTS.md` §3, `referee.py` — the time rule is stated wrongly

Both documents say three overruns of at most 50 ms are tolerated, a fourth loses, and a single
overrun of more than 50 ms over the limit loses on the spot. The recordings show the platform's
actual rule in its own words (match 900574900): *"$0 exceeded the time limit: 85 ms, 1st strike"*
(frame 298), *"56 ms, 2nd strike"* (352), *"60 ms, 3rd strike"* → *"$0: timeout"* (358). Elsewhere
a 97 ms turn and a 51 ms turn each drew a first strike and nothing more. So: **an overrun is a
strike; the third strike loses.** Nothing in the evidence supports the "more than 50 ms over"
clause. `referee.py` loses on the fourth (`overruns > 3`).

**Edit.** Both documents: *"a turn over the limit is a strike; the third strike loses the match
(observed on the platform). Whether a single very long turn loses at once is not known."*
`referee.py`: lose on the third strike.

### 4. `harness/README.md` — the calibration paragraph is wrong for this harness

*"The reference bot's own scores on these maps are in the range 120–220 with a mean near 180, and
against itself it wins about half the time."* Measured, reference against itself on all 24 maps
in both seats (`reference-vs-reference-48.json`): scores **59–220, mean 130**; 16 wins, 16 losses,
**16 draws**; mean length 198 turns; 2 of 48 reach turn 300; 18 of 48 end because *both players
have nothing left*. The 120–220 / 180 figures are the ladder figures from `CHAMPION-BEHAVIOUR.md`
§1. A mirror match on a point-symmetric map is a different regime — both bots log out the same
map and the ending clock does the rest — and the implementer reads step 2 against this paragraph.

**Edit.** Quote the measured mirror baseline and say why it is lower than the ladder numbers;
ship `reference-vs-reference-48.json` in the harness as the step-2 baseline.

### 5. `DOMAIN.md` §2 — the apple-farm line quotes one reading of a six-reading series

*"An apple-focused farm | 17.6"* is round 6 (2026-08-28 14:17Z, rank 99 of 176), read the same
afternoon the reference bot itself re-read 18.2. Rounds 1–4 (2026-08-27) read **19.8, 19.8, 18.6,
19.9** against the reference's 21.2 that day. The table's own rule is *"compare readings only
against readings taken the same day"*; this line breaks it. Same paragraph: *"the same bot re-read
18.2 three days later"* — the re-read (2026-08-28 15:22Z) was the next day.

**Edit.** Give the series with the same-day reference beside each; "the next day".

### 6. `CHAMPION-BEHAVIOUR.md` §4 — a citation contradicts the document's own range

*"game 900571124, turn 32 — the latest purchase in the corpus"* — the corpus maximum is turn 35
(`observations.json` → `train.turn.max`; the same paragraph says "range 1..35"). Turn 32 is the
latest among the eight matches cited.

**Edit.** "the latest among the cited matches", or cite the turn-35 match.

### 7. `RULES.md` §9 + `referee.py` — the platform accepts numeric item codes

Opponents in the recordings print `PICK 0 1` and `PLANT 0 3`, and the platform executes them:
match 900571120 turn 1, `PICK 0 1` moves one LEMON from the shack to troll 0 (lemons 4 → 3,
carry +1 lemon); turn 4, `PLANT 0 1` plants it. So 0 PLUM, 1 LEMON, 2 APPLE, 3 BANANA are valid
item arguments. `referee.py` treats a numeric item as a fatal "bad item" — 1,282 of the 40,458
recorded turns would have lost the match in the harness. The implementer will not print numbers,
so the experiment is unaffected; but §12's promise *"only an unrecognised verb is fatal"* is then
not what the harness does. (Lowercase verbs — `move`, 721 times — are also accepted by the
platform; `referee.py` uppercases them, which is fine.)

**Edit.** One line in `referee.py` accepting digits 0–5 as item codes, and one sentence in §9.

### 8. `RULES.md` §6 — one word

*"aged a random number of ticks between 1 and base_cooldown × 7"* — the generator uses the tree's
**effective** cooldown (water-adjusted) × 7. No consequence for the implementer; one word.

## Harness robustness (not rule defects, but the implementer will hit them)

- **No read timeout.** `referee.py` blocks on `readline()`; a candidate that hangs or forgets
  to flush hangs the whole run silently. A hard wall (a few seconds) that scores the match as
  "stopped responding" is ten lines.
- **No per-turn trace.** The refinement loop asks the implementer to *"list the gaps as game
  observations"*, but the runner prints one result line per match. A `--trace FILE` writing each
  turn's state and both command lines is twenty lines and the runner already has everything.
- The module docstring says *"every match is played twice by default (--both-seats)"*; the flag
  is off by default. One line.

## What passed — verified by execution

**Leakage.** Zero `.rs`. No source, module, function or parameter names in any document. A grep
for our internal vocabulary over the six documents finds only ordinary English ("noise band",
"phase", "door", "hard floor"). The binary is stripped (`file`: stripped; `nm`: no symbols);
the one `_ZN` fragment `strings` finds is the Rust demangler's own literal, not a symbol; the
only local string is the build path `/tmp/claude-1000/cleanroom-build/reference.rs`, which
names nothing. The two substitute-rule numbers that coincide with constants in the source (buy by
turn 35; the loop from turn 251) are both fully supported by the recordings (the latest purchase
is turn 35; the 251 boundary is sharp in 152 matches), so a spectator could have written them.
Finding 1's threshold (`>= 5`) is *not* the source's rule — it is not a leak, it is just wrong.

**Parity.** The champion rebuilt from `readable/denial-off-champion.rs` (sha `4ce3d1e8…`, the
sha the spec-work notes record) with the same toolchain (rustc 1.97.1), `reference_parity.py`
re-run against the shipped binary (sha `b24c3a0e…`): **9,502 seat-turns over 24 maps, 0 differ**.

**The observations.** `spec-work/measure.py` re-run rewrites `observations.json` with **no
diff**. The corpus facts in `CHAMPION-BEHAVIOUR.md` (160 matches, seats 77/83, heights
33/43/45/39, 73 full matches, shortest 81, the verb counts, the 20 bundles bought, harvest talent
0 in 160/160, the seed order, the 251 boundary) are the file's numbers.

**`RULES.md` against the platform.** `referee_vs_recordings.py` replays all 160 recordings
through `referee.py`, each turn from the platform's own recorded state, and compares the next
state (39,176 turns; 1,282 skipped for finding 7): inventories, carries, talents, tree sets,
sizes, fruits and **cooldowns** agree everywhere except finding 2 and the one timeout match;
**87 of 87** rule-based early endings are predicted on the exact turn (72 matches ran to 300 in
both); all 1,164 move differences are the documented random tie-break — 1,135 landed on another
equal-best cell, and the remaining 29 stayed put because a teammate stood on the randomly chosen
cell (the platform logs *"can't move, target blocked"*). The constants (cooldowns 8/8/9/6, water
3/3/2/4, health 4+2s / 8+3s / 2+1s, cost n + t², wood 4, ending clock distance/speed + 6) match
the audited simulator in `rust/src/game/engine.rs`. The decoder's tree clocks, which §8.1
cautiously calls reconstructed, matched the platform's next state in every turn but two.

**`DOMAIN.md`.** The ladder readings in §2 match the readings ledger (three heroes 11.71 /
12.03; orchard 14.65 / 13.51 / 18.84 / 16.66 / 16.64; the floor 19.2 / 19.1 / 17.3; the ring
10.8 at 172) except finding 5; the corpus numbers in §1 match Track T's field review; the
starting stock (five draws from 2..10) matches the official generator.

**The harness.** Runs from the package directory alone; 48 mirror matches in 15 s; 0 crashes.
