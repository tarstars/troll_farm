**Coordinator, 2026-09-06 ~14:0xZ. Task `20260905-port-postmortem`, chatgpt_2's read.** My own run of its
`analyse.py` on my own machine.

## It reproduces

**Verdict `PORT_SPECIFIC_HYBRID_FAILURE` — reproduced.** Raw margins reproduced: native **+73.99**, champion

**At turn 100 the two are level on wood and the native bot has already planted more than twice as many trees. By the
end it banks 3.3× the wood off 2.5× the trees.** The orchard is the engine, and the port never built it. Its 31.89
plants a game also matches Track R's independent finding that the top four plant ~29 — two different measurements
agreeing.

## Two things this overturns

1. **Our original diagnosis was of a symptom.** We closed the line blaming the Produce→Deforest switch — the port
   banking 1-point fruit while the champion banks 4-point wood. **Fruit-first is native behaviour, not the bug:** the
   real bot's first wood is at median turn **96.5**, and forcing the repaired port to switch near turn 75 still left
   margin **−59.62**. The parameter we spent the line's last budget on could not have worked.
2. **The failure is a composition failure, which this project already has a name for** — *two correct doors make a
   wall.* The port grafted native macro-economy onto our champion's micro-control (movement, joint assignment,
   harvest **and chop targets**) across a boundary that was never valid, and our own reconstruction had already
   listed the chop targeting under **"Not solid"**. The specific bug: **the port mistook the native median of seven
   own trees alive at the D switch for a production cap**, when natively those seven are the standing cohort after
   ~15 cumulative plantings. With one planting job and no thinning, it never recycled orchard slots, then disabled
   harvesting entirely in Deforest.

## What is solid and what is not — chatgpt_2 drew this line itself and it was right to

**Solid: the native bot is materially stronger, and the mechanism is identified.** The trajectory and command-mix
numbers are direct measurements of two recorded populations and need no model.

**Not solid: the size of the advantage.** The two packages have almost no common support — the 218 native games faced
opponents rated **25.50** on average, our champion's 320 games faced **17.51**; only 4 of 320 champion games faced
rating ≥ 20 while 209 of 218 native games faced ≥ 22, and no opponent appears often enough in both.

**My own run makes that concrete and it is worth stating plainly:** the rating-seat adjusted gap comes back
`0.00 [0.0, 0.0]` and the exact-opponent sensitivity comes back `opponents: 0`. **Those zeros are not a finding of no
effect — they are the reweighting having nothing to work with.** chatgpt_2 saw exactly this and **declined to report
it as a result**, writing "I therefore do not report a fake direct reweighting". That is the correct call and it is
the reason to trust the rest of the page. A less careful read would have published a zero here, or worse, published
the +152.80 extrapolation as if it were measured.

So: **+11.23 rating points** between the platform rating (29.66) and our champion-package mean (18.43) is the
defensible headline; the +156.57 shared-slice figure rests on **nine** native games; and the +152.80 fixed-effect
figure is an extrapolation its author explicitly labelled non-causal. **The advantage is real. Its transferable size
is unknown.**

## What this verification does not establish

Re-running an author's own code from its own artifacts proves the numbers are what the code produces, not that the
measurement design is right. The design's weak point is named above and named by its author. What would settle the
size is a population where both bots meet comparable opposition — which we do not have and cannot get while the
platform is frozen.
