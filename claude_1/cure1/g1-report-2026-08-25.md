## The one-paragraph verdict

The build is done and the parity half is green with room to spare: with the rule off the arm is
the champion **byte for byte on 34 fixtures and on all 240 panel games**, and the two-phase
hold-seeded fixed point demonstrably repairs the ordering hazard that sent G-0 back. With the rule
on, the dance signal collapses — **D-1 episodes 27 → 1**, regressive-detour turns **1,290 → 618**,
blocking games **43 → 41**. But three things are not green and I am not going to bury them under
that: a **new P3** violation the charter's own text forbids, a **ten-fold D-4 growth** that is the
hold rule's own turns, and a **poison arm the P4 gate does not catch** — it parks a troll for 194
consecutive turns and the gate stays quiet. My recommendation is that this does **not** proceed to
G-2 as built.

The rule is the construction ruling's, not the charter's superseded pseudo-code:
`resolve_move_conflicts_hold` runs `hold_pass` as PASS(K) with the K holders' own cells added to
the **initial** reserved set, unions in each pass's holders, and stops at the first pass that adds
none; only that final pass mutates `blocked_turns` or emits telemetry. The static `MoisanBot`
entry points are untouched. `blocked_turns` is a new `BTreeMap<i32,u8>` on `YamoBot`, and the
resolver is called from `YamoBot::commands` beside `remember_selected_regeneration`.

NARRATE v4 is v3's payload plus, per unit, `r=` (branch `P|L|H|R|W|N`) and `b=` (post-decision
counter), plus three per-turn fields the ruling required and no outside observer could derive:
`pz=` passes, `sp=` stale protections, `wc=` W-collisions. Longest payload observed: **142
characters** against the 2,000 budget.

**codex_1 #2b — the equal-distance detour — is NOT CONSTRUCTIBLE, and is reported as such rather
than passed.** On a 4-connected grid the BFS distances of adjacent cells differ by exactly one,
and a free orthogonal neighbour of a reachable cell is itself reachable, so the Manhattan fallback
can never apply to one side of the comparison only. `toward_goal[detour] == d_cur` cannot happen:
the predicate's `<=` is exactly `<`. The clause is dead code, which codex_1 may want to say
explicitly in the definition rather than leave as a control nobody can run.

**v4 decode controls, 38 of 38 fired** (`claude_1/narrate4/results/v4-decode-controls.json`):
every inherited v3 check re-run, plus the branch code, the `b` arithmetic, the `pz` bound, the
rule-off clauses, the three required per-turn fields, malformed/missing/duplicated `r` and `b`,
and **mutual refusal in both directions against the LIVE v3 decoder**.

**The poison arm is NOT caught. This is the most serious result in the report.**
`poison-hold-forever.rs` (`W = 255`, holds on every blocked step) was run over the same 240 games:

| | poison | candidate | base |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

and its telemetry variant shows **2,689 hold turns with a longest consecutive hold run of 194
turns** — a troll standing still for essentially an entire 200-turn game. The P4 liveness window
is 60. The gate did not fire, and the poison arm looks *better* than the candidate on every
headline number.

The mechanism is visible in the gate's own definition: `fuzz_panel.progress_turns` counts a turn
as progress if **the own inventory changed OR any own unit's cargo changed**. It is a game-level
predicate. One parked troll beside a working teammate produces progress every window, so P4 can
never see it. The charter's control was written as "must be caught by the P4 gate"; the gate
cannot catch it, which means **no green from the P4 clause licenses this candidate either** —
including the 16 → 15 in section 3.

The per-unit instrument that *can* see it is the one this task built. On the panel:

| --- | --- | --- |
| `H` share of unit-turns | 0.00 % | **1.67 %** |

| idle-with-work (`H`+`W`) | **0.73 %** | **2.28 %** |

The base's 0.73 % reproduces the 0.72 % baseline the G-2 kill rule cites, which is a useful

candidate is at 2.28 %. That is a forecast, not a G-2 result — G-2's metric is defined on real
games through the accepted adapter — but it is the number I would expect to kill the read, and it
should be known before an Arena action is spent.

## 6. What I recommend, and what is not mine to decide

Not mine: whether the charter's bar is 35 or 43; whether a P3 violation on one orchard-eligible
game is fatal; whether D-4's reading of a deliberate wait should stand; whether P4 should be
repaired to a per-unit clause. All four are the coordinator's and codex_1's.

Mine to say plainly: **I do not think this should go to G-2 as built.** The behaviour change it
was chartered to make is real and large — D-1 27 → 1, regressive detours halved — but the safety
net that was supposed to catch its failure mode does not work, the candidate's own idle share is
above the G-2 kill line on the panel, and one clause of the charter's panel gate is failed
outright. An Arena read spent now is likely to come back killed by rule (c), and we would have
paid for it to learn what the panel already says.
