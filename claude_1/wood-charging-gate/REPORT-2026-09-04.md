**The champion has no third troll** — it trains exactly one and never gathers for another — so the card's
"moment a troll would commit to a funding trip" does not exist in the base; the one variable is the gated
funding pathway as a whole (stated in the ack before the build). When the gate declines, the bot is the
champion byte for byte (the bed: differs from the champion on 1 of 34 frozen situations, the one whose
stock allows a third troll).

**The rule as built** (`gate.rs.in`). With two trolls, every turn while 100 turns remain, for each of the
27 chop-and-carry shapes whose bill the map can pay: the funding time from the doors (the starter's fruit
trips by the champion's own `collection_eta`; the trained troll's iron trips, a load a trip); **WITHOUT** =
4 × (fruit turns × the starter's realised rate + iron turns × the trained troll's realised rate);
**WITH** = 4 × the third troll's realised rate × the turns left after its arrival − the bill's fruit at
face value. A realised rate is the trip rate from the live board (the champion's own valuation of the
best tree from our doors, wood per turn of one round trip) × κ. The gate admits the best net shape whose
WITH is strictly greater than WITHOUT; nothing is remembered between turns; every input is measured from
the doors as a set (the stage-2A lesson against position-dependent flip-flops).

## 5. What the forecast under test got wrong, read off the outcome

The card said the forecast was the part most likely to fail, and it did, in a way the numbers locate:

1. **WITH was over-stated by roughly ten times.** The forecast promised 80–300 points of wood from the
   third troll; the realised whole-game wood of the arm is *below* the champion's on 15 maps of 22 with a
   troll, and the median map is within ±3 units either way. A trip rate × turns left assumes a forest that
   is always there to chop. It is not: on these maps the third troll arrives at turn 107 into a forest
   two trolls a side are already felling faster than it regrows, and what it chops is largely wood our
   own two trolls would have chopped. **v1's contested-forest reading was the right kind of estimate and
   the record's "108 units felled after arrival" made it look too small; the smoke says it was not.**
2. **WITHOUT was under-stated by two to three times.** The forecast charged 17–70 points of foregone wood;
   the realised cost by turn 50 alone was 149 units over 24 games, **25 points a game**, because the
   funding took a median 103 turns during which the starter banked almost nothing (0 wood by turn 50 on
   12 maps). The bill's fruit is fetched one item a trip by the only troll that can harvest, and the
   trees are shared: the ETA is fair on paper, but the wood the trolls *would* have banked in those turns
   is the champion's realised 0.17 a turn for the pair, not the 0.03 + 0.14 split assumed for turns the
   starter spends walking.
3. **The gate re-decides every turn from the board, and the board only got worse for the troll as the
   funding went on** (the forest thinning, turns left falling), yet it flipped to decline in only 2 games:
   the WITH margin was so large that no realistic thinning closed it. An honest gate with a fair WITH would
   have abandoned most of these purchases mid-way — and by then the wood was already gone.

The one direction this leaves open, for the coordinator and the owner and not for me to build: a forecast
whose WITH is the troll's **marginal** wood — the record's own measurement of what a third troll adds to
the pair's whole-game wood, which the smoke says is about zero on average and negative once the fruit
and the funding are charged — would decline nearly always, and then the candidate is the champion.
That is v1, and it is the honest reading of this slice.
