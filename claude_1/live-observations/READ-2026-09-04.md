## How each observation was measured, and two things found on the way

**Neither bot's record carries a target.** The champion's MOVE is rewritten by its resolver into a
single step, and on exactly those turns the v6 diagnostics line's chosen target reads NONE; its
"want" field is the argmax candidate and ignores teammates' claims (it names a tree a teammate is
already chopping while the troll walks the other way). So switching is measured **without reading
intent**: each troll's timeline is cut into *trips*, from the turn after its last action (or its
spawn) to the turn it next acts (HARVEST/CHOP/DROP/PICK/PLANT/MINE) at a cell D. The shortest trip is
ceil(bfs(start, D)/speed) turns; the **excess** is the turns spent beyond it; a **step away** is a
move that increases the distance to D (the troll walked away from where it ended up going: a change
of mind, or a dance); the two-cell dance (on the same cell as two turns ago, having moved) is counted
beside it. A trip the game ends before it finishes is counted apart. The split is at our third
troll's TRAIN (the dispatcher's window in the new bot) and, beside it, at the fixed turn 70.

**Provenance is exact in the replay, not inferred.** The record carries the opponent's command
stream, and a PLANT names the planter. The adjacency inference the bot could run live (a new tree
with an opponent troll on or orthogonally beside the cell the turn before) is then scored against
that truth, which gives the error rate the card asks for.

**Trees left standing** are read from the final state with kind, size, health and door-distance;
then `chop_candidates`' own arithmetic (predict_tree, chop_outcome, the carry-home test, the
hands-full test) is replayed for every troll of ours at every turn from 200, so each end-standing
tree has the last turn on which it was a bankable candidate for one of our trolls, what that troll
did on that turn instead, and, on the turn the candidate list empties for good, the cause per
(troll, tree). The value of an unbanked cut is what the opponent took from that tree after the
earliest turn one of ours could have felled it ignoring the walk home.

**A correction to the 15:47Z ruling's troll timeline (the referee tooltip's "turn" is a frame).**
The ruling read the dispatcher's third troll at median turn 147 from the tooltips' `turn` field and
called the bench's 70.5 "a bench artefact". Checked game by game against the reconstructed state

**Cost in points.** At each bot's own points per troll-turn, the excess turns are worth, as a
ceiling (a saved turn is worth less than an average turn): control A **11.5**, control B **12.3**,

**Reading.** The champion's own play carries the thrash the owner described at a steady 6 % — one
trip in ten has a step away — in both controls, and Track D's four dancing-troll candidates
(2026-08-25/26) all died trying to cure it, which is the standing evidence on how recoverable it is.
The dispatcher build doubles it, and the doubling is *not* confined to the dispatcher's window:

- **Inside the window** (turns 1 to the third troll's TRAIN, median 75) the dispatcher's own
  assignment thrashes at 14.6 % against the control's 8 % in the same turns; only 51 of its 1,128
  steps away coincide with the telemetry's argmax flipping (the dispatcher does not steer by that
  field), so these are its own re-pointings. Doubled, but 11 turns a game: under the resolution.
- **After the window** the champion's own joint assignment, now with three trolls, thrashes at
  13.4 % against 6 % — and the character changes: idle turns en route go from 7 a game to 33, the
  dance from 2 per 100 to 4.7, unfinished trips at the end from 19 turns to 44. Three trolls on the
  champion's claim-and-resolve loop collide, re-point each other and wait; the share of steps away
  that coincide with an argmax flip (32 %) is the champion's own ratio (38 %), so it is the same
  mechanism at a higher rate, not a new one.
- **The third troll's marginal turn is worth less**: 0.290 points per troll-turn against 0.366, and
  the dispatcher's 21 extra points a game (209 against 188) came against a weaker field (its
  opponents scored 198 to control B's opponents' 210); the rating, 4.13 lower, is the measure.

*Verdict on rule vs consequence:* **consequence.** The switching is the price of a third troll on
a two-troll assignment loop, paid mostly after the dispatcher hands over. It is not a switching
rule to fix in the dispatcher (3 points of ceiling in its window) and it is not a new defect in
the champion (its own rate is unchanged). If a three-troll build ever returns, the champion's
assignment loop is where the 15-point ceiling sits — that is a size, not a proposal; the card is
a read and the dispatcher is dead.
