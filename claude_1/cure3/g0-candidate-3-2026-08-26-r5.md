## 0. What r5 is, and the three findings it adds

Two rulings closed the two questions r4 left open, and they close them in the direction that costs
the rule the least machinery: a contest **releases** the younger goal (there is no yielding, no
parked-and-goalless troll), and a `Tree` goal is **done when the carry fills at the tree** (so the
walk-back is cut at its source, not bounded by a margin). r4's R4(c) fallbacks, its `xy=`/yield-run
counts, and its "preserve a type-mismatched tree" are all withdrawn. §1 is the whole rule under the
rulings; §4 is the selector; §8 is the loop proof re-argued on the ruled reading.

r5 also reports **three findings against the base that were not visible when either ruling was
written**. All three are about predicates the rulings name; none of them changes a ruling's intent,
and each is implemented here in the way that keeps the intent:

- **F1 — a `Tree(c)` goal is not always a chop goal.** `Target::Tree(c)` is also carried by
  **`HARVEST`** candidates (`:709` idle-harvest, `:2056` endgame harvest). The ruling's `done`
  predicate says "last emitted command was `CHOP` at `c`", which never fires for a harvest-motivated
  tree goal, so the walk-back the capacity middle was chosen to cut stays open for that whole class.
  r5 reads `done` as **`CHOP` or `HARVEST` at `c` with the carry now full** — the ruling's own
  words for why ("the goal has yielded everything this troll can take"). **Named as a deviation
  from the ruling's letter; §3.2.**
- **F2 — the `type` release cause has no referent in the chop path.** `MoisanBot::chop_candidates`
  (`:836-904`) filters on `health <= 0` and reachability and prediction only; there is **no kind
  filter**. `type_to_cut` is a **score bonus** (`+900/(1+d)`, `:888`) and it is set once and never
  changed (`:1145-1146`, `if self.type_to_cut.is_none()`). So a tree cannot "stop matching the type
  the planner cuts": nothing matches or fails to match for admissibility, and the value cannot
  change after turn 1. This also **falsifies my own r4-block-response §1.2**, which adopted the
  cause on the argument that a type-mismatched goal is *permanently* not-live. It is not: the tree
  is still a candidate. §3.3 implements the cause where it does have a referent and reports the
  general case as a count instead of promoting it to `gone` — with the reason. **This is the one
  place r5 does not do what a ruling says, because the ruling's predicate does not exist.**
- **F3 — `Bank(c)` has neither an "accepts" predicate nor a reachable `gone`.** The ruling asked me
  to name the champion's predicate for "accepts" and to say so if there is none. **There is none:**
  `DROP` is emitted unconditionally when the unit stands on a door cell (`:596-611`) and no
  capacity, item or fullness test exists anywhere on that path. Per the ruling's own conditional,
  `Bank` **gone is the walkable test only** — and that test is **structurally unreachable**, because
  `view.walkable` is built once from the static map (`:329-355`) and bank cells are
  `ortho_neighbors(shacks[0]) ∩ walkable` by construction (`:592-594`). A `Bank` goal therefore ends
  only by **done** (a `DROP`) or **dead**. `rb=` is **not** in v6; an always-zero counter is an
  inert check and this programme has paid for those.

## 1. The rule, exact text (replaces R4 of r4 §1 in full)

> **R5.** Each own troll remembers at most one goal: `kept_goals: BTreeMap<i32, Target>` plus its
> birth turn `kept_since: BTreeMap<i32, i32>`, persisted on the bot exactly as
> `regeneration_commitments` (`:535`) already is. `Target` is `Copy + Eq + Ord` (`:457-463`).
>
> **(a) Release.** Immediately after `reconcile_regeneration_commitments(view)` (`:2069`) and
> before any candidate is built, every kept goal is tested by the four world predicates of §3 in
> the fixed order **dead, gone, impossible, done**; the first to fire erases the entry. A goal that
> survives is **valid**. A fifth cause, **contested** (§4), fires later in the turn, inside the
> selector.
>
> **(b) Restrict.** After `force_unique_door_clear` (`:2172`) and before `MoisanBot::select`
> (`:2174`): for each unit `u` holding a valid kept goal `g`, let `L = by_id[u]` and
> `L|g = [c in L : c.target == g]`, **order preserved**. If `L|g` is non-empty the goal is **live**
> and `by_id[u] := L|g`. If `L|g` is empty the goal is **not live** this turn: `by_id[u]` is left
> untouched and the goal is **not** erased. No candidate is added, no score is changed, no resolver
> clause is touched, no lock and no timer.
>
> **(c) Decide.** §4. If no unit is live-restricted this turn (`kq == 0`), `MoisanBot::select` runs
> **unmodified** and steps (c.2)–(c.4) do not execute. Otherwise the champion's `select` runs on the
> restricted lists, and a **contest** — a restricted decision that would hand `wait()` to a unit
> whose unrestricted list `L` held a non-`WAIT` candidate, or `best_pair == None` on the two-unit
> path — is resolved by **releasing the younger contested goal** (`xc=`) and re-deciding, never by
> overruling a valid goal with a score.
>
> **(d) Record.** After `MoisanBot::resolve_move_conflicts` (`:2175`) and beside
> `remember_selected_regeneration` (`:2176`): **only units that entered the selector without a
> valid kept goal** — including a unit whose goal was released as contested this turn — take one,
> by the post-resolver exact-match rule of §6. `kept_since` is set when an entry is created and
> never touched afterwards.

**No margin, no `M`, no constant of any kind appears in R5.** The only numerals in the rule are the
zero in "non-empty" and the `<= 0` of the capacity test.

### 1.1 What the structural guarantee is now

r4 §1.1 said honestly that a restriction can park a troll where a bonus could not, and that R4(c)
was the replacement for the guarantee the charter correction removed. **Under Ruling C the
guarantee comes back in a different and stronger form:**

> A troll is never both goal-restricted and unable to act. If the restriction is what makes the
> turn undecidable, the restriction is **removed** — the goal is released, the troll is
> unrestricted, and it is decided over its full list exactly as the champion would.

The residual is not parking but a `wait()` handed to an *unrestricted* partner that has nothing
compatible with a kept troll's target (§4, `xw=`). That is a real cost, it is counted, and it is
bounded by one turn: the partner holds no goal, so nothing carries it into the next turn.

What still survives from r4: **restriction never invents a goal.** `L|g ⊆ L`, so every command the
rule can cause is a command the champion offered this turn.

## 2. Validity, liveness, and a correction to r4 §2

- **Valid** — no release predicate has fired. A property of the *world*.
- **Live** — at least one candidate in `by_id[u]` this turn carries the goal. A property of *what
  the planner happened to offer*. Not stored; it is exactly `L|g` non-empty.

A valid-but-not-live goal is **preserved and unrestricting** (`ERASE_WHEN_NOT_LIVE = false`,
accepted by Ruling 2).

**Correction to r4 §2.** r4 justified preservation with "a teammate standing on a tree removes it
from `chop_candidates`, which is precisely the exchange turn". **That is false.**
`MoisanBot::chop_candidates` (`:836-904`) has no unit-occupancy filter at all — `view.walkable` is
static and a teammate standing on a tree does not remove it. The exchange turn is **not** a
not-live turn, which *strengthens* §8 rather than weakening it (both trees stay offered) but
removes r4's stated reason for the preservation rule. The real not-live cases, re-read from the
base, are these four, and they are what preservation is actually for:

1. **Producer switch.** The per-unit candidate producer is chosen fresh every turn (`:2098-2137`):
   `endgame_candidates` for a regeneration-committed unit or in endgame, `main_candidates`
   otherwise, `early_candidates` in the opening. `endgame_candidates` returns **bank candidates
   only** for any unit with `total_carried() > 0` (`:1936-1939`); `main_candidates` does the same
   for a unit carrying a fruit under `safe_regeneration` (`:1779-1782`). A `Tree` goal held by such
   a unit is not live for as long as it carries.
2. **Full carry** — `free_capacity() <= 0` routes to `bank_candidates` and returns (`:1802-1805`),
   and `chop_candidates` itself returns empty (`:842-844`). **Under Ruling 2 this case is now a
   `done` release, not a not-live turn** (§3.2), which is exactly how the capacity middle cuts the
   walk-back.
3. **Door clearance.** `force_unique_door_clear` (`:1480-1518`) *replaces* a unit's whole list with
   a single forced candidate — `DROP` at the door, a forced `MOVE`, or a bare `wait()` for the
   transit unit. R5(b) runs after it (`:2172` → `:2174`), so a door-cleared unit is simply not live
   and the door clearance is never outvoted by a kept goal.
4. **Endgame admissibility.** `chop_candidates` drops a live tree when `predict_tree` returns
   `None`, when the predicted size or health is `<= 0` (a chopped tree regrowing), or when
   `travel + chop + return + 1 > TOTAL_TURNS - turn + 1` (`:859-882`). Near the end of a game every
   tree goal goes not-live and stays that way.

**The residual walk-back, named.** Case 1 is not closed by the capacity middle: a unit carrying
*some* wood in endgame, or a fruit under `safe_regeneration`, banks with its `Tree(c)` goal
preserved and walks back to `c` afterwards even if a better tree stands by the shack. Ruling 2
closes the full-carry path, which is the common one; this one stays open and is **measured, not
argued** — `nl=` (§5.2) and `ka=`, with §9.10's risk gate over both.

## 3. The release predicates, each with its observable

Evaluated at the top of `commands()` against the current `view` and a one-turn snapshot
`last_command: BTreeMap<i32,String>`, `last_cell: BTreeMap<i32,Cell>`, `last_carried:
BTreeMap<i32,i32>`, `last_inventory: [i32;6]`. Order fixed: **dead, gone, impossible, done**; first
firing wins, so the census sums exactly and no turn is double-counted.

**Why `last_cell` is new in r5.** The ruling's `done` says "`CHOP` **at `c`**", but the champion's
chop command is `format!("CHOP {}", unit.id)` (`:893`) — **it carries no cell**, and so does
`HARVEST` (`:707`, `:2051`). "At `c`" is only recoverable as *the cell the unit occupied when it
emitted the command*, so the snapshot must hold it. The unit's current cell would usually do (a
`CHOP` does not move the unit), but "usually" is not a predicate and `resolve_move_conflicts` is a
rewrite step; the snapshot is taken from the same emitted line the recording uses, so the two can
never disagree.

### 3.1 Dead — `rx=`
`unit.id` absent from `view.units.iter().filter(|u| u.player == 0)`. Erase. Ids are not reused, so
no successor inherits the entry.

### 3.2 Done — `rd=` — **the capacity middle**

| goal | done iff |
|---|---|
| `Tree(c)` | `last_command[u]` was `CHOP <u>` **or** `HARVEST <u>`, **and** `last_cell[u] == c`, **and** the unit's carry is now full: `unit.free_capacity() <= 0` |
| `Bank(c)` | `last_command[u]` was `DROP <u>` and `last_cell[u] == c` (any remaining carry — the troll re-picks, possibly the same bank; there is no loop in banking) |
| `Cell(c)` (plant site, incl. the regeneration `PICK`, whose target is `Target::Cell(unit.cell)`, `:1798`/`:1967`) | `view.plant_at(c).is_some()` **and** `last_command[u]` was `PLANT <u> <kind>` |
| `Shack` | `unit.total_carried() == 0` |

The `Tree` row is Ruling 2 with **F1's extension to `HARVEST`**. The ruling's stated reason is "the
goal has yielded everything this troll can take"; a harvest fills the same `carry` array
(`total_carried` sums all six items, `:102-104`), so a harvest-motivated `Tree(c)` goal reaches the
same state by the same route, and reading the letter (`CHOP` only) would leave the walk-back open
for every fruit goal in the endgame — the exact cost the capacity middle was chosen to cut.
**This is a deviation from the ruling's letter and codex_1 should rule on it; the switch is
`DONE_ON_HARVEST`, default `true` as written here.**

Two consequences worth stating because they are the point of the ruling:

- **The first chop does not release.** The charter's literal "chopped there" is corrected; a single
  swing is progress toward the goal, and releasing on it is the champion's re-pick, which is the
  loop's mechanism (r4 §3.2, upheld).
- **The full-carry troll's goal is done by construction**, so it banks with **no** goal and re-picks
  freely afterwards. It cannot walk back past a better tree. `kc=` still counts the turns the rule
  holds a troll on a tree it is already chopping — where it is inert, since the champion scores that
  same `CHOP` at `10_000.0` (`:1947`) or at the top of `chop_candidates`.

If a `progress_event` fires for a unit **away from** its goal, nothing is released.

### 3.3 Gone — `rg=`, with sub-counts, and finding F2

| goal | gone iff | count |
|---|---|---|
| `Tree(c)` | `view.plant_at(c)` is `None`, **or** that plant's `health <= 0` (`:114`, and as `reconcile_regeneration_commitments` reads it, `:1694-1698`) | `rf=` |
| `Tree(c)`, harvest form | the unit's producer this turn is `idle_harvest_candidates` and the plant at `c` no longer matches the kind that producer seeks (`:714`, `plant.kind != kind`) | `rt=` |
| `Bank(c)` | `c` not in `view.walkable` — **and nothing else; see F3.** Structurally unreachable on this base, reported as such rather than as a counter | `rw=` |
| `Cell(c)` | `c` not in `view.walkable`; **or** `view.plant_at(c).is_some()` while `last_command[u]` was not `PLANT` (someone else took the square); **or** the unit no longer carries the fruit and holds no `regeneration_commitments` entry (`:1687`) | `ro=` |
| `Shack` | never | — |

`rf + rt + rw + ro == rg` exactly.

**F2, in full.** Ruling 2's `Tree` gone clause reads "the plant no longer matches the type the
planner cuts this turn (it no longer admits the action)". On this base that clause has **no referent
in the chop path**: `chop_candidates` never tests `plant.kind`, `type_to_cut` only adds
`900.0/(1+opponent_distance)` when it matches (`:886-890`), and `self.type_to_cut` is assigned once
and never reassigned (`:1145-1146`). The only kind filter over `Target::Tree` candidates anywhere in
the file is the idle-harvest producer's (`:714`), and that is what `rt=` counts.

**What I do with the rest of the clause's intent, and why not `gone`.** The general form of "it no
longer admits the action" is "no candidate in `L` carries `Target::Tree(c)` this turn" — which is
**exactly the definition of not-live**. Promoting it to `gone` would erase kept goals on every
producer-switch and door-clearance turn (§2, cases 1 and 3), i.e. precisely the turns Ruling 2
preserved goals for; it would also make `ERASE_WHEN_NOT_LIVE = false` and the gone clause
contradict each other. So r5 **reports** it instead of releasing on it: `nl=` (§5.2), split by
cause, is the observable that would show the clause has real referents. If `nl=` is dominated by
some cause the rule should release on, that is a **finding for the packet** under §9.10 — a ruling
for G-1, not a knob.

### 3.4 Impossible — `ri=`

No path on the walkable map **with the teammate's cell treated as free**. The champion's own BFS has
that property: `bfs_distances(&view.walkable, &[unit.cell])` (`:230`, `:845`) walks `view.walkable`,
a static map-derived set (`:329-355`) that never removes occupied cells:

```
let d = bfs_distances(&view.walkable, &[unit.cell]);
impossible = !d.contains_key(&goal_cell)   // goal_cell: c for Tree/Bank/Cell, shacks[0] for Shack
```

No new BFS on any turn the unit already has candidates built. A standing teammate is never an
impossibility — it is the swap rule's business, exactly as the charter says.

### 3.5 Contested — `xc=`, the fifth cause

Ruling 1 = C. Defined operationally in §4.3, counted in the census as a release
(`rx + rg + ri + rd + xc == kr`), and pre-committed in §9: **`xc > 0` on any recorded exchange turn
of the six loop games is a BLOCK on the arm.**

### 3.6 Release latency — the charter's obligation, discharged

The bot sees the world only between engine turns; an event during the engine's resolution of turn
`t` first appears in the `view` at `t+1`. R5(a) runs **before any candidate is built** at `t+1`, and
all four world predicates read that same `view` plus the one-turn snapshot. Therefore **a kept goal
restricts zero turns after its invalidating event is observable**, and the event-to-release latency
is exactly one engine turn — the minimum this interface admits. The contested cause has **zero**
latency: it is decided inside the turn it arises.

The residual is not latency but **blindness**: `done` reads `last_command`, so if
`resolve_move_conflicts` rewrote the command, the snapshot must reflect the rewrite. It does — the
snapshot and the recording both read the **final emitted line** (§6), so they cannot disagree.

Observable for the obligation: **`ka=`** — the maximum `view.turn - kept_since[u]` over valid kept
goals this turn. §9.10 pre-commits what a bad `ka` means.

## 4. The selector under Ruling C, path by path

`MoisanBot::select` (`:937`) has three paths. **`ids` is `candidates_by_id.keys()`, so the
champion's order is ascending unit id**, and the returned commands are positional. R5 changes the
*decision* order in one case only and **always emits in ascending-id order**, so stdout parity is a
property of the arms and not of the rule.

**Gate (R5(c) first sentence).** If `kq == 0` — no unit is live-restricted — `select` runs
unmodified and nothing below executes. This is what makes the **rule-off arm byte-identical in play
to its base** a theorem rather than a hope (§9.1).

### 4.1 One unit (`:944-951`)
`max_by(total_cmp)` over `L|g`. The kept goal wins by construction; `max_by` returns the **last**
maximal element, so ties are decided by list order exactly as in the champion. `L|g` non-empty ⇒ no
`WAIT`. **No contest is possible on this path.**

### 4.2 Two units (`:952-972`) — the path the dance runs on
Full `|A| × |B|` joint maximisation over `compatible` (`:912`) and `stock_compatible` (`:931`),
keeping the first strictly-greater sum.

- **(a) Both live-restricted.** The product is `L|g_A × L|g_B`. Every surviving pair assigns both
  trolls their own goals; the sum only chooses between commands carrying the same two targets.
  If `best_pair` is `Some`, done. If `best_pair == None`, **contest** → §4.3.
- **(b) Exactly one live-restricted** (call it `K`, its partner `P`). The product is
  `L|g_K × L_P`: the charter's sentence is literally what the code does — the restricted troll
  contributes exactly its candidate and the joint maximisation chooses `P`'s goal around it. If
  `best_pair == None`, there is no second goal to release, so the **phased** decision runs:
  `K` takes `max_by(total_cmp)` over `L|g_K`; `P` takes the first candidate of `L_P`, sorted
  descending by score as the champion's greedy sorts (`:979`), that is `compatible` with `K`'s
  target and passes `used_stock`; if none is, `P` gets `MoisanBot::wait()` and **`xw += 1`**.
  **The kept troll is never the one that waits.** Counted `xp += 1`.
- **(c) Neither restricted.** Unmodified champion.

### 4.3 Contested release — the resolution, for every unit count

> **R5(c.3).** A **contest** is: `best_pair == None` on the two-unit path with **both** units
> live-restricted (§4.2a); or, on the greedy path, a live-restricted unit whose every restricted
> candidate collides with an earlier phase-1 assignment (§4.4). On a contest, the **younger** of the
> contesting kept goals — larger `kept_since`, ties by larger unit id — is **released**: erased,
> counted `xc += 1`, its unit unrestricted for the rest of the turn. The restriction is then rebuilt
> and the decision re-run from the top of §4.
>
> **At most one release per troll per turn**, so the procedure terminates in at most `|units|`
> iterations and is a **pure function of the turn's state**. The elder is never touched. No
> challenger ever overrules a *valid* goal, because the released goal is not valid when the
> challenger is scored.

The released troll is goalless when §6 runs, so R5(d) lets it record a **fresh** goal from what it
actually did this turn. This is the whole reason C beats r4's D and the coordinator's first D: under
D the yielding troll acts unrestricted with **no goal recorded** and, when the elder finishes, snaps
back to a goal chosen before the conflict — the rule silently off while `ka=` reads healthy.

### 4.4 Three or more units (`:973-995`)
The champion sorts each unit's candidates descending and greedily assigns in ascending-id order
against `used_targets` / `used_stock`.

> **R5(c.4), two phases.** **Phase 1:** live-restricted units in ascending `(kept_since, id)`, each
> taking the highest-scoring candidate of its restricted list that is `compatible` with the
> accumulated `used_targets` and passes `used_stock`; if none is, that unit's goal is **released**
> (`xc += 1`, `xg += 1`) and it drops to phase 2. **Phase 2:** every other unit, in the champion's
> ascending-id order, over its **full** list, greedy against the accumulated sets exactly as the
> champion does, `wait()` when nothing fits — counted `xw += 1` **only** when that unit's full list
> held a non-`WAIT` candidate, so champion-native waits and the door-clearance `wait()` (`:1508`)
> are never counted as the rule's cost. **Phase 2 cannot un-assign phase 1.**

With no live-restricted unit, phase 1 is empty and phase 2 **is** the champion's loop, unchanged —
the gate of §4 makes this vacuous, and it is stated because the containment proof uses it.

**Frequency of the `>= 3` path is itself pre-registered as a count** (§9.8): the champion trains one
second troll, so I expect this path to be rare-to-absent on the 240-game corpus, and if it is absent
the phase-2 rule is **untested code** — a fact the panel must state, not hide.

### 4.5 Where the rule sits, restated
**After** `force_unique_door_clear` (`:2172`), so door clearance cannot be outvoted and its
wholesale list replacement is not deleted by a restriction that preceded it. **Before** `select`
(`:2174`), because the preference is only correct inside the maximisation.

## 5. Telemetry v6

v6 is **its own decoder** with **mutual refusal against v4 and v5** (all three directions asserted
as a control), per the charter and my card.

### 5.1 Grammar
```python
VERSION  = "v6"
UNIT_RE  = re.compile(r"^u(\d+)=([^/]+)/([^/]+)/r=([^/]*)/b=([^/]*)/k=([012])$")
META_RE  = re.compile(r"^(pz|sp|wc|sw|so|sn|sf|kp|kq|kl|kr|rd|rg|ri|rx|rf|rt|ro|nl|ka|kc|xc|xw|xn|xp|xg|xd|xj)=(\d+)$")
```
No `?`, no optional group: a v6 unit token without `/k=` is a decode error exactly as a v5 token
without `/b=` is. Every per-turn field is REQUIRED. **`m=` is deleted** — there is no margin
constant to disambiguate a wire with, which is itself the version's signature. **`xy=` and the
longest-yield-run of the superseded Ruling 1 are not in the grammar** (there is no yielding under
C), and **`rb=` is not in the grammar** (F3: it could only ever read zero).

`k=` is **three-valued**: `k=2` the unit was restricted and its emitted command carried the kept
goal; `k=1` the unit held a **valid but not-live** goal (unrestricted this turn, goal preserved);
`k=0` the unit held no valid kept goal at the end of the turn — including a unit whose goal was
released as contested, which is why `xc` and `k` are read together and never separately.

### 5.2 Census
`kp=` units entering the turn with a valid kept goal · `kq=` of those, live-restricted · `kl=` of
those, not live · `kr=` goals released this turn with `rd= rg= ri= rx=` the world reasons and `xc=`
the contested ones (`rd+rg+ri+rx+xc == kr`) · `rf= rt= rw= ro=` the `gone` sub-counts
(`rf+rt+rw+ro == rg`) · `nl=` not-live unit-turns split by the four causes of §2 as
`nl_producer`/`nl_door`/`nl_admissibility`/`nl_other` (the full-carry cause is absent by
construction under §3.2, and a non-zero `nl_other` is a finding) · `ka=` max kept-goal age ·
`kc=` `Tree`-goal units whose emitted command was `CHOP` · `xw=` rule-caused waits · `xp=`
two-unit phased turns · `xg=` phase-1 collisions on the `>=3` path · `xn=` contested turns
(turns on which at least one `xc` fired). `pz=1`, `sp=0`, `b=0` remain invariants — R5 adds no
holder — and a violation is a defect in R5, not a curiosity.

### 5.3 `xd=` and `xj=` — what the rule gave up, reported and never tested
r3's `rho` stops being a threshold and becomes the price tag. Flooring, positivity and saturation
rules are r2 §3.2's, verbatim.

**`xd=` — per-turn maximum single-unit give-up, basis points**, over units live-restricted this turn
whose best **pre-restriction** score `S_free` over `L` exceeds their best kept-goal score `S_kept`
over `L|g`, with `S_kept > 0`:
```
xd = max over those units of  floor(10000 * (S_free - S_kept) / S_kept)
```
floor toward zero, clamp at `999999` and emit exactly that (a saturated value is decode-visible and
read as "at least 100×", never as a measurement); `xd=0` when no unit qualifies; units with
`S_kept <= 0` are excluded and counted in `kq=` only.

**`xj=` — the joint give-up on the two-unit path**, emitted when both units were live-restricted:
```
xj = floor(10000 * (S_B - S_A) / S_A)
```
`S_A` = the chosen restricted pair's score sum, `S_B` = the best **unrestricted** pair's sum for the
same turn. `xj=0` when fewer than two units were restricted or `S_A <= 0`; if the unrestricted
maximisation has no pair either, `xj=0` and the turn is in `xn=`.

`xd` is the per-troll diagnostic; **`xj` is the successor to r3's `rho`** and is the number the owner
should read to know what absolute keep costs. Both are emitted because reading only `xd` on a
two-unit turn attributes a joint decision to one troll.

**Neither is ever tested.** No threshold on `xd` or `xj` appears anywhere in R5.

## 6. Recording, carried verbatim from r4 §6 with the contested case named

> Recording happens **after** `MoisanBot::resolve_move_conflicts` (`:2175`), against the **final
> emitted command line**, never against `select`'s output. For each own unit **that entered the
> selector without a valid kept goal — including a unit whose goal was released as contested this
> turn** — the emitted command carrying that unit's id in field 1 is matched by **exact string
> equality** against that unit's **pre-restriction** candidate list `L`:
>
> - **exactly one match** — its `target` becomes the kept goal, unless the target is `Target::None`,
>   in which case no entry is created;
> - **two or more matches with the same `target`** — that target becomes the kept goal;
> - **two or more matches with differing targets** — **no entry**. Position is never a tie-break;
> - **zero matches** — **no entry** (`resolve_move_conflicts` can replace a selected `MOVE` with
>   something no candidate proposed);
> - **no emitted command carrying that unit's id** (dead, or a bare `WAIT`) — **no entry**.
>
> Ids are parsed exactly as `remember_selected_regeneration` (`:1702-1715`) parses them, so the two
> recording steps cannot disagree about which command belongs to which unit. The same pass fills
> `last_command` and `last_cell` for the next turn's release tests.

A unit that kept a valid goal through the selector is never re-recorded: **release is the only way a
goal ends**, and recording only ever fills an empty slot. Erasure-on-ambiguity stays the safe
direction — a missing kept goal costs one turn of preference; a wrong one is a preference for
something the troll is not doing.

## 7. Plan-keeping needs no new machinery — accepted as a prediction

predicate, because of a fact about the champion rather than a design of mine:

## 8. The loop proof, under contested release and the capacity middle

Source is a recording, not a rerun: `claude_1/cure2/loop-anatomy-2026-08-25.md` §2 and

At the first post-exchange turn `t+1` of every exchange with a goal trade:

1. The mover's kept goal is its **own** tree `T2`, recorded at `t` (R5(d) could not overwrite it at
   `t`: it held a valid goal). It is **valid**: `T2`'s plant exists with `health > 0` (not gone, and
   `rt` cannot fire — the mover's producer is `main_candidates`, not idle-harvest); `T2` is in
   `bfs_distances(&view.walkable, &[mover.cell])`, since the mover walked from beside it (not
   impossible); the mover **chopped nothing at `T2`** — its last command was the exchange `MOVE` —
   so `done` cannot fire under the capacity middle **whatever its carry is**, because the first
   conjunct fails; it is alive.
2. It is **live**: `chop_candidates` offers every reachable tree with `health > 0` and no occupancy
   filter (the r4 §2 claim to the contrary is corrected in §2 above), and `T2` is reachable, so
   `L|T2` is non-empty.
3. The partner's kept goal `T1` — the tree the mover now stands on — is valid and live by the same
   three tests.
4. `compatible(Tree(T1), Tree(T2))` is `T1 != T2` — true. Neither command is a `PICK`, so
   `stock_compatible` is true (`:931-936`). **`best_pair` is exactly the keeping pair**, and no score

   contest arises and `xc = 0`.**
5. Therefore the mover's `target` at `t+1` is its own tree, not the cell it stands on. Candidate 2's
   exchange clause 6 (`target != landing`) fails for the reverse direction, and **no second exchange
   can fire**.

The proof is three equality tests and a subset; it depends on no inequality, which is the point of
the charter correction. It is now also **independent of the carry state**, which the capacity middle
