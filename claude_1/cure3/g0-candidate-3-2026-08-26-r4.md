## 0. What changed, and the one thing that changed with it

`M` is gone, so the rule can no longer be a score multiplier. A multiplier with no margin is not a
multiplier: "the kept goal always wins for that troll" is a **restriction of that troll's candidate
list**, which is exactly the shape my r1 §4 argued against on the two-unit joint path. That
objection was correct and it does not go away because the charter changed — so this packet's real
content is **the mechanics that make a restriction safe**, not the rule sentence, which is now one
line long.

Three consequences follow and each is worked out below rather than asserted:

1. **A restriction can leave the joint selector with no pair at all** (`compatible` /
   `stock_compatible` can reject every restricted cross product). The champion then falls through
   to the greedy path and can emit `WAIT` — **the parking failure mode, now reachable**, where the
   bonus form made it structurally impossible (r1 §1.1). §4 defines the fallback that closes it.
2. **"Done" cannot mean "chopped there"** without destroying the loop proof one turn after the
   exchange. §3.2 is a finding against the charter's own wording, with both readings written out
   and a ruling asked for.
3. **The margin did not disappear from the instrument, only from the decision.** `rho` is still
   the number that says what the rule cost; v6 emits it as `xd=`/`xj=` and **never tests it**. §5.

## 1. The rule, exact text (replaces R3 of r1 §1 and r2 §1)

> **R4.** Each own troll remembers at most one goal: `kept_goals: BTreeMap<i32, Target>` plus its
> birth turn `kept_since: BTreeMap<i32, i32>`, persisted on the bot exactly as
> `regeneration_commitments` (`:535`) already is. `Target` is `Copy + Eq + Ord` (`:457`).
>
> **(a) Release.** Immediately after `reconcile_regeneration_commitments(view)` (`:2069`) and
> before any candidate is built, every kept goal is tested by the four release predicates of §3.
> Any one firing erases the entry. A goal that survives is **valid**.
>
> **(b) Restrict.** After `force_unique_door_clear` (`:2172`) and before `MoisanBot::select`
> (`:2174`): for each unit `u` holding a valid kept goal `g`, let `L` be `by_id[u]` and
> `L|g = [c in L : c.target == g]`, **order preserved**. If `L|g` is non-empty, `by_id[u] := L|g`.
> If `L|g` is empty the goal is **not live** this turn: `by_id[u]` is left untouched and the goal
> is **not** erased. No candidate is added, no score is changed, no resolver clause is touched,
> no lock and no timer.
>
> **(c) Fallback.** If the restriction makes `select` infeasible, the turn is decided by the
> unrestricted lists — §4, which is the only place a challenger ever wins against a valid kept
> goal, and it is an infeasibility, not an overrule.
>
> **(d) Record.** After `MoisanBot::resolve_move_conflicts` (`:2175`) and beside
> `remember_selected_regeneration` (`:2176`): **only units that entered the turn without a valid
> kept goal** take one, by the post-resolver exact-match rule of r2 item 2, carried verbatim below
> (§6). A unit that entered the turn with a valid kept goal keeps it whatever it did, including
> on a not-live turn. `kept_since` is set when an entry is created and never touched afterwards.

**No margin, no `M`, no constant of any kind appears in R4.** The only numeral in the rule is the
zero in "non-empty".

### 1.1 The structural guarantee is *weaker* than it was, and this is the honest headline

r1 §1.1 could say "a kept goal cannot park a troll" because a bonus only re-weights candidates the
unmodified planner already produced. **A restriction cannot say that.** It can shrink a unit's list
to one candidate that the joint selector then cannot pair, and the champion's own fall-through
hands that unit `wait()` (`:989`). §4 is therefore not a tidy-up: it is the replacement for the
guarantee the charter's correction removed, and if §4 is wrong the rule is wrong.

What *does* survive: **restriction never invents a goal.** `L|g` is a subset of `L`, so every
command the rule can cause is a command the champion offered this turn. The rule can misallocate a
troll; it cannot make one do something the champion would not have done.

## 2. Validity and liveness are different, and the difference is load-bearing

- **Valid** — no release predicate of §3 fires. Validity is a property of the *world*.
- **Live** — at least one candidate in `by_id[u]` this turn carries the goal. Liveness is a
  property of *what the planner happened to offer*, and is not stored: it is exactly the condition
  under which `L|g` is non-empty.

A valid-but-not-live goal is **preserved and unrestricting**. This is the deliberate reading of the
charter's "nothing overrules a valid kept goal": the planner declining to offer a goal for a turn
is not the goal becoming invalid, and erasing on it would re-create the loop (a teammate standing
on a tree removes it from `chop_candidates`, which is precisely the exchange turn).

**The price, named before the panel measures it.** A chopper whose carry fills has no chop
candidate (`free_capacity() <= 0` routes to `bank_candidates`, `:1802`), so its `Tree(c)` goal goes
not-live, it banks unrestricted, and on the turn its carry empties the goal is live again and it
**walks all the way back to `c`** even if a better tree stands next to the shack. Under the bonus
form this cost was bounded by `M`; under absolute keep it is unbounded. This is the single largest
own-score risk of the no-margin form, it is not hypothetical, and it is measured by `ka=` (kept-goal
age), `xd=` (score given up) and the score-in-units table — §7.4, §7.10.

**The alternative I am not choosing, stated so the reviewer can choose it:** erase the goal on a
not-live turn in which the unit's emitted command carried a different non-`None` target. That kills
the walk-back cost and re-opens the loop (the exchange turn is a not-live turn for the *partner's*
tree, not for the mover's — so it survives the six games — but it makes the rule's semantics
"the goal is whatever you last did", which is nearly the champion). **One-line switch:
`ERASE_WHEN_NOT_LIVE = false` as specified.** I am not proposing to measure both arms; that is a
second candidate, not a knob.

## 3. The four release predicates, each with its observable

Evaluated at the top of `commands()` against the current `view` and a one-turn snapshot
(`last_command: BTreeMap<i32, String>`, `last_carried: BTreeMap<i32, i32>`, `last_inventory:
[i32; 6]`). Order is fixed: **dead, gone, impossible, done** — first firing wins, so the census
sums to `kr=` exactly and no turn is double-counted.

### 3.1 Dead — `rx=`
`unit.id` is absent from `view.units.iter().filter(|u| u.player == 0)`. Erase. Ids are not reused
by the engine, so no successor inherits the entry. Observable: `rx=` counts erasures this turn.

### 3.2 Done — `rd=`, **and the charter-wording finding**

The charter says *"progress at the goal (the accepted `progress_event`: chopped / picked / dropped
/ planted / banked there)"*. Written per `Target` kind against the previous turn's emitted command
and the current view:

| goal | done iff |
|---|---|
| `Bank(c)` | `unit.total_carried() == 0` and `last_command[u]` was `DROP <id>` |
| `Shack` | `unit.total_carried() == 0` |
| `Cell(c)` (plant site, incl. the regeneration `PICK` whose `target` is `Target::Cell(unit.cell)`, `:1798`/`:1967`) | `view.plant_at(c).is_some()` **and** `last_command[u]` was `PLANT <id> <kind>` |
| `Tree(c)` | **see below — I propose it never fires** |

**The finding.** If a `CHOP` at `c` discharges a `Tree(c)` goal, then the mover releases its goal on
the very turn it arrives and chops, and the next turn it re-picks from scratch — which is the
champion's behaviour and the mechanism of the loop (r1 §5.1: the goals stay with the cells). The
loop proof would then hold for exactly one turn past the exchange. A single chop is progress
**toward** a tree goal, not completion **of** it; the completion of `Tree(c)` is the tree ceasing to
exist, which §3.3 already handles as *gone*. **I therefore propose `Tree(c)` has no `done`
predicate, only `gone`.**

I am not free to make this call: it contradicts the charter's plain list. Both readings are
specified so the ruling is one word.

- **`DONE_ON_CHOP = false` (proposed).** `Tree(c)` releases only on gone / impossible / dead.
  Counted quantity: `kc=` — units whose kept goal is a `Tree` and whose emitted command was `CHOP`,
  i.e. how many turns the rule holds a troll on a tree it is already chopping (where it is inert:
  the champion would choose the same `CHOP`, scored `10_000.0` at `:1947` in the endgame path and
  the top of `chop_candidates` elsewhere).
- **`DONE_ON_CHOP = true` (the charter's literal words).** Then §8's loop proof must be re-argued
  turn by turn and I do not currently believe it survives. If the reviewer rules this way, I will
  say so in the packet rather than build to a proof I do not have.

If a `progress_event` fires for a unit **away from** its goal, nothing is released. "Progress"
without "there" is not in the charter and is not in this rule.

### 3.3 Gone — `rg=`

| goal | gone iff |
|---|---|
| `Tree(c)` | `view.plant_at(c)` is `None`, or that plant's `health <= 0` (`:134`, and `plants[i].health` as `reconcile_regeneration_commitments` reads it, `:1694-1698`) |
| `Bank(c)` | `c` not in `view.walkable` |
| `Cell(c)` | `c` not in `view.walkable`; **or** `view.plant_at(c).is_some()` while `last_command[u]` was **not** `PLANT` (someone else took the square — the charter's "cell occupied by a plant when the goal was to plant there"); **or** the unit no longer carries the fruit and holds no `regeneration_commitments` entry (`:1687`) — the fruit is gone |
| `Shack` | never |

A tree that merely stops matching `type_to_cut` is **not** gone — it goes not-live and is preserved
(§2). That is a deliberate difference from "no longer admits the action" and it is named because a
reviewer could reasonably want the opposite.

### 3.4 Impossible — `ri=`

No path on the walkable map **with the teammate's cell treated as free**. The champion's own BFS
already has that property: `bfs_distances(&view.walkable, &[unit.cell])` (`:230`, `:845`) walks
`view.walkable`, a static map-derived set (`:329-355`) that never removes occupied cells. So the
predicate is:

```
let d = bfs_distances(&view.walkable, &[unit.cell]);
impossible = !d.contains_key(&goal_cell)      // goal_cell: c for Tree/Bank/Cell, shacks[0] for Shack
```

and it costs **no new BFS** on any turn the unit already has candidates built. A standing teammate
is therefore never an impossibility — it is the swap rule's business, exactly as the charter says.

### 3.5 Release latency — the charter's obligation, discharged

The charter requires: *a kept goal cannot outlive its usefulness by more than the release latency.*

The bot observes the world only between engine turns. An event during the engine's resolution of
turn `t` first appears in the `view` handed to `commands()` at `t+1`. R4(a) runs **before any
candidate is built** at `t+1`, and all four predicates are computed from that same `view` plus the
one-turn snapshot the bot already holds. Therefore:

> **a kept goal restricts zero turns after its invalidating event is observable**, and the
> event-to-release latency is exactly one engine turn, which is the minimum any bot in this
> interface can achieve.

The residual is not latency but **blindness**: `done` for `Bank`/`Cell` reads `last_command`, so if
`resolve_move_conflicts` rewrote the command the unit did not do what the snapshot says. That is
why §6's recording matches the **emitted** line post-resolver, and why `last_command` is the
emitted line too, not `select`'s output. Same source, one rule, no disagreement possible.

Observable for the obligation itself: **`ka=` — the maximum `view.turn - kept_since[u]` over all
valid kept goals this turn.** §7.10 pre-commits what a bad `ka` means.

## 4. The selector, path by path — and the parking hole, closed

`MoisanBot::select` (`:937`) has three paths and R4(b) hands each of them subsets of the lists the
champion built.

### 4.1 One unit (`:944-951`)
`max_by(|a, b| a.score.total_cmp(&b.score))` over `L|g`. The kept goal wins by construction. `max_by`
returns the **last** maximal element, so a tie between two candidates carrying the same goal is
decided by list order exactly as in the champion — unchanged, and counted (§7.7).
`L|g` non-empty ⇒ no `WAIT`. **No fallback needed on this path.**

### 4.2 Two units (`:952-972`) — the path the dance runs on
Full `|A| x |B|` joint maximisation over `compatible` (`:912`) and `stock_compatible` (`:931`),
keeping the first strictly-greater sum. With both units restricted the product is `L|g_A x L|g_B`.

**When both keep, the joint scoring has nothing left to choose** — every surviving pair assigns
both trolls their own goals, and the sum only picks between commands carrying the same two targets.
When exactly one keeps, the charter's sentence is literally what the code does: the restricted
troll contributes exactly its candidate, and the joint maximisation **chooses the other troll's
goal around it**.

**The hole.** `best_pair` stays `None` if every restricted cross pair is rejected. Reachable cases:
- `stock_compatible` — both units restricted to `PICK` candidates for the **same item** with
  `inventory[item] < 2` (`:931-936`). Both goals are `Target::Cell(own cell)` and distinct, so
  `compatible` passes and only the stock test rejects. This is a real reachable state.
- `compatible` — one unit unrestricted with every candidate targeting the other's kept goal cell.
- Either list restricted to a single candidate that fails both tests against every partner.

The champion then **falls through to the greedy path** (`:973`) with the restricted lists, where
`.unwrap_or_else(Self::wait)` (`:989`) can hand a troll `WAIT`. That is the parking failure mode.

> **R4(c), two-unit form.** If the restricted two-unit maximisation yields `best_pair == None`,
> **re-run `select` for that turn on the unrestricted `by_id`** (both units) and use its result.
> No goal is released. The turn is counted in `xn=` and `xp=`.

Deterministic (a pure function of the turn's state), and it never leaves the joint path for the
greedy one, so the champion's pair structure is preserved on exactly the turns the rule would
otherwise break it.

### 4.3 Three or more units (`:973-995`)
Descending `sort_by` (stable) then a greedy pass over `used_targets` / `used_stock`. A restricted
unit whose only candidates collide with an earlier unit's `used_targets` gets `wait()`.

> **R4(c), greedy form — two phases.** Phase 1: greedy over the restricted lists, in the champion's
> `ids` order. Phase 2: any unit that received `wait()` in phase 1 **while its unrestricted list
> held a non-`WAIT` candidate** is re-decided against the accumulated `used_targets` /
> `used_stock`, with its full list, in ascending id order. `used_targets` / `used_stock` are
> updated after each unit exactly as the champion does. No goal is released. Counted in `xn=` and
> `xg=`.

Phase 2 cannot un-assign a phase-1 unit, so it cannot loop and cannot change a decision the
restriction did not cause.

**Frequency of the `>= 3` path is itself pre-registered as a count** (§7.7): the champion trains one
second troll, so I expect this path to be rare-to-absent on the 240-game corpus, and if it is
absent the phase-2 rule is untested code — which is a fact the panel must state, not hide.

### 4.4 Where the rule sits, restated
**After** `force_unique_door_clear` (`:2172`), so the door clearance cannot be outvoted by a kept
goal — the same reason as r1, and it matters more now, because a restriction that preceded it would
delete the candidates that function inserts. **Before** `select` (`:2174`), because the preference
is only correct inside the maximisation.

## 5. Telemetry v6 — the margin leaves the decision and stays in the instrument

Carried from r2 item 3 and amended for the no-margin form. v6 is **its own decoder** with **mutual
refusal against v4 and v5** (all three directions asserted as a control), per the charter and my
own card.

### 5.1 Grammar
```python
VERSION  = "v6"
UNIT_RE  = re.compile(r"^u(\d+)=([^/]+)/([^/]+)/r=([^/]*)/b=([^/]*)/k=([012])$")
META_RE  = re.compile(r"^(pz|sp|wc|sw|so|sn|sf|kp|kq|kl|kr|rd|rg|ri|rx|ka|kc|xn|xp|xg|xd|xj)=(\d+)$")
```
No `?`, no optional group: a v6 unit token without `/k=` is a decode error, exactly as a v5 token
without `/b=` is. Every per-turn field is REQUIRED. **`m=` is deleted** — there is no margin
constant to disambiguate a wire with, which is itself the version's signature.

`k=` is now **three-valued**, because two-valued would hide the case §2 is about:
`k=2` the unit was restricted and its emitted command carried the kept goal;
`k=1` the unit held a **valid but not-live** goal (unrestricted this turn, goal preserved);
`k=0` the unit held no valid kept goal.
A restricted unit that did **not** emit its goal is only possible via R4(c); those turns are
identified by `xn>0` and the unit reads `k=1` there by the not-live-or-not-restricting rule.

### 5.2 Census
`kp=` units entering the turn with a valid kept goal · `kq=` of those, restricted (goal live) ·
`kl=` of those, not live · `kr=` goals released this turn, with `rd= rg= ri= rx=` the four reasons
(`rd+rg+ri+rx == kr` exactly, by the fixed order of §3) · `ka=` max kept-goal age in turns ·
`kc=` `Tree`-goal units whose emitted command was `CHOP` (§3.2) · `xn=` fallback turns, split
`xp=` (two-unit) and `xg=` (greedy). `pz=1`, `sp=0`, `b=0` remain invariants — R4 adds no holder —
and a violation is a defect in R4, not a curiosity.

### 5.3 `xd=` and `xj=` — what the rule gave up, reported and never tested
The r3 quantity `rho` does not disappear; it stops being a threshold and becomes the price tag.
Definitions keep r2 §3.2's flooring, positivity and saturation rules verbatim.

**`xd=` — per-turn maximum single-unit give-up, basis points.** Over units that were **restricted**
this turn and whose best **pre-restriction** score `S_free` (over the full `L`) exceeds their best
kept-goal score `S_kept` (over `L|g`), with `S_kept > 0`:
```
xd = max over those units of  floor(10000 * (S_free - S_kept) / S_kept)
```
`floor` toward zero, clamp to `999999` and emit exactly that (a saturated value is decode-visible
and read as "at least 100x", never as a measurement), `xd=0` when no unit qualifies, units with
`S_kept <= 0` excluded and counted in `kq=` only.

**`xj=` — the joint give-up on the two-unit path.** Emitted when both units were restricted:
```
xj = floor(10000 * (S_B - S_A) / S_A)
```
`S_A` = the chosen restricted pair's score sum, `S_B` = the best **unrestricted** pair's score sum
for the same turn, same flooring and saturation. `xj=0` when fewer than two units were restricted
or when `S_A <= 0`; if the unrestricted maximisation has no pair either, `xj=0` and the turn is in
`xn=`.

`xd` is the per-troll diagnostic; **`xj` is the successor to r3's `rho`** and is the number the
owner should read to know what absolute keep costs. Both are emitted because reading only `xd` on a
two-unit turn attributes a joint decision to one troll — the same class of error as reading a pair
goal off a unit token.

## 6. Recording, carried verbatim from r2 item 2, with one gate added

> Recording happens **after** `MoisanBot::resolve_move_conflicts` (`:2175`), against the **final
> emitted command line**, never against `select`'s output. For each own unit **that entered the
> turn without a valid kept goal**, the emitted command carrying that unit's id in field 1 is
> matched by **exact string equality** against that unit's **pre-restriction** candidate list `L`:
>
> - **exactly one match** — its `target` becomes the kept goal, unless the target is
>   `Target::None`, in which case no entry is created;
> - **two or more matches with the same `target`** — that target becomes the kept goal;
> - **two or more matches with differing targets** — **no entry**. Position is never a tie-break;
> - **zero matches** — **no entry**. `resolve_move_conflicts` can replace a selected `MOVE` with
>   something no candidate proposed;
> - **no emitted command carrying that unit's id** (dead, or a bare `WAIT`) — **no entry**.
>
> Ids are parsed exactly as `remember_selected_regeneration` (`:1702-1715`) parses them, so the two
> recording steps cannot disagree about which command belongs to which unit.

The added gate is the italicised clause: **a unit with a valid kept goal is never re-recorded.**
Under the bonus form recording was the ordinary way a goal ended; under absolute keep, **release is
the only way a goal ends**, and recording only ever fills an empty slot. Erasure-on-ambiguity is
still the safe direction: a missing kept goal costs one turn of preference; a wrong one is a
preference for something the troll is not doing.

## 7. Plan-keeping — Candidate 0's disease, and it needs no new machinery

The charter asks for the two-step goal "a fruit picked to plant is kept until it is planted" as
**one predicate**. It already is one, and the reason is a fact about the champion rather than a
design of mine:

- the regeneration `PICK` candidate carries `target: Target::Cell(unit.cell)` (`:1795-1798` and
  `:1964-1967`) — **the cell the troll intends to plant in**, not the pick itself;
- the `PLANT` candidate for that cell carries the **same** `Target::Cell(cell)` (`:1916-1927`);
- the bank clause's `DROP` carries `Target::Bank(cell)` (`:590-610`) — a **different** target.

So a troll that picks for regeneration records `Cell(c)`; on every later turn `L|Cell(c)` contains
the `MOVE`-toward-`c` or the `PLANT`, and the `DROP` is simply not in `L|Cell(c)`. **R4(b) alone
forbids the `PICK`→`DROP` two-cycle**, with no special case, no clause about regeneration and no
reference to Candidate 0's fallback. Release is the ordinary §3: **done** when a plant appears at
`c` after this unit's `PLANT`; **gone** when `c` leaves `walkable`, when someone else's plant
occupies `c`, or when the fruit is no longer carried and no `regeneration_commitments` entry holds
it; **impossible** when `c` is unreachable; **dead**.

The one interaction to state: `remember_selected_regeneration` (`:1702`) and
`reconcile_regeneration_commitments` (`:1682`) are untouched, and R4 does not read or write
`regeneration_commitments` except in the "fruit gone" test of §3.3. Two independent memories of the
same intention, and the panel must show they never disagree — pre-registered as a **counted**
quantity: turns where a unit holds a `Cell(c)` kept goal and no `regeneration_commitments` entry,
or the reverse.

**This is a prediction, not a claim.** Candidate 0's `m061` two-cycle is the test: G-2 re-reads
`m061` on top of Candidate 3, and if the `PICK`↔`DROP` cycle is still there this paragraph is
wrong.

## 8. The loop proof, for the no-margin form

Source is a recording, not a rerun: `claude_1/cure2/loop-anatomy-2026-08-25.md` §2 and
`claude_1/cure2/results/loop-anatomy.json` (four panel games `m078:0`, `m090:0`, `m090:1`,
`m118:1`; two fixtures OSC-006, OSC-007), plus the 23-exchange input table of r3 §2.

At the first post-exchange turn `t+1` of every exchange with a goal trade:

1. The mover's kept goal is its **own** tree `T2` (recorded at `t`, and R4(d) cannot overwrite it
   at `t` because it held a valid goal). It is **valid**: `T2`'s plant exists with `health > 0`
   (not gone); `T2` is in `bfs_distances(&view.walkable, &[mover.cell])` since the mover walked
   from beside it (not impossible); the mover chopped nothing at `T2` (not done, under
   `DONE_ON_CHOP = false`; and not done under either reading, because it has not chopped `T2`);
   it is alive.
2. It is **live**: `chop_candidates` (`:836`) offers every walkable tree the unit can reach, and
   `T2` is reachable. So `L|T2` is non-empty and the mover is restricted to it.
3. The partner's kept goal `T1` — the tree the mover now stands on — is valid and live by the same
   three tests, so the partner is restricted to `L|T1`.
4. `compatible(Tree(T1), Tree(T2))` is `T1 != T2` — true. Neither command is a `PICK`, so
   `stock_compatible` is true. **`best_pair` is exactly the keeping pair**, and no score can choose
   otherwise because there is nothing else in the product. R4(c) does not fire.
5. Therefore the mover's `target` at `t+1` is its own tree, not the cell it stands on. Candidate 2's
   exchange clause 6 (`target != landing`) fails for the reverse direction, and **no second
   exchange can fire**.

The proof is now three equality tests and a subset — it no longer depends on any inequality, which
is the whole point of the charter correction. **The three r3 rows that were NOT SCOREABLE resolve
instead of being dropped:**

- **OSC-007:11 and m118:1:11** — the partner's tree died. §3.3 **gone** fires, the partner is
  unrestricted, and the rule is **inert** at exactly the turn the champion's own loop ended. This
  is the positive control for `rg=`.
