## 3. A second correction to my own packet: the swept region was too narrow

My r2 sweep took `K1, K2 ∈ [4,14]` and `Delta ∈ {1,3}` and found a worst case of exactly
**0.2500 at `Delta = 1, K = 4`**, which is why `M = 0.25` was chosen and why the residual was

**on** the tree it is about to chop, with the tree two chops from falling. The region I swept did
not contain the data. Your independent arithmetic check reproduced my bound faithfully; the bound
was right about its region and the region was wrong.

That is the second time in three revisions that a claim of mine about *where* the hard case lives
has been wrong (r1 said `Delta = 3`; r2 said `K <= 4` at `Delta = 1` was the untested corner and
implied `K >= 4` covered the rest). The pattern is that I keep reasoning about the region instead
of measuring it, and the measurement was available the whole time.

## 4. Why this is structural, and not an argument for a bigger `M`

`rho` **rises monotonically along a loop, in every one of the four looping games**: OSC-006 and
`m078:0` go 0.0667 → 0.0889 → 0.1200 → 0.1667 → 0.2444 over five exchanges; `m090:0` goes 0.1053 →
0.1368 → 0.1842 → **0.2698**; `m090:1` goes 0.0231 → 0.0389 → 0.0625 → 0.1019. The cause is visible
in the inputs: the shared tree's `K` falls as the pair chops it (`K = 7,6,5,4,3` down the OSC-006
column) while the far tree's `K` stays put, so the trading pair's advantage grows without bound as
`K → 1`. **Any fixed multiplicative `M` is therefore exceeded eventually, given enough exchanges
before the tree falls.** `M = 0.25` survives a four-exchange loop and fails a five-exchange one; it
happens that `m090:0` is the game with the fifth exchange at the smallest `K`.

So the honest reading of item 1 is not "0.25 was slightly too small". It is that **the fixed-margin
form of the rule cannot discharge the chartered obligation** — no constant `M` proves "no second
exchange in these six games" for a loop of unbounded length, and a constant chosen to fit the
longest loop in this corpus is a fit to the corpus, which is the retuning the charter forbids.

**What I am not doing:** proposing `M = 0.3`, proposing an `M` that depends on `K`, proposing a
per-game `M`, or moving the check into G-1. Each of those is a design change and needs your ruling
or the owner's, not my hand.

**What a ruling could reasonably choose between**, stated so the options are on the table and not
so I can pick one: (a) accept a rule that preserves the keeping pair *except* in the endgame of a
cluster, with the exception measured and pre-registered rather than proved away; (b) change the
rule's form — a margin against the *marginal* gain, or a bound on the number of exchanges per pair,
neither of which I have designed; (c) **request a charter correction**, which is the branch your
ruling explicitly permits if the recordings cannot support the required calculation. They can
support it, and it comes out negative; so (c) applies to the *obligation*, not to the evidence.
