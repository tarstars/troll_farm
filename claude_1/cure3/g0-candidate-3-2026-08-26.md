## 1. The rule, exact text

> **R3.** Each own troll remembers at most one goal: `kept_goals: BTreeMap<i32, Target>`, one
> `Target` per unit id, persisted on the bot across turns exactly as `regeneration_commitments`
> (`:531`) already is.
>
> **(a) Release.** At the start of `commands()`, before any candidate is built, every kept goal is
> tested by the release predicate of §3 and dropped if it fires.
>
> **(b) Keep.** After the per-unit candidate lists are built and after `force_unique_door_clear`
> (`:2168`) has had its say, and before `MoisanBot::select` (`:2170`), every candidate whose
> `target` equals that unit's surviving kept goal **and whose `score` is strictly positive** has
> its score multiplied by `1 + M`. Nothing else changes: **no candidate is added, removed,
> filtered or reordered**, no resolver clause is touched, no lock and no timer is introduced.
>
> **(c) Record.** After `select` returns, each unit's chosen command is looked up in that unit's
> own **pre-bonus** candidate list; the matching candidate's `target`, when it is not
> `Target::None`, becomes that unit's kept goal for the next turn. On no match, or on
> `Target::None`, the unit's entry is removed.

`Target` is `Copy + Eq + Ord` (`:452`), so the map and the equality test need nothing new.

Placement matters and is part of the rule: **after** `force_unique_door_clear`, because that
function rewrites candidate lists to clear the door and must not be outvoted by a kept goal;
**before** `select`, because §4 shows the preference is only correct inside the maximisation.

### 1.1 The one structural safety property, stated before anything else

The bonus applies only to candidates **the unmodified planner produced this turn**. It cannot
invent a goal, cannot resurrect one, and cannot keep a troll pursuing something the champion is no
longer offering: if no candidate this turn carries the kept target, the multiplication touches
nothing and the ordinary planner decides the turn unaltered. **A kept goal therefore cannot park a
troll.** That is the answer to the failure mode the charter's order 2 pre-announces, and it is a
property of where the rule sits, not of the release predicate's generosity. §3 is the second line
of defence, not the first.

---

## 2. Validity

A kept goal `g` for unit `u` is **live on turn `t`** iff at least one candidate in `by_id[u]` on
turn `t` has `target == g`. Liveness is not stored, computed or branched on — it is exactly the
condition under which the multiplication in R3(b) has an effect, so it needs no code. A goal that
is not live on a turn is left in the map (it may become live again on the next turn, e.g. a tree
that was momentarily out of `chop_candidates` because a teammate stood on it) unless a release
in §3 fires; but it is overwritten at the end of the turn by R3(c) whenever the unit chose
something with a different target, which is the common case. **Recording is the ordinary way a
goal ends; §3 is for the cases recording cannot see.**

## 3. Release predicate

Evaluated at the top of `commands()` against the current view and a one-turn snapshot
(`last_carried: BTreeMap<i32, i32>`, `last_command: BTreeMap<i32, String>`, `last_inventory:
[i32; 6]`, `no_progress_turns: BTreeMap<i32, u32>`). Any one firing drops the goal.

- **R-a — death.** `unit.id` is absent from this turn's own units. The map entry is erased (and
  ids are not reused by the engine, so nothing inherits it).
- **R-b — disappearance.** `Tree(c)` and `view.plant_at(c)` is `None` or that plant's
  `health <= 0`; or `Bank(c)` / `Cell(c)` and `c` is not in `view.walkable`. `Shack` never
  disappears.
- **R-c — progress at it.** The accepted `progress_event` for this unit, per A2 as used by
  `detect_d1` and quoted in `claude_1/dance1/definitions-g1-r3-2026-08-24.md` §0: `total_carried()`
  changed since last turn, **or** own inventory changed on a turn whose selected command for this
  unit was `DROP` or `PICK`, **or** a plant appeared or disappeared at the unit's own cell. This is
  the charter's "progress at it (the accepted `progress_event`)" and it is taken verbatim rather
  than re-derived, so the panel and the detector agree on the word by construction.
- **R-d — completion.** `Bank(c)` or `Shack` and `unit.total_carried() == 0`; or `Cell(c)` held for
  a conversion and `view.plant_at(c)` is now `Some`. Completion is a state, not an act: a `CHOP` is
  not a completion, which is why felling a tree is R-b and not R-d.
- **R-e — staleness backstop.** A kept goal that survives **7 consecutive turns with no
  `progress_event` for its unit** is released, and its unit's counter reset. The number is not a
  taste constant: the accepted D-1 detector declares a dance at `k >= 3`, i.e. **>= 7 states with
  zero progress events** (same source as R-c). A goal that has produced nothing for as long as the
  accepted instrument needs to call a stall is released rather than defended. **R-e can only ever
  release, never keep**, so it cannot create the failure mode it exists to prevent.

R-e is the backstop; §1.1 is the guarantee. A reviewer who thinks R-e is the load-bearing half
should read §1.1 first, because if §1.1 is wrong the rule is wrong whatever R-e says.

## 4. The selector interaction — and why a per-unit filter is wrong

`MoisanBot::select` (`:933`) has **three** paths, and the dance runs on the second one:

- **One unit (`:941-946`)** — `max_by(|a, b| a.score.total_cmp(&b.score))`. A boosted kept
  candidate wins iff its boosted score beats every challenger. Note `max_by` returns the **last**
  maximal element, so an exact tie between a boosted kept candidate and a challenger is decided by
  position in the list. Ties are pre-registered as a counted quantity (§7), not argued away.
- **Two units (`:948-968`)** — a full `|A| x |B|` **joint** maximisation over `compatible` (`:908`)
  and `stock_compatible` (`:927`), keeping the first strictly-greater sum. **This is the path that
  governs a two-troll dance, and on it a preference expressed as a per-unit filter is not merely
  suboptimal, it changes the problem**: filtering each unit's list independently can delete the
  half of a pair that the joint maximum needs, and can leave `best_pair` empty where the champion
  had a pair. Expressed as a score bonus, the same preference becomes a term inside the sum, the
  pair structure is untouched, and the margin is **joint** — the pair keeps both goals unless the
  best trading assignment beats the keeping assignment by `M` **on the sum**. §5 shows the loop
  proof leans on exactly that.
- **Three or more units (`:969-`)** — a descending `sort_by` (stable, so equal scores keep list
  order) then a greedy pass over `used_targets` / `used_stock`. Boosted kept candidates rise; the
  collision bookkeeping is untouched and the rule adds no exception to it.

The bonus is uniform across all three paths **because all three maximise the same `score` field**,
which is the whole reason for choosing a score bonus over any other shape.

### 4.1 Two limits of the multiplicative form, stated not buried

1. **Non-positive scores are excluded.** `fruit_candidates` (`:717`) scores
   `base_score - (travel + wait)` and can in principle go non-positive; multiplying a negative
   score by `1 + M` moves it the wrong way. R3(b) therefore applies the factor only to
   `score > 0`. `MoisanBot::wait()` is `score: 0.0` with `Target::None` (`:901-906`) and is
   excluded twice over. **Pre-registered count:** turns on which a kept goal's best candidate had
   `score <= 0` and the bonus was inert.
2. **The margin is relative, so it means different things on different score families.**
   `chop_candidates` scores `1000.0 * wood / turns` (`:883`) — a ratio, where `M` is exactly a
   tolerated **trip inflation** (§5.3). `bank_candidates` scores `8000` / `7000 - dist` (`:599`)
   and the conversion scores are large offsets, where the same `M` is a much larger absolute
   allowance. The rule is being introduced to fix a chopper dance, so it is tuned on the ratio
   family; the offset families are where a too-large `M` would do damage, and that is what the
   cap in §5.3 and the panel's D-1/D-3/P3/P4 rows are for.

## 5. The loop proof — six games, from the recorded goals

Source, and it is a recording not a rerun: `claude_1/cure2/loop-anatomy-2026-08-25.md` §2 (the
12 exchanges with both units' chosen goals at `t-1`, `t`, `t+1`, read off the instrument arm's
wire) and `claude_1/cure2/results/loop-anatomy.json`. The four panel games are `m078:0`, `m090:0`,
`m090:1`, `m118:1`; the two fixtures are OSC-006 and OSC-007.

### 5.1 The mechanism, in one sentence

The exchange puts the mover on the partner's square, that square is a live tree in **11 of the 12**
recorded exchanges, and on arrival the champion's planner re-picks the tree under the mover's feet
and hands the mover's old goal to the troll now standing where it came from: **the goals stay
attached to the cells, not the trolls**, and the same standing-partner block re-forms in the
opposite direction two turns later. Every reversal in the recording is accompanied by a goal
change on **both** sides.

### 5.2 What R3 does to that, per game

The claim to be proved is: *no second exchange can fire.* The rule reaches it by removing the goal
change, which the recorded wire shows is present on both sides of every reversal — so removing it
removes the reversal, provided the keep survives the margin test at the first post-exchange turn.

- **m078:0 and OSC-006** (same map fragment, 5 exchanges each, `c=(1,3)`, `T=(2,2)`, `L=(2,3)`).
  At `t+1` the mover stands on `TREE(2,3)` holding kept goal `TREE(2,2)`; the partner stands at
  `(1,3)` holding kept goal `TREE(2,3)`. The keeping assignment is
  `{mover -> TREE(2,2), partner -> TREE(2,3)}` — one step each, both `compatible` (distinct
  targets). The trading assignment the champion chose is
  `{mover -> TREE(2,3), partner -> TREE(2,2)}` — zero steps and two steps. Both assignments cover
  the **same two trees with the travels swapped**, so the joint test of §4 is symmetric and the
  boost applies to **both** terms of the keeping sum. On the keep, the mover steps to `(2,2)` and
  chops, the partner steps back onto `(2,3)` and chops, neither is blocked by a standing teammate,
  and the exchange predicate is never reached: **no second exchange**.
- **m090:0** (4 exchanges over t=6..12, `c=(1,5)`, `T=(5,5)`, `L=(2,5)`) — identical shape with the
  kept goal **three** steps beyond the landing rather than one. Same joint symmetry, and the
  recording's own counterfactual sentence agrees: the mover "would have carried on from (2,5) to
  `TREE(5,5)`, three more steps east, while `u0` chopped `TREE(2,5)`". The larger `Delta` is what
  §5.3 is about.
- **m090:1** (4 exchanges over t=12..21, `c=(6,5)`, `T=(2,5)`, `L=(5,5)`) — mirror image of
  `m090:0`, three steps west, same argument.
- **m118:1 and OSC-007** (2 exchanges each, `c=(6,6)`, `T=(2,5)`, `L=(5,6)`) — same shape one cell
  over. The second reversal in the recording ends only because `TREE(5,6)` dies; under R3 the
  partner's goal is released by **R-b** at that turn, which is the release predicate doing exactly
  the job it exists for and is worth checking at G-1 as a positive control.
- **m090:0 t=3** is the one exchange in the whole set with **no** goal trade — the mover was
  carrying wood to the bank and neither unit re-picked. R3 is **inert** there: no goal changed, so
  there is nothing to keep. This is the packet's own negative control, and if G-1 shows a
  behaviour change on `m090:0` t=3 the rule is doing something it is not supposed to do.

### 5.3 Where the proof is conditional, stated as a condition and not as a hope

The keep survives only if the trading assignment does not beat the keeping assignment by `M`. On
the chop score `1000 * wood / turns`, with equal wood and a kept goal `Delta` steps further than
the challenger, the **per-unit** test is

> keep iff `T_kept <= (1 + M) * T_chal`, i.e. **`M >= Delta / T_chal`**

where `T` is the candidate's whole trip (`travel + chop + return + 1`, `:875`). With trips in the
8-15 range, `M = 0.15` buys **1.2 to 2.2 turns** of extra travel. That covers `Delta = 1`
(m078:0, OSC-006) and **does not obviously cover `Delta = 3`** (m090:0, m090:1) on the per-unit
test. On the **joint** test of §4 the two travels are swapped between the same two trees, so the
asymmetry partly cancels and the condition is weaker — which is why §5.2 argues jointly and why
the rule must be a score bonus.

**I am not asserting the joint test is satisfied on m090 at `M = 0.15`; the exact scores are a
measurement and they belong to G-1.** What is pre-registered here, before the run:

- `M = 0.15` as the charter proposes, **capped at 0.25** by pre-registration. A larger `M` is
  Candidate 1's failure mode relocated into the planner, and I will not raise it silently.
- If any of the six games needs `M > 0.15` on the joint test, **the packet reports the required
  value per game and the rule is re-ruled** — it is not re-tuned to whatever makes the six games
  green. Tuning a constant until the corpus agrees is how an instrument stops measuring.
- If C-5 is not 0 on all six at the accepted `M`, that is reported as a **partial** fix with the
  residual games named, not as a pass.

### 5.4 What the proof does not cover

The recorded goals are the *instrument arm's* wire (base + swap + v5 telemetry). The champion the
PR modifies is the **swap arm's base**, so the loop proof is an argument about the arm Candidate 2
built, not about the merged champion, and it is only as good as the swap rule staying as it is.
This is the same identity caution the C-5 evidence carries and it travels with this packet.

## 6. Telemetry — the charter asks for a v5 extension, and v5 cannot carry it

Read from the decoder, not from memory (`claude_1/narrate5/narrate5.py`):

- `UNIT_RE = ^u(\d+)=([^/]+)/([^/]+)/r=([^/]*)/b=([^/]*)$` — anchored. Appending `/k=1` to a unit
  token is **off grammar** and `decode` raises `GateError` (`:95`), by design: "never guesses a
  missing field into a default".
- `META_RE = ^(pz|sp|wc|sw|so|sn|sf)=(\d+)$` — a new per-turn field is likewise off grammar
  (`:87-95`).
- `VERSION = "v5"` is checked before anything else and anything else is REFUSED (`:79-81`).
- Scores are not integers, and `META_RE` accepts `\d+` only, so a margin cannot be emitted as a
  ratio without a representation decision.

**So the extension is a v6 decoder**, not a v5 addition: new `VERSION`, `UNIT_RE` extended with an
optional `/k=([01])` group, `META_FIELDS` extended, and the margin emitted as an **integer
basis-point** field (`x=` in units of 1/10000 of the kept score, so `x=1500` is 15.00 %) to stay
inside `\d+`. Consequences the reviewer should rule on:

1. A v6 arm's wire **cannot be read by the accepted v5 decoder**, so any run that compares a
   Candidate 3 arm against a Candidate 2 arm must decode each with its own version, and
   `narrate4`/`narrate5` mutual-refusal controls must be re-asserted for v6 against v5.
2. `b=` is identically 0 and `pz=1` / `sp=0` in every v5 arm because no rule adds a holder. **R3
   adds no holder either**, so those three invariants are pre-registered as unchanged for v6, and a
   violation is a defect in R3, not a curiosity.
3. Proposed v6 tokens: per unit `k=1` when the unit's chosen candidate carried its kept goal and
   `k=0` otherwise; per turn `kp=` goals kept, `kr=` goals released this turn with a one-letter
   reason census `ka/kb/kc/kd/ke` matching R-a..R-e, and `x=` the largest overrule margin in basis
   points on that turn (0 when none). Every field REQUIRED, per v5's own rule that a field which
   may go missing will go missing where it matters.
