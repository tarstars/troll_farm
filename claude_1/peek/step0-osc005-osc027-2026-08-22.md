**The integrator's standing doubt is CONFIRMED on both fixtures, with one number corrected.**
It was recorded as "turn 52, 34 turns after the episode ends". The episode's own `turn_end` is

## Why "cannot be determined" is the honest answer and not a hedge

`claude_1/swap1/g1-event-table-2026-08-21.json` records **fires only** — 36 rows, one per fire.
A widened trigger fires exactly where the current one does not, so the table is **structurally
incapable** of answering the question directly: the turns it would have to speak about are the
turns it does not record. No amount of re-reading it changes that. This is the same failure shape
as a green guard that cannot see what it is trusted to cover, and I am naming it rather than
inferring around it.

## What IS determinable, and it matters for step 1

Two facts, both from artifacts, and both cut against PEEK reaching R-1's busy-blocker half.

**1. Both episodes are genuinely reproduced on the base, so "no fire in-window" is a fact about
this world and not about a different game.**
`claude_1/regrade2/regrade34-identity-2026-08-21.json` lists **OSC-005 and OSC-027 among the 11
the champion reproduces**, where the champion is `claude_1/chop4c/candidate-door1.rs`
sha256 `547fa706…` — **byte-identical** to swap R-1's base
`cgauto/submissions/candidate-door1-pure-deletion.rs` (both sha256 `547fa706cc1c684a1f8c2a08…`,
checked). "Reproduces" there means the frozen window command lines match **and** the board at the
window's first turn matches (`fixture_harness.episode_identity`), so the in-window command lines
quoted below are the base run's own behaviour, not merely the original recording bot's.

And the candidate is the same world as the base in-window in both cases:
`g1-sweep-2026-08-21.json` gives OSC-005 `first_fire_turn: 52`, `pre_first_fire_identical_to_base:
true`; OSC-027 `fires: 0`, `whole_game_identical_to_base: true`. Turns are absolute game turns —
`fixture_harness.spec_for` rebuilds the whole game from turn 1 rather than resuming from the
frozen entry state — so turn 52 and window 9–20 are on the same clock.

**2. In both windows the partner-state test is NOT what is stopping a fire.**
Rev 1 has **both** paths live: the yield path (partner `WAIT`) and the no-detour path (partner
working). The sweep proves the second is not theoretical — OSC-006's 27 fires are all
`no_detour_path` with the partner on `CHOP`.

- **OSC-005, turns 9–18:** the blocker (unit 0, on the APPLE at (10,1)) emits **`WAIT` on 10 of
  the 12 window turns** (`CHOP` at t19, `MOVE` at t20). The yield path's partner condition is
  therefore already satisfied for most of the window, and rev 1 still never fires.
- **OSC-027, turns 25–30:** the blocker (unit 0, on the BANANA at (10,2)) emits **`CHOP` for six
  consecutive turns** — the busy-blocker shape R-1 is about — and the no-detour path, which is
  exactly the path built for that shape, still never fires.

So in one window the WAIT partner was available and in the other the busy partner was available,
and **neither produced a fire**. Whatever blocks the trigger in these two episodes sits *upstream*
of the partner-state test — no own-unit collision is presented at the seam, or another
precondition rejects it. PEEK changes what the seam decides **when a collision arrives**; it does
not create collisions. **A widening of the partner-state test alone cannot make rev 1 fire inside
either window.** That is a determinate negative, and it is the load-bearing result of step 0.

What remains genuinely open — and is the entire residual — is whether a collision *is* presented
in-window and rejected by one of the trigger's other preconditions. The fire-only table cannot
say, and both fixtures' own `unresolved` fields say the same thing from the other direction: the
goal the bot held during the window is not observable in the transcript.

## One risk the recorded commands do surface, unprompted

OSC-027 turn 24 is `MOVE 0 10 2;MOVE 2 9 2`: unit 0 starts the tick **on (9,2)** and unit 2's step
lands on (9,2). Depending on whether the seam tests occupancy at start-of-tick or after
resolution, that tick is a **collision-shaped site where nothing is wrong** — unit 0 vacates of
its own accord and the base resolves it correctly. A widened trigger is a candidate to fire
*there*, adding a swap on a tick that needed none. I did not measure this and I am not claiming
it happens; it is a named false-positive site for step 2's construction review, from the fixture's
own frozen commands.

## The instrument that would settle the residual, offered and NOT built

A **collision census**: one probe-only line emitted at the seam on **every** tick where an own
unit's proposed step lands on a cell an own unit occupies — whether or not the trigger fires, and
naming which precondition rejected it when it does not. Probe-side only, the same `FIRE_ROW`
insertion point that the builder already keeps out of the delivery candidate, so the candidate
stays byte-identical and probe parity is re-provable exactly as it was on 2026-08-21. It converts
both "cannot be determined" rows into determinate answers and is the cheapest thing that can.
It is **not** built under this card, which forbids a build; it needs codex_1 or local_claude_1 to
say whether step 0 gets a second, instrumented pass before step 1 is ruled.
