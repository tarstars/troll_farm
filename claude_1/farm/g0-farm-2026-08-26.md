## 0. What this packet decides

The contract §6 leaves five things open and the card makes them the deliverable:

1. the denial **aim-selection** rule — which trees we chop to starve the enemy's next troll;
2. the **latch threshold** — the number at which planting stops forever;
3. the **denial round criterion K** — how many rounds of "the aim count did not fall" ends denial;
4. whether the **capacity-2 second troll** is trained before the farm starts;
5. the exact **state machine** TRAIN → DENY(+mothers) → FARM → WOOD, its one-way edges, and the
   observable that fires each edge.

It also pre-commits the panel gates (§9) and names the telemetry (§8).

## 1. Five measured facts that change the contract

The contract asked for the latch and K to be calibrated from the turn corpus
`data/processed/turns.jsonl.gz`. **That corpus cannot answer either question**, for a stated
reason: it keeps the commands a bot issued and drops the board, so hut coordinates — and therefore
the ring — are not reconstructible from it. codex_1 recorded the same limit in
`codex_1/top10/field-comparison-2026-08-26.md` §4 ("unavailable: shack/state coordinates absent").

The **raw replays** answer both. `data/raw/games/*.json` carry (a) a setup frame whose map marks
both huts, `0` and `1`, so the ring is exact, and (b) a per-frame `summary` of the referee's
**accepted** events, so a plant, a harvest or a chop is a fact and not a command that may have been
refused. Troll cells come from the accepted `moved to (x, y)` lines. `claude_1/farm/ring_pressure.py`
does this; it read all 290 replays, 580 game-seats, **0 unattributed events**.

The five facts:

### 1.1 A game is 300 turns, not 200

The contract §1 ends "Game length 200 turns." Measured over all 290 replays: 301 keyframes
(the setup frame plus turns 1–300) in **266 of 290** games; the minimum is 49 (an early stall) and
**92% run the full length**. The champion's own endgame test is `view.turn > 250`
(`readable/door1-champion.rs:2061`), which is only sensible against a 300-turn game.

This is not a footnote. It is the single most favourable fact for a farm: a seed planted at turn 30
reaches size 4 at turn ~54 and is chopped for 16 points with **246 turns still to run**. A plot can
turn over eight to ten times in a game. Every timing in this packet is stated against 300.

### 1.2 Wood fills a troll, whatever its capacity

`docs/mechanics.md:61–62` — "**Wood = 1 fruit then full**". One unit of wood occupies the whole
carry. So carry capacity buys nothing at all for the wood leg of the loop; it buys only the fruit
leg (carry a seed out and a banana back). This is the fact that decides §6.

### 1.3 Two PLANT commands of different types cancel — **on one cell**, not across the turn

**Round 1 got this wrong and round 2 corrects it.** `docs/mechanics.md:94–96` reads
"Same-type simultaneous PLANT commands **on one cell** create one plant …; mixed-type simultaneous
PLANT commands cancel". The cancellation is scoped to a **shared cell** in the same turn, not to
arbitrary mixed-type plants anywhere on the board in one turn. Both reviewers answered Q1 the same
way from the same lines (codex_1's review §Q1; local_claude_1's ack of 20:23:19Z).

The consequence is that **design invariant P has no work to do**. The champion's
`MoisanBot::compatible` (`:912`) already rejects any pair of candidates whose targets are the same
cell, and `select` (`:937`) only ever returns a compatible pair, so our two trolls cannot both be
issued a PLANT on one cell in the first place. §7 therefore records P as *redundant, retained as a
measurement only*, and the round-1 behaviour — stripping the other troll's non-banana PLANT on a
farm-plant turn — is **removed from the design**. Round 1 would have deleted the champion's
unrelated regeneration plant on some turns: an unauthorised behavioural change bought with a
misreading. Gate V7 stays, as a check on the claim, not as a mechanism.

### 1.4 The ring is not eight cells; sometimes it is three

The contract's geometry — 4 orthogonal plots, 4 diagonal mothers — assumes eight walkable ring
cells. Measured over 580 game-seats:

| walkable cells | orthogonal (plots) | diagonal (mothers) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 49.0% | 43.8% |
| 3 | 34.8% | 28.6% |
| 2 | 12.4% | 20.7% |
| 1 | 3.8% | 6.9% |
| 0 | 0% | 0% |

Mean ring size 6.38 of 8. **6.9% of seats can hold at most one mother** and 3.8% at most one plot;
water takes one or more ring cells in 35% of seats. The design therefore states the ring as
"up to 4 plots, up to 4 mothers, degrade to what exists, never fewer than zero", and — decisive —
**when only one orthogonal cell is walkable, that cell is the shack's only door and is never
planted** (§2.4). A farm that assumes eight cells strands a troll on one seat in twenty-five.

### 1.5 The leaders' ring is under real but modest enemy pressure — and that is the latch number

"my ring work" = our own accepted harvests and chops on our own ring cells.

| group | min | lower quartile | **median** | upper quartile | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

## 2. The state machine

Four states, three edges, **every edge one-way**. The bot is in exactly one state; the state is a
single field on `YamoBot` and the only writer is the transition test in `commands()`, run once per
turn before any candidate is built.

```
  TRAIN ──e1──> DENY ──e2──> FARM ──e3──> WOOD
   (no banana action)  (+mothers only)   (plots+mothers)  (no planting ever again)
```

There is no path back from any state. The latch of owner decision 2 is the edge **e3**; once WOOD
is entered the bot plants nothing for the rest of the game except the late wave of §2.5, which is
not farming and is explicitly permitted (contract §9).

### 2.0 TRAIN — no banana action of any kind

Entered at turn 1. The bot is byte-for-byte the champion. The owner's standing rule of 2026-08-10
("no banana action of any kind before the second troll is trained", no exceptions) is enforced
here by construction: the farm code is not reached.

**e1, TRAIN → DENY.** Observable: our own troll count reaches 2. Fires the same turn the referee
accepts the TRAIN. Our bot trains at turn ≈ 9 (codex_1's field table); the leaders' median first
plant is turn 15 (§1.5). **The standing rule costs us nothing**: planting from turn 10 is earlier
than the leaders plant, so the rule and the evidence do not conflict and no departure is needed.

### 2.1 DENY — chop the aim, plant mothers only

Denial is income, not sacrifice: the wood from a felled enemy tree is ours at 4 points a unit
(contract §1). The aim rule is §3. During DENY, **the second troll may plant mothers on diagonal
ring cells and nothing else may be planted** (owner decision 3). The reason is arithmetic: a mother
needs 24 turns to reach size 4 and ~30 turns to its first fruit; a mother planted at turn 10 is
producing seeds at turn 40, a mother planted after a denial that ends at turn 90 is producing at
turn 120 — a third of the game gone.

**e2, DENY → FARM.** Round 1 listed four conditions and left them overlapping: `a` and `d` are both
true whenever zero aim trees remain, and the turn-120 deadline can coincide with any of them. A
reason that is written twice, or written differently on two runs of the same game, is not a
diagnostic. Round 2 fixes the order.

**The exit test is evaluated exactly once per turn, at the top of `commands()`, before any
candidate is built.** The conditions are checked in this fixed order and **the first that holds
wins; the remaining ones are not evaluated**; the reason is written once and the field is then
immutable for the game:

| order | code | condition | observable |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | `t` | the hard deadline | turn ≥ 120 (§10 departure D3) |
| 2 | `c` | the enemy trains a third troll | opponent unit count reaches 3 |
| 3 | `a` | the aim trees are all felled | no living plant of the aim species remains on the enemy's half |
| 4 | `d` | no aim tree is left in reach | at least one aim tree lives on the enemy's half, but none is reachable by BFS from either troll |
| 5 | `b` | reproduction | K consecutive denial rounds in which the aim count did not fall (§5) |

The deadline is first because it is the one condition that must never be pre-empted: it exists so
that denial cannot eat the game, and a bot that reports `a` on turn 200 has already lost the farm.
`a` precedes `d` because "none left" is a stronger and simpler statement than "none reachable", and
the round-2 wording of `d` makes them **mutually exclusive** rather than merely ordered: `d`
requires an aim tree to exist. `b` is last because it is the only reason that needs history.

**The first round's baseline.** Round 1 compared each round's closing aim count "with the previous
round's" and never said what the first round compares against. It compares against the aim count
recorded **when the aim was first selected** — the count at the moment the round opened, not zero
and not the whole-board count. So round 1 of denial can itself be a non-falling round, and the
streak is well defined from the start.

**The aim-change reset.** Counts of two different species are not comparable — three surviving
apples and three surviving plums are the same integer and a different world. If the aim species
changes between rounds, the non-falling streak **resets to zero** and a fresh baseline is taken at
the new aim's selection. Because a reset delays `b`, the design **prefers holding one aim through
the whole of DENY**: the aim is reselected only when the current aim is invalidated — no living
tree of that species remains on the enemy's half, or none is reachable — which are exactly the
`a`/`d` conditions, so in practice an aim change inside DENY is rare and never a preference.

A **hard deadline** is added (§10 departure D3): if nothing else has fired by turn **120**, e2
fires with reason `t`. The contract has no deadline and the owner accepted the risk of an
incomplete denial; a bot with no deadline can spend the whole 300 turns denying and never farm at
all, which is the failure the contract is written to avoid.

### 2.2 FARM — the loop

Per turn, the farm proposes candidates into the champion's existing scoring pool. It never
overrides the champion; it competes.

- **Plots** (orthogonal ring cells, up to 4, minus the door exclusion of §2.4): if empty, plant a
  banana; if size 4, chop it; if growing, leave it. A banana at size 4 has health 2 + 4 = 6, so six
  hits at chop power 1, and yields 4 wood = **16 points from one seed**.
- **Mothers** (diagonal ring cells, up to 4, at least 2 where the map allows; §1.4 says 6.9% of
  seats allow only one): plant, grow to size 4, then **harvest and never chop**. A size-4 banana
  makes one fruit per 6 turns (4 next to water) and stores 3.
- **Seed routing**: a harvested banana goes to an empty plot if one exists, otherwise to the shack
  as 1 point and as reserve for the late wave. The 2026-08-04 defect — "harvested bananas are not
  being collected to the tent" — is gate V6 of §9.
- **Mothers sit away from the enemy** where the map offers a choice: of the walkable diagonals,
  prefer those whose BFS distance to the enemy hut is greatest ("do not create fruit the opponent
  can harvest before us", owner 2026-08-02).
- Water adjacency is a tie-break inside plots and inside mothers, never a siting rule that moves the
  farm off the ring (contract §6).

**e3, FARM → WOOD.** The latch. §4.

### 2.3 WOOD — no planting, ever again

The champion's ordinary wood behaviour, unmodified: chop the best trees in reach, ours and theirs.
Standing ring trees are chopped as any other tree. This state is the champion.

### 2.4 The door exclusion

The shack cell is not walkable; a troll DROPs from an orthogonally adjacent cell. Where **only one**
orthogonal ring cell is walkable (3.8% of seats, §1.4) that cell is the shack's only door and is
**never planted**. A tree cell is walkable, so a planted door does not block movement — but a troll
standing on a tree to harvest it cannot move that turn, and the champion already carries
`force_unique_door_clear` machinery precisely because a single-door shack is fragile. The farm does
not add pressure there. Where two or more orthogonal cells are walkable, all are plots.

### 2.5 The late wave

Unchanged from the champion, which already implements it (`conversion_chop_turns`,
`endgame_candidates`, `readable/door1-champion.rs:1846`): plant every held banana while a tree can
still reach size ≥ 1 before the end and chop it, bank what cannot mature. The farm does not touch
this code. Against a 300-turn game the champion's `turn > 250` endgame gate is the right one and is
left alone.

## 3. The aim-selection rule (contract §6, open item 1)

**Aim = the species that funds the enemy's next troll, resolved by cost, then by scarcity, then by
distance.**

Training the *n*-th troll costs, per talent, `n + talent²` of one fruit: plums for speed, lemons for
capacity, apples for harvest power, iron for chop power (`docs/mechanics.md:90–92`). Bananas and
wood are never a training cost — **denying bananas denies nothing**. So:

1. **Candidate species** = {plum, lemon, apple}. Bananas are never an aim. Iron is not a tree.
2. **Choose the species the enemy is shortest of, relative to a third troll's cost.** We can see the
   opponent's shack inventory in the view (`view.inventories[1]`). For each species *s*, let
   `need(s) = cost_of_third_troll(s) − inventory[1][s]`, clipped at 0, where the third troll's cost
   is evaluated at the enemy's cheapest plausible stat line (the fallback line the champion uses for
   its own second troll). Aim at `argmax need(s)`; ties broken by the smaller standing count of
   that species on the enemy's half.
3. **Restrict to reach**: only trees of the aim species whose BFS distance from our troll is finite
   and which are at least as close to the **enemy** hut as to ours — "near the enemy's hut" made
   exact, and it is exactly the complement of the geometry the superseded Spec A used for our own
   side (2026-08-15).
4. **Recompute the aim at most once per denial round** (§5), never per turn. Per-turn recomputation
   is the A→B→A loop the worker rules of contract §3 forbid; the sticky aim is the fix.

The champion's existing `type_to_cut` (`focus_type`, `:553`) is **not** this and is not reused: it
picks lemon-or-plum by distance from *our* hut to feed *our* training. The aim is a second,
separate field. *Departure D1 in §10 records that the contract's "the species the enemy needs for
its next troll (or the species it has fewest of)" is made a single ordered rule rather than a
choice, because a bot cannot execute "or".*

## 4. The latch (contract §6, open item 2; owner decision 2) — **recalibrated in round 2**

Round 1 set this rule at a threshold ratio of **1.0**, argued from the whole-game leader median of
0.21 (§1.5). codex_1's defect 1 said that inference was invalid: whole-game quartiles do not
establish the false-trigger rate of a rule that reads the last 60 turns. The rule was therefore
run. **It fires on 43% of leader seats.** The 1.0 threshold is withdrawn.

### 4.1 The measurement that overturned round 1

`claude_1/farm/latch_sim.py` replays the rolling rule, exactly as §4.2 states it, over all 580
game-seats, using the per-turn event lists `ring_pressure.py` already records. No replay is re-read
and no new attribution is invented; only the decision rule is new.

Round 1's rule (`w = 60`, `N = 8`, ratio 1.0, fires on the first qualifying turn):

| group | seats | fires | rate | first-trigger turn (q1 / median / q3) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| leaders (goq, yaichi, Stounate) | 37 | 16 | **43.2%** | 51 / 65 / 121 |
| field | 402 | 187 | 46.5% | 22 / 52 / 112 |
| `tass` (agent 6536563) | 141 | 49 | 34.7% | 92 / 129 / 218 |

A latch that stops a leader from farming in nearly half its games, at a median of turn 65 with a
quarter of the fires before turn 51, is not a tail-cutter. It is a farm that mostly does not run.

Raising the ratio alone barely moves it — 2.0 still fires on 40.5% of leader seats, 3.0 on 35.1% —
because the failure is not the ratio. It is that **a single unlucky 60-turn stretch is enough**.
Early in the game our own ring work is thin, so any burst of enemy chopping wins the comparison
once, and once is all a one-way latch needs.

### 4.2 The rule, as revised

Two terms, both counted from referee-accepted effects, both computable in-game:

- `fe(w)` = enemy chop hits on our ring cells over the last `w` turns;
- `fw(w)` = our own accepted harvests and chops on our ring cells over the same window.

The latch fires — once, irreversibly — on the first turn at which the following has held on
**`M` consecutive turns**:

```
    turn        >= w                     (the window is full; no judging on a partial window)
and fw(w)       >= F                     (we are actually running a ring economy to judge)
and fe(w) + fw(w) >= N                   (enough evidence in the window)
and fe(w)       >  R * fw(w)             (the farm feeds them more than us)
```

with **w = 60**, **F = 6**, **N = 12**, **R = 2.0**, **M = 15 consecutive turns**.

Three of those five are new in round 2, and each answers a distinct way round 1 fired early:

- **`turn >= w`** — round 1 evaluated a short window from turn 1, so the opening was judged on a
  handful of events. The `N` gate was supposed to prevent this and did not.
- **`fw >= F`** — the comparison `fe > R·fw` is meaningless when `fw` is 0 or 1: two enemy chops
  beat it. `F = 6` requires that we have done real work on our own ring in the window before the
  ring's value to us is judged. For the farm this gate is nearly free — a running farm harvests and
  chops its ring continually — and for a bot that is not farming it is not, which is correct, since
  the latch exists only to stop farming. §4.4 states what that asymmetry costs the calibration.
- **`M = 15`** — the condition must hold for a quarter of the window before the latch is thrown. A
  one-way, game-long decision should not be taken on one turn's reading. This is the single change
  that does most of the work.

### 4.3 What the revised rule does on the same 580 seats

Measured on the **ring-economy** seats — the 506 of 580 (87.2%) that have at least one 60-turn
window with `fw >= 6`, which is the closest thing this corpus holds to a bot running a ring
economy, and which includes 36 of the 37 leader seats:

| rule | econ-seat fire rate | leader-econ fire rate | median first trigger |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| round 1: `N=8, R=1.0, M=1`, partial windows | 41.1% | 41.7% | 83 |
| **round 2: `w=60, F=6, N=12, R=2.0, M=15`** | **6.7%** (34/506) | **5.6%** (2/36) | **148** |

Over all 580 seats the revised rule fires on 34, i.e. 5.9%. Its earliest fire anywhere in the
corpus is turn 74; quartiles of the first-trigger turn are 101 / 148 / 206. So on this population
the latch is quiet, and when it does speak it speaks in the second half of the game, which is the
behaviour a one-way switch has to have.

Sensitivity of the choice, so the reviewer can see the shape rather than one row: at
`R=2.0, M=10, F=4, N=12` the econ rate is 11.9% and leaders 19.4%; at `R=2.5, M=15, F=6` it is
4.3% and leaders 5.6%; at `R=3.0, M=20, F=6, N=16` it is 2.4% and leaders 0%. The chosen point is
the loosest one at which leaders and the general population agree, and the packet declines to go
tighter because a latch that never fires is not evidence of safety, only of silence.

### 4.4 What this measurement cannot do, stated before the panel

**No seat in these 290 replays is running a banana farm.** The corpus can therefore bound the rule
on one side only: it establishes that the revised rule does **not** fire on ordinary play, and that
round 1's rule did. It cannot establish the true-positive rate, because the population contains no
instance of the thing the latch is for.

Round 1 tried to fill that gap with the whole-game "tail" (the 40 seats at ratio ≥ 2.0). Round 2
does not, and says why: those seats are mostly seats where the bot barely worked its own ring at
all — a small denominator, not a farm being harvested by the enemy. The `F = 6` gate removes them
by construction, which is right for the rule and disqualifying for the tail as a validation set.
Any figure of the form "the revised rule catches X% of the tail" would be measuring the wrong
population, so none is quoted.

The true-positive side moves to the panel, pre-registered in §9: the farm-on arm reports the
**window** distribution of `fe`/`fw` and the realised `fl`, and expectation 3 of §9 is written so
that it can be wrong.

### 4.5 Frozen window semantics

The build implements these and nothing else; the simulator implements the same, which is what makes
the two comparable:

1. The window at turn `T` is the **inclusive** turn range `[T-w+1, T]`. Turns before `w` are not
   evaluated at all (`turn >= w`).
2. Events are counted **per event, not per turn**: two accepted chops on our ring in one turn count
   two.
3. **One enemy hit** = one accepted damage event by one enemy troll on one of our ring cells. In
   game the bot cannot observe damage events, only health, so the build counts, per `(turn, ring
   cell)`, **one** hit when that cell's plant lost health and an enemy troll stands on the cell —
   whatever the size of the loss. A troll chops at most once a turn, so this equals the replay
   counter unless two enemy trolls occupy one cell, in which case the build undercounts by one and
   latches **later**. The approximation errs on the side of not latching, which is the only side
   where an error is cheap.
4. A health loss on a ring cell with **no** enemy troll standing on it is **not** counted: it is not
   attributable to the enemy and may be ours.
5. The test is evaluated **once per turn**, after the referee's events for that turn have been read
   into the view, in the same pass as the state transition test of §2.

## 5. The denial round criterion K (contract §6, open item 3)

A **round** is: "we felled every aim tree we could see and reach". Concretely — the round opens
when the aim is (re)selected, and closes on the first turn at which no aim tree is both alive and
reachable *or* 40 turns have passed, whichever is first. At each close we record the count of aim
trees on the enemy's half. The count is compared with the previous round's.

**K = 2.** Denial ends with reason `b` after **two consecutive rounds whose closing aim count did not
fall below the previous round's**.

**Why 2 and not 3.** The enemy replants. Measured: accepted plants within Chebyshev distance 2 of
the planter's own hut, per game-seat over 300 turns — leaders median **29** (quartiles 21–35, n=37),
the rest of the field median **14** (quartiles 5–25, n=402). That is one new tree near their shack
every **10 turns** against a leader and every **21** against the field, and the field's planting
rate per 50 turns barely declines across the whole game (2.8 → 3.4, §1.5). A denial round takes 20–40 turns (travel plus six chop hits
for a banana, twenty for an apple), so **one to two replacement trees appear inside every round we
run**. Against a planting opponent the aim count is a treadmill, and a K of 3 spends 60 to 120 turns
— a fifth to two fifths of the game — proving something two rounds already showed. K = 2 with the
turn-120 deadline of §2.1 bounds denial at 120 turns in the worst case and typically ends it near
turn 60–90, which puts the farm in production while five sixths of the game remains.

## 6. Is the capacity-2 troll trained first? (contract §6, open item 4)

**No change to the champion's training choice. The farm does not ask for capacity 2 and must not.**

The contract §2 prefers capacity 2 because "it carries a seed and a banana to bank in one trip".
That is true for the fruit leg. But §1.2 measured the other leg: **wood fills a troll whatever its
capacity** (`docs/mechanics.md:61–62`, "Wood = 1 fruit then full"). The farm's income is wood — 4
points a unit against 1 for a banked banana — so the leg that capacity would help is the minor one,
and a plot is one or two steps from the shack by construction, so the trip it saves is one turn.

Against that, capacity 2 costs 5 lemons + 2 plums + 2 apples + 2 iron, and the champion's opening
policy (`YamoOpeningPolicy::TUNED_CARRY`, `:504`) already searches the stat line by estimated time
to afford it and abandons a line that is more than 15 turns of extra ETA. Forcing capacity 2 would
overrule a tuned, measured opening for a one-turn saving on the minor leg. **The farm therefore
takes whatever second troll the champion's opening policy chooses**, and the panel reports the
realised stat line so the question can be reopened with evidence rather than preference. This is
departure D2 in §10.

The build extends the diagnostic line the contract §5 requires ("plants, mother harvests, latch

The farm group, **nine** tokens (seven in round 1; `fE`/`fW` added by defect 2), ≤ 44 characters:

| token | meaning |
|---|---|
| `fs=` | state: `0` TRAIN, `1` DENY, `2` FARM, `3` WOOD |
| `fp=` | accepted farm plants so far this game |
| `fh=` | accepted mother harvests so far |
| `fl=` | the turn the latch fired, `0` if it has not |
| `fd=` | denial end reason, one of `a b c d t`, `-` while denying |
| `fe=` | enemy chop hits on our ring, cumulative over the game |
| `fw=` | our own ring work events, cumulative over the game |
| `fE=` | **window** enemy hits `fe(60)` **at the latch turn**, frozen there; `-` until the latch fires |
| `fW=` | **window** own ring work `fw(60)` **at the latch turn**, frozen there; `-` until the latch fires |

**Round-2 correction (defect 2).** Round 1 claimed that "`fl`, `fe` and `fw` together make the
latch auditable from the wire alone". They do not, and the claim is withdrawn. `fe` and `fw` at
game end are **cumulative over 300 turns**; the rule of §4 reads a 60-turn window; events from
before that window are mixed into the cumulative pair and cannot be subtracted out of it. A
reviewer given only the cumulative counters cannot recompute what the rule saw at turn `fl`.

`fE` and `fW` repair that: they are the two window values **at the moment the latch fired**,
written once and never updated. Gate L4 of §9 recomputes `fE > 2.0 · fW`, `fW >= 6` and
`fE + fW >= 12` for every game with `fl > 0`, and the panel fails on any game where the frozen
snapshot does not satisfy the rule that is supposed to have fired. The `M = 15` persistence
requirement is not recomputable from a single snapshot; it is checked instead against the panel's
own per-turn trace, which the local panel has and the wire does not.

That is nine tokens, not seven. **The width claim is now measured rather than asserted**
(`group_width()` in the decoder): the group is **exactly 44 characters while every value is a
single digit**, and **56 characters** at the widest values a 300-turn game can produce — `fl` up to
three digits, `fp`, `fh`, `fe`, `fw` up to three, the two window fields up to two. 44 is the floor,
not the ceiling, and round 2's flat "≤ 44 characters" was the floor quoted as a bound. It stays
first for the reason above, and the panel census records the realised payload maximum so the number
is corrected from the run rather than from this estimate. It stays first, before every v6
token, for the reason above: if the tail is cut, the farm readout still comes home. Two of the nine
carry `-` for the whole of a game in which the latch never fires, which is the common case.
