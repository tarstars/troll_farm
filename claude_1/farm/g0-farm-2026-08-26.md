## 0. What this packet decides

The contract §6 leaves five things open and the card makes them the deliverable:

1. the denial **aim-selection** rule — which trees we chop to starve the enemy's next troll;
2. the **latch threshold** — the number at which planting stops forever;
3. the **denial round criterion K** — how many rounds of "the aim count did not fall" ends denial;
4. whether the **capacity-2 second troll** is trained before the farm starts;
5. the exact **state machine** TRAIN → DENY(+mothers) → FARM → WOOD, its one-way edges, and the
   observable that fires each edge.

It also pre-commits the panel gates (§9) and names the telemetry (§8).

## 1. Five measured facts that change the contract

The contract asked for the latch and K to be calibrated from the turn corpus
`data/processed/turns.jsonl.gz`. **That corpus cannot answer either question**, for a stated
reason: it keeps the commands a bot issued and drops the board, so hut coordinates — and therefore
the ring — are not reconstructible from it. codex_1 recorded the same limit in
`codex_1/top10/field-comparison-2026-08-26.md` §4 ("unavailable: shack/state coordinates absent").

The **raw replays** answer both. `data/raw/games/*.json` carry (a) a setup frame whose map marks
both huts, `0` and `1`, so the ring is exact, and (b) a per-frame `summary` of the referee's
**accepted** events, so a plant, a harvest or a chop is a fact and not a command that may have been
refused. Troll cells come from the accepted `moved to (x, y)` lines. `claude_1/farm/ring_pressure.py`
does this; it read all 290 replays, 580 game-seats, **0 unattributed events**.

The five facts:

### 1.1 A game is 300 turns, not 200

The contract §1 ends "Game length 200 turns." Measured over all 290 replays: 301 keyframes
(the setup frame plus turns 1–300) in **266 of 290** games; the minimum is 49 (an early stall) and
**92% run the full length**. The champion's own endgame test is `view.turn > 250`
(`readable/door1-champion.rs:2061`), which is only sensible against a 300-turn game.

This is not a footnote. It is the single most favourable fact for a farm: a seed planted at turn 30
reaches size 4 at turn ~54 and is chopped for 16 points with **246 turns still to run**. A plot can
turn over eight to ten times in a game. Every timing in this packet is stated against 300.

### 1.2 Wood fills a troll, whatever its capacity

`docs/mechanics.md:61–62` — "**Wood = 1 fruit then full**". One unit of wood occupies the whole
carry. So carry capacity buys nothing at all for the wood leg of the loop; it buys only the fruit
leg (carry a seed out and a banana back). This is the fact that decides §6.

### 1.3 Two PLANT commands of different types in the same turn cancel each other

`docs/mechanics.md:95` — "Mixed-type simultaneous PLANT commands cancel without planting." With two
trolls planting, that is a live failure mode: both plants are lost and no error is raised that we
would notice. **Design invariant P (§7)**: in any turn in which our two trolls both plant, they plant
the same species. The farm plants only bananas, so the invariant binds only across the farm and the
champion's existing regeneration planting, which can plant another fruit. *Ask to codex_1 (§11 Q1):
confirm from the referee source whether "simultaneous" means same-turn or same-cell. The invariant
above is safe under either reading, so the build does not wait on the answer.*

### 1.4 The ring is not eight cells; sometimes it is three

The contract's geometry — 4 orthogonal plots, 4 diagonal mothers — assumes eight walkable ring
cells. Measured over 580 game-seats:

| walkable cells | orthogonal (plots) | diagonal (mothers) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 49.0% | 43.8% |
| 3 | 34.8% | 28.6% |
| 2 | 12.4% | 20.7% |
| 1 | 3.8% | 6.9% |
| 0 | 0% | 0% |

Mean ring size 6.38 of 8. **6.9% of seats can hold at most one mother** and 3.8% at most one plot;
water takes one or more ring cells in 35% of seats. The design therefore states the ring as
"up to 4 plots, up to 4 mothers, degrade to what exists, never fewer than zero", and — decisive —
**when only one orthogonal cell is walkable, that cell is the shack's only door and is never
planted** (§2.4). A farm that assumes eight cells strands a troll on one seat in twenty-five.

### 1.5 The leaders' ring is under real but modest enemy pressure — and that is the latch number

Referee-accepted events per game-seat, by 50-turn bucket, over 300 turns.
"my ring work" = our own accepted harvests and chops on our own ring cells.
"foe chops my ring" = the opponent's accepted chops standing on a cell of *our* ring.

| group | n | median first plant | plants/50t | foe chops my ring /50t | my ring work /50t |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| leaders (goq, yaichi, Stounate) | 37 | turn **15** | 4.0 5.4 4.8 5.6 6.4 4.0 | 1.8 3.4 3.8 1.8 1.0 1.3 | 2.4 7.6 9.8 8.8 7.6 10.0 |
| field (36 other bots) | 402 | turn 13.5 | 2.8 2.4 3.1 3.7 4.0 3.4 | 3.1 2.5 2.7 1.9 1.6 1.5 | 3.0 7.5 10.4 12.1 12.3 12.7 |
| `tass` in this sample (agent 6536563, ladder score ≈ 11) | 141 | turn 23 | 3.8 5.0 3.9 3.5 3.5 2.2 | 0.8 3.0 4.5 4.2 5.4 5.2 | 6.2 15.7 15.9 17.2 16.2 15.7 |

The ratio that matters — foe chops on my ring, divided by my own work on my ring, over the whole
game:

| group | min | lower quartile | **median** | upper quartile | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| leaders | 0.00 | 0.05 | **0.21** | 0.56 | 2.83 |

**The leaders operate at a ratio near 0.2 and tolerate 0.56 without stopping. The distribution has
a long tail: the worst seats run at 3 to 16.** That tail is the shape of the August-2 disaster —
"wins narrowly, loses catastrophically", worst game −348. The latch exists to cut the tail and
nothing else. §4 sets it at 1.0 on a windowed measurement, which is above every leader's normal
operating point and below the tail.

*Sample honesty, stated once and not repeated: 37 leader game-seats and 141 `tass` game-seats out
of 580. The `tass` rows here are agent 6536563 at ladder score ≈ 11, **not** the current champion
and **not** the b100 (agent 6553250); they are used only as a same-referee reference for the ratio,
never as a claim about our bot. The buckets are stable across the three groups, but a 37-seat
leader sample sets a threshold's order of magnitude, not its third digit. The threshold is chosen
to be insensitive to that: it sits at 5× the leader median.*

## 2. The state machine

Four states, three edges, **every edge one-way**. The bot is in exactly one state; the state is a
single field on `YamoBot` and the only writer is the transition test in `commands()`, run once per
turn before any candidate is built.

```
  TRAIN ──e1──> DENY ──e2──> FARM ──e3──> WOOD
   (no banana action)  (+mothers only)   (plots+mothers)  (no planting ever again)
```

There is no path back from any state. The latch of owner decision 2 is the edge **e3**; once WOOD
is entered the bot plants nothing for the rest of the game except the late wave of §2.5, which is
not farming and is explicitly permitted (contract §9).

### 2.0 TRAIN — no banana action of any kind

Entered at turn 1. The bot is byte-for-byte the champion. The owner's standing rule of 2026-08-10
("no banana action of any kind before the second troll is trained", no exceptions) is enforced
here by construction: the farm code is not reached.

**e1, TRAIN → DENY.** Observable: our own troll count reaches 2. Fires the same turn the referee
accepts the TRAIN. Our bot trains at turn ≈ 9 (codex_1's field table); the leaders' median first
plant is turn 15 (§1.5). **The standing rule costs us nothing**: planting from turn 10 is earlier
than the leaders plant, so the rule and the evidence do not conflict and no departure is needed.

### 2.1 DENY — chop the aim, plant mothers only

Denial is income, not sacrifice: the wood from a felled enemy tree is ours at 4 points a unit
(contract §1). The aim rule is §3. During DENY, **the second troll may plant mothers on diagonal
ring cells and nothing else may be planted** (owner decision 3). The reason is arithmetic: a mother
needs 24 turns to reach size 4 and ~30 turns to its first fruit; a mother planted at turn 10 is
producing seeds at turn 40, a mother planted after a denial that ends at turn 90 is producing at
turn 120 — a third of the game gone.

**e2, DENY → FARM.** Fires on the first of the contract's four, exactly one of which is recorded:

| code | condition | observable |
|---|---|---|
| `a` | the aim trees are all felled | no plant of the aim species remains on the enemy's half |
| `b` | reproduction | K consecutive denial rounds in which the aim count did not fall (§5) |
| `c` | the enemy trains a third troll | opponent unit count reaches 3 |
| `d` | no aim tree is left in reach | no aim tree is reachable by our BFS from either troll |

A **hard deadline** is added (§10 departure D3): if no condition has fired by turn **120**, e2
fires with reason `t`. The contract has no deadline and the owner accepted the risk of an
incomplete denial; a bot with no deadline can spend the whole 300 turns denying and never farm at
all, which is the failure the contract is written to avoid.

### 2.2 FARM — the loop

Per turn, the farm proposes candidates into the champion's existing scoring pool. It never
overrides the champion; it competes.

- **Plots** (orthogonal ring cells, up to 4, minus the door exclusion of §2.4): if empty, plant a
  banana; if size 4, chop it; if growing, leave it. A banana at size 4 has health 2 + 4 = 6, so six
  hits at chop power 1, and yields 4 wood = **16 points from one seed**.
- **Mothers** (diagonal ring cells, up to 4, at least 2 where the map allows; §1.4 says 6.9% of
  seats allow only one): plant, grow to size 4, then **harvest and never chop**. A size-4 banana
  makes one fruit per 6 turns (4 next to water) and stores 3.
- **Seed routing**: a harvested banana goes to an empty plot if one exists, otherwise to the shack
  as 1 point and as reserve for the late wave. The 2026-08-04 defect — "harvested bananas are not
  being collected to the tent" — is gate V6 of §9.
- **Mothers sit away from the enemy** where the map offers a choice: of the walkable diagonals,
  prefer those whose BFS distance to the enemy hut is greatest ("do not create fruit the opponent
  can harvest before us", owner 2026-08-02).
- Water adjacency is a tie-break inside plots and inside mothers, never a siting rule that moves the
  farm off the ring (contract §6).

**e3, FARM → WOOD.** The latch. §4.

### 2.3 WOOD — no planting, ever again

The champion's ordinary wood behaviour, unmodified: chop the best trees in reach, ours and theirs.
Standing ring trees are chopped as any other tree. This state is the champion.

### 2.4 The door exclusion

The shack cell is not walkable; a troll DROPs from an orthogonally adjacent cell. Where **only one**
orthogonal ring cell is walkable (3.8% of seats, §1.4) that cell is the shack's only door and is
**never planted**. A tree cell is walkable, so a planted door does not block movement — but a troll
standing on a tree to harvest it cannot move that turn, and the champion already carries
`force_unique_door_clear` machinery precisely because a single-door shack is fragile. The farm does
not add pressure there. Where two or more orthogonal cells are walkable, all are plots.

### 2.5 The late wave

Unchanged from the champion, which already implements it (`conversion_chop_turns`,
`endgame_candidates`, `readable/door1-champion.rs:1846`): plant every held banana while a tree can
still reach size ≥ 1 before the end and chop it, bank what cannot mature. The farm does not touch
this code. Against a 300-turn game the champion's `turn > 250` endgame gate is the right one and is
left alone.

## 3. The aim-selection rule (contract §6, open item 1)

**Aim = the species that funds the enemy's next troll, resolved by cost, then by scarcity, then by
distance.**

Training the *n*-th troll costs, per talent, `n + talent²` of one fruit: plums for speed, lemons for
capacity, apples for harvest power, iron for chop power (`docs/mechanics.md:90–92`). Bananas and
wood are never a training cost — **denying bananas denies nothing**. So:

1. **Candidate species** = {plum, lemon, apple}. Bananas are never an aim. Iron is not a tree.
2. **Choose the species the enemy is shortest of, relative to a third troll's cost.** We can see the
   opponent's shack inventory in the view (`view.inventories[1]`). For each species *s*, let
   `need(s) = cost_of_third_troll(s) − inventory[1][s]`, clipped at 0, where the third troll's cost
   is evaluated at the enemy's cheapest plausible stat line (the fallback line the champion uses for
   its own second troll). Aim at `argmax need(s)`; ties broken by the smaller standing count of
   that species on the enemy's half.
3. **Restrict to reach**: only trees of the aim species whose BFS distance from our troll is finite
   and which are at least as close to the **enemy** hut as to ours — "near the enemy's hut" made
   exact, and it is exactly the complement of the geometry the superseded Spec A used for our own
   side (2026-08-15).
4. **Recompute the aim at most once per denial round** (§5), never per turn. Per-turn recomputation
   is the A→B→A loop the worker rules of contract §3 forbid; the sticky aim is the fix.

The champion's existing `type_to_cut` (`focus_type`, `:553`) is **not** this and is not reused: it
picks lemon-or-plum by distance from *our* hut to feed *our* training. The aim is a second,
separate field. *Departure D1 in §10 records that the contract's "the species the enemy needs for
its next troll (or the species it has fewest of)" is made a single ordered rule rather than a
choice, because a bot cannot execute "or".*

## 4. The latch (contract §6, open item 2; owner decision 2)

**Observable** — both terms referee-accepted and both computable in-game from the view, no memory
of the map required beyond the ring:

- `foe_ring_hits(w)` = chop hits landed by enemy trolls standing on a cell of our ring, over the
  last `w` turns. In-game this is counted as: an enemy unit stands on one of our ring cells and the
  plant on that cell lost health this turn.
- `own_ring_work(w)` = our own accepted harvests and chops on our ring cells over the same window.

**Rule.** The latch fires — once, irreversibly — on the first turn at which

```
    own_ring_work(w) + foe_ring_hits(w) >= N        (enough evidence to judge)
and foe_ring_hits(w)  >  own_ring_work(w)           (the farm feeds them more than us)
```

with **w = 60 turns** and **N = 8 events**.

**Why those numbers.** The threshold ratio is 1.0. The leaders run at a whole-game median of
**0.21** and a upper quartile of **0.56** (§1.5); 1.0 is 5× the leaders' median, so a bot behaving
like a leader never latches. The tail runs at 2.8 to 16, and latches. `w = 60` is one fifth of a
300-turn game and twice the ~30-turn maturation of a mother, so the window covers at least one full
crop generation and cannot be tripped by a single unlucky turn. `N = 8` is chosen from the bucket
table: the leaders accumulate 7.6–10 own ring events per 50 turns from bucket 2 onward, so `N = 8`
is reached in a normal 60-turn window and the latch is not being asked to judge on two events. Before
`N` is reached the latch cannot fire at all, which by construction protects the opening.

**Faithfulness to the owner's criterion.** The owner's words are "it lowers the enemy's moves per
wood unit". Enemy chop hits on our ring is that quantity's numerator: every hit is enemy work spent
on wood our seed grew, on a cell one step from our own shack, so their travel cost is the cost of a
tree they did not have to plant or wait for. When they land more of those hits than we land our own
harvests and chops there, the ring is a better deal for them than for us. `foe_ring_hits` and
`own_ring_work` are both counted in accepted events, so the ratio is not inflated by commands the
referee refused.

**Once and never reset.** The state is a single `bool`; the only writer is the transition test; the
test is `if !latched && …`. Gate L1 of §9 measures this on the panel.

## 5. The denial round criterion K (contract §6, open item 3)

A **round** is: "we felled every aim tree we could see and reach". Concretely — the round opens
when the aim is (re)selected, and closes on the first turn at which no aim tree is both alive and
reachable *or* 40 turns have passed, whichever is first. At each close we record the count of aim
trees on the enemy's half. The count is compared with the previous round's.

**K = 2.** Denial ends with reason `b` after **two consecutive rounds whose closing aim count did not
fall below the previous round's**.

**Why 2 and not 3.** The enemy replants. Measured: accepted plants within Chebyshev distance 2 of
the planter's own hut, per game-seat over 300 turns — leaders median **29** (quartiles 21–35, n=37),
the rest of the field median **14** (quartiles 5–25, n=402). That is one new tree near their shack
every **10 turns** against a leader and every **21** against the field, and the field's planting
rate per 50 turns barely declines across the whole game (2.8 → 3.4, §1.5). A denial round takes 20–40 turns (travel plus six chop hits
for a banana, twenty for an apple), so **one to two replacement trees appear inside every round we
run**. Against a planting opponent the aim count is a treadmill, and a K of 3 spends 60 to 120 turns
— a fifth to two fifths of the game — proving something two rounds already showed. K = 2 with the
turn-120 deadline of §2.1 bounds denial at 120 turns in the worst case and typically ends it near
turn 60–90, which puts the farm in production while five sixths of the game remains.

## 6. Is the capacity-2 troll trained first? (contract §6, open item 4)

**No change to the champion's training choice. The farm does not ask for capacity 2 and must not.**

The contract §2 prefers capacity 2 because "it carries a seed and a banana to bank in one trip".
That is true for the fruit leg. But §1.2 measured the other leg: **wood fills a troll whatever its
capacity** (`docs/mechanics.md:61–62`, "Wood = 1 fruit then full"). The farm's income is wood — 4
points a unit against 1 for a banked banana — so the leg that capacity would help is the minor one,
and a plot is one or two steps from the shack by construction, so the trip it saves is one turn.

Against that, capacity 2 costs 5 lemons + 2 plums + 2 apples + 2 iron, and the champion's opening
policy (`YamoOpeningPolicy::TUNED_CARRY`, `:504`) already searches the stat line by estimated time
to afford it and abandons a line that is more than 15 turns of extra ETA. Forcing capacity 2 would
overrule a tuned, measured opening for a one-turn saving on the minor leg. **The farm therefore
takes whatever second troll the champion's opening policy chooses**, and the panel reports the
realised stat line so the question can be reopened with evidence rather than preference. This is
departure D2 in §10.

## 7. The worker rules of contract §3, as measured tests

The contract states four rules that "every earlier attempt broke" and requires them as measured
tests, not assumptions. Two are already structurally guaranteed by the champion; two are new work.
Each has a gate in §9.

| # | rule | status in the champion | what the farm must do |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | a troll carrying wood keeps going until it drops it or loses it | not guaranteed | the farm proposes **no candidate at all** for a troll whose carry is non-empty and whose current target is the shack; the farm's candidate list for that troll is empty, so the champion's banking candidate wins uncontested |
| W2 | trolls never chase each other's occupied tree or cell | **guaranteed**: `MoisanBot::compatible` (`:912`) rejects any pair of candidates whose targets are the same cell, and `select` (`:937`) only ever returns a compatible pair | the farm emits its candidates with a proper `Target::Cell`/`Target::Tree`, never `Target::None`, so the existing guarantee covers it. A farm candidate with `Target::None` is a defect |
| W3 | target choice is sticky enough to prevent A→B→A loops | Candidate 3's keep rule addresses this and is **not** in the farm arm (`KEEP_RULE_ENABLED=false`) | the farm supplies its own stickiness at the level it owns: the aim is recomputed at most once per round (§3.4), and a plot or mother assignment, once made for a troll, is held until the cell's job is done or the cell dies |
| W4 | two of our trolls never plan the same ring cell for the same turn | same guarantee as W2 | same as W2 |
| P | two same-turn PLANTs are the same species (§1.3) | not guaranteed — the champion can plant a regeneration fruit | the farm strips any non-banana PLANT from the other troll's candidate list on a turn in which a farm PLANT is selected; if that is not expressible in the selection pass, the farm withdraws its own PLANT instead. Never both |

## 8. The telemetry: v7 = v6 plus one farm group, placed first

The build extends the diagnostic line the contract §5 requires ("plants, mother harvests, latch
turn, denial end reason, enemy chops on the ring"). Two constraints shape it.

**The wire budget.** The v6 payload is already up to **328 characters a turn**, and across the 290
collected games the longest `MSG` the platform ever returned is **127**
(`claude_1/instrument6/wire_budget.py`). Whether the platform truncates is untested, not disproved.
Therefore the farm group is **short and placed immediately after `t=`**, before every v6 token, so
that if the tail is cut the farm readout still comes home. This is a design consequence of an open
risk, not a claim that truncation happens.

**The decoder contract.** `claude_1/narrate6/narrate6.py` asserts at import that no grammar field is
without a consumer and no consumed field is off the grammar (r6 C3). Adding fields to v6 breaks that
assertion, so the farm arm emits **`NARRATE v7`** with its own decoder `claude_1/narrate7/narrate7.py`
carrying the same assertion. v6 stays exactly as it is; L-1's bots are untouched.

The farm group, seven tokens, ≤ 34 characters:

| token | meaning |
|---|---|
| `fs=` | state: `0` TRAIN, `1` DENY, `2` FARM, `3` WOOD |
| `fp=` | accepted farm plants so far this game |
| `fh=` | accepted mother harvests so far |
| `fl=` | the turn the latch fired, `0` if it has not |
| `fd=` | denial end reason, one of `a b c d t`, `-` while denying |
| `fe=` | enemy chop hits on our ring, cumulative |
| `fw=` | our own ring work events, cumulative |

`fl`, `fe` and `fw` together make the latch auditable from the wire alone: the reviewer can
recompute the rule of §4 from the counters and check the turn it fired.

## 9. Panel gates, pre-committed

One local panel: 240 games + 34 fixtures, three arms — **farm-off** (base), **farm-on**,
**instrument** (farm-on + v7) — configured as `claude_1/cure2/cure2-ruleoff-config.json` but for
the arm, games directory and task, so the existing matched floor stays valid. Probe arms are
stdout-parity-gated byte-identical to their own arms on both seats before any output is read.

**Validity first. Every V gate is go/no-go for the ladder slot. A single failure is a BLOCK on my
own arm and the slot is not used.**

| gate | statement | failure |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Containment**: farm-off is byte-identical in play to the champion on all 240 games, with `fs=0` and `fp=0` on every turn | any counterexample |
| V1 | no new blocked game — the count of games ending in a referee block is not above farm-off's | any increase |
| V2 | no no-progress turn introduced — the count of turns in which one of our trolls emits `WAIT` while a farm candidate was available is 0 | any occurrence |
| V3 | **W1**: no game in which a troll carrying wood changes target away from the shack before dropping it | any occurrence |
| V4 | **W2/W4**: no turn in which our two trolls' selected commands name the same cell | any occurrence |
| V5 | **W3**: no A→B→A target sequence on any troll within 6 turns | any occurrence |
| V6 | **the 2026-08-04 defect**: mother harvests that were not routed to a plot are banked; count of bananas harvested minus (plot plants + banked) is 0 at game end, carry allowed for | any nonzero residue |
| V7 | **P**: no turn with two selected PLANT commands of different species | any occurrence |
| L1 | the latch fires at most once per game and `fl` never returns to 0 | any reset |
| L2 | denial ends by exactly one of `a b c d t` and `fd` is written exactly once | any second write |
| L3 | the latch never fires before `N = 8` ring events are in the window | any early fire |
| R1 | the ring degrades: on seats with fewer than 4 walkable orthogonal cells the farm plants only walkable cells, and on a single-door seat it plants none of them | any plant on a non-walkable or single-door cell |

**Value, reported and never a verdict.** Own-score delta against farm-off, every changed game named
with its own delta; the realised second-troll stat line (§6); the distributions of `fl`, `fd`, and
of `fe/fw` at game end against the leaders' 0.21 median of §1.5. The value number is a go/no-go for
the ladder slot, never a promotion. The ladder is the judge (contract §4).

**Pre-registered expectations, written so they can be wrong.**

1. **More than half of 240 games change.** The farm acts from turn ~10 onward on every map.
2. **Own score on the panel goes down, not up, by 5 to 20 points a game.** The local bench
   overstated the 2026-08-02 farm by about 10 ladder points (contract §4) and the farm spends early
   turns on seeds that pay after turn 60. If the panel shows a *large* gain I will look for a
   containment bug before celebrating.
3. **The latch fires in 10–20% of games**, concentrated where the opponent is a heavy chopper —
   that is the tail of §1.5, which is what it is for. If it fires in more than half the games the
   threshold is wrong and the packet is at fault, not the build.
4. **`fe/fw` at game end lands near the leaders' 0.2–0.6**, because the ring geometry and the crop
   are the leaders'.

## 10. Departures from the contract, named

| # | contract says | this packet says | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | aim = the species the enemy needs "or the species it has fewest of" | one ordered rule: cost-shortfall, then standing count, then reach (§3) | a bot cannot execute "or"; the two readings disagree on real boards |
