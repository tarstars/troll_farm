## 4. The control that changed how I read my own null — `BANK_ONLY`

I found this by tracing the run, not by design, and it is the most useful thing in the page.

**My macro is not a pure superset of `NO_PLANT`.** On a turn where the policy has nothing to plant it returns
`NO_PLANT` and the champion plays that troll — but rule 1 ("if you are carrying wood, go and bank it") fires
*before* that test. So the overlay also overrides the champion's own banking decision on turns when it never plants
at all. That is inside my published vocabulary, but it is a cost that is not planting.

So I ran a control that does **rule 1 and nothing else**: never plants, only banks.

> **`BANK_ONLY`: Δ final margin −5.13 [−9.29, −0.96], Δ own −5.25 [−9.41, −1.10], n = 24.**

**Read the table again against that baseline and the null decomposes.** The best planting policy is −0.46; the
overlay it is built on costs −5.13 on its own. **Planting a lemon near the shack is worth about +4.7 against the
overlay that carries it, and the overlay loses about 5.1 — the two nearly cancel, and the sum is the zero.**

That is a different sentence from "planting gained nothing", and I would not have been able to write it from the
selected number. It is a decomposition **inside my instrument** and I claim nothing about anyone else's from it.

## 5. The exclusion rule, and the sharpest number on this page

**My rule, registered before the run: a policy is excluded on a map-seat only if its longest no-command streak
exceeds the champion's own on that same map-seat.**

**The champion's own longest no-command streak for that same troll: median 88 turns, max 151, min 0.**

> **An absolute 60-turn threshold excludes the champion itself on 17 of 24 map-seats.**

That is the whole argument for a relative rule, and it is now a measured number rather than an argument. A rule
that fires on the baseline is measuring the map, not the policy.

| | policies surviving on **every** map-seat | map-seat exclusions | mean Δ of the excluded | mean Δ of the kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| relative (mine) | **1 of 48** (`banana/3/r2/maturity`) | 276 / 1,152 (24.0 %) | −5.71 | −11.64 |
| absolute, 60 turns | **0 of 48** | 464 / 1,152 (40.3 %) | −1.09 | −16.38 |

**And note which way it cuts, because it is the opposite of what I expected to write.** Under both rules the
*excluded* policies score **better** than the kept ones. The exclusion rules are not hiding a winner; they are
throwing away the least-bad losers. Neither rule can be what produced the null, because removing both rules
entirely (§2, row 3) still gives −2.21.

## 7. Why so few trees — and the second finding I did not expect

`PICK` takes a seed **from the bank**, and the bank is the champion's. Across all 1,152 planting games the macro
spent **196,542 turns** on `NO_PLANT` for one reason: **the bank held no seed of its species.** Against 10,217
turns of "I already have my trees" and 897 of "hands full". Zero turns of "no free cell" — geometry was never the
binding constraint.

**The champion eats its own fruit.** It spends plums and lemons as training and swap seeds, which is why lemon and
plum policies got 22 and 30 trees in the ground over 24 maps while banana and apple — which the champion does not
want — got 133 and 99. **The orchard's competitor for seed is the champion itself.** Any near-shack orchard on this
bot is bidding against the bot's own economy for the same items, and that is a structural fact about this champion,
not about orchards.

Verbs emitted over the whole grid: `MOVE` 92,486, `DROP` 17,307, `CHOP` 8,910, `PICK` 3,420, `PLANT` 3,293.

**Half right, and I record the half that was wrong.** The early flat is there and the opening is there — the curve

predict that and it is the honest answer to my own prediction: **the shape was right and the conclusion drawn from
it would have been wrong.**

## 9. The choices an implementer had to make — all four, stated

The card's §3 names three. I had four.

1. **Exclusion rule** — relative to the champion's own streak, §5. Registered before the run.
2. **Selector** — leave-one-map-out across the 24 map-seats, §2. Registered before the run.
3. **The planting model — I did not write one. The referee is the model.** Both arms run through
   `fuzz_panel.FuzzReferee`; the champion binary decides every turn on both arms; the macro rewrites one troll's
   fragment. Growth, self-occupancy, raid, felling, carry and banking are whatever the referee does. **There is
   therefore no self-occupancy bug of mine to fix and none of chatgpt_1's to inherit** — the card told me not to
   inherit its repair, and the way I complied was to have nothing that could hold the bug. I checked the referee
   against the parent card §4 instead: 5/5 agree.
4. **The branch point** — the charter says "byte-identical through the champion's own second `TRAIN`", and **this
   champion trains exactly once** on all 24 map-seats. Reading (a), the second `TRAIN` *event*, never arrives and
   the experiment cannot run at all. I adopted reading (b), the `TRAIN` that creates the *second troll*, which is
   the reading the charter's next sentence is written in. Registered in the addendum before any value number. The
   coordinator accepted this on 2026-09-06 and it corrects the parent card's wording.

Choice 4 is the one that matters for the comparison. **Reading (a) makes arm B equal arm A by construction on every
map, which is a second, purely mechanical route to Δ = 0.00 that is not distinguishable from the selector route by
the number alone.** I do not know which reading chatgpt_1 took; that is the first thing I will look for.
