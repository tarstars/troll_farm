# Orchard kinetics — progress at 2026-09-04, and the premise of the card is now measured

Task `20260904-orchard-kinetics`. Budget 2026-09-06 06:00Z. This is a progress report, not the
final one: questions 1, 3-value and 4 are answered, question 2 is answered *analytically* from a
measurement that was missing until today, and the paired comparison the card's amendment asks for
is named as the remaining work.

Everything below is referee-measured. `kinetics.py` agrees with `sim/engine.py` tick by tick on
8 species×cell timelines over 120 turns; every cycle in `value.py` is driven through the referee
and its turns and points are read off the referee's own inventory; the forest census replays the
collected 160-game champion corpus through the exact reconstructor.

---

## 0. The headline, in one paragraph

**The owner's reading of the wood-charging gate's death — "there is nothing left for the third
troll to cut" — is half right, and the half that is wrong is the half that matters.** At the
median turn the third troll arrives (108) there are still **6 wild trees standing on our reachable
side carrying 24 wood, 96 points**. What is gone is not the wood: it is the *near* wood. **Zero of
those trees are within 4 steps of the door from turn 75 onward**, and the survivors sit at a median
door-distance of **13**. The troll's problem is not scarcity, it is walking. And that is exactly
the defect an orchard fixes, because an orchard is planted where you want it. Measured on the
referee, a chop-3 carry-4 worker earns **0.5 points a turn** felling a tree 13 steps out and
**1.7–3.2 points a turn** felling one at the door. **So the card's dead-on-paper condition is not
met: an orchard does put more convertible wood in front of a turn-100 troll than the wild forest
does — not more wood, but wood at about three times the rate per worker-turn.**

## 2. The value of a planting turn against a chopping turn — the card's new reporting line

`value.py` → `results/value.json`, `results/fell_on_maturity.json`, `results/cycle_by_distance.json`.
The scale is **points per worker-turn**, the only scale on which `PLANT` can compete inside a
searched action space. A banked fruit is 1 point, a banked wood 4 (`sim/engine.py`).

**Referee-measured cycles**, one worker from the shack door, whole round trip including the DROP:

| cycle | turns | points | per turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| CHOP a mature banana at the door, chop 3, carry 4 | 5 | 16 | **3.20** |
| CHOP a mature plum/lemon at the door, chop 3, carry 4 | 7 | 16 | **2.29** |
| CHOP a mature apple at the door, chop 3, carry 4 | 10 | 16 | **1.60** |
| the same banana with carry 1 (the starter) | 5 | 4 | 0.80 |
| HARVEST a full tree at the door, hp 3 | 4 | 3 | **0.75** |
| HARVEST a full tree at the door, hp 1 | 4 | 1 | 0.25 |
| PLANT one seed at the door | 5 | **−1** | deferred |

(The PLANT row is measured from the shack itself, so it carries one step the CHOP and HARVEST rows —
which are measured door to door — do not. Door to door the plant cycle is 4 turns at distance 1, and
that is the figure used in the chain arithmetic below.)

Two things fall out of that table immediately and neither was in the record:

- **Carry, not chop, is what a wood cycle turns on.** The same banana is 3.20 points a turn to a
  carry-4 troll and 0.80 to a carry-1 one, because a size-4 tree drops 4 wood and the starter can
  bring one home. **A chop upgrade on a carry-1 troll buys almost nothing**, which is a plain
  explanation for both optimizers picking small trolls that no post-mortem has offered.
- **A planting turn costs a point before it earns one**: PICK spends a fruit from the bank, so the
  plant cycle is −1 point and 5 turns before the tree exists.

**The whole chain, plant → grow → fell, at the raid survival the record measures**
(`fell_on_maturity.json`, 40 map-seats, plant at turn 10 on the nearest free cell, fell the moment
the tree is full so the hazard only runs over the growing window):

| species | survival to the felling turn | points per worker-turn | against the best *standing wild* tree |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BANANA** | 0.971 | **1.73** | **0.97×** |
| PLUM / LEMON | 0.969 | 1.41 | 0.78× |
| APPLE | 0.969 | 1.11 | 0.61× |

**So while a wild tree is still standing near you, planting never beats chopping it — banana ties
it and everything else loses.** That is the honest answer to "is planting worth a turn", and it is
why an orchard is not a value engine. **It is a reserve.** Its whole worth is that it exists at a
distance of 1 when the wild forest at distance 1 is gone.

**And the cost of distance, measured on the referee** (`cycle_by_distance.json`, chop 3, carry 4):

| door-distance | banana | plum | apple |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3.20 | 2.29 | 1.60 |
| 2 | 2.29 | 1.78 | 1.33 |
| 4 | 1.46 | 1.23 | 1.00 |
| 8 | 0.84 | 0.76 | 0.67 |
| 12 | 0.59 | 0.55 | 0.50 |
| 16 | 0.46 | 0.43 | 0.40 |

## 3. The measurement the card was missing — the forest at the turn the troll arrives

`forest_census.py` → `results/forest-census-champ.json`. The champion of record's **160 collected
ladder games** (`41234663`, agent 6693889), every game replayed through the exact reconstructor so
each turn carries the real board; all 160 fit, none dropped. A tree is *wild* if it stands at turn 1
and is counted dead the first turn its cell is empty, never counted again. Everything is restricted
to the cells our shack can reach.

| turn | wild trees | wild wood | median door-distance | nearest | trees within 4 steps | wood within 4 | all trees reachable | games with no wild left |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 16 | 50 | 8 | 1 | **4** | **13** | 16 | 0 |
| 50 | 11 | 44 | 10 | 4 | 1 | 4 | 13 | 0 |
| 75 | 9 | 36 | 12 | 6 | **0** | **0** | 11 | 0 |
| 100 | 7 | 28 | 12 | 7 | 0 | 0 | 10 | 4 |
| **108** | **6** | **24** | **13** | **7** | **0** | **0** | 10 | 6 |
| 150 | 4 | 16 | 13 | 9 | 0 | 0 | 7.5 | 16 |
| 200 | 2 | 8 | 13 | 9 | 0 | 0 | 6 | 27 |
| 300 | 2 | 8 | 14 | 10 | 0 | 0 | 6 | 30 |

**Three findings, and the first two are new.**

1. **The forest is not empty at turn 108 — it is far.** 24 wood, 96 points, still standing in the
   median game, in 154 of 160 games. But the nearest is 7 steps out and the median 13.
2. **The near forest is exhausted by turn 75, and it was never big:** 4 trees and 13 wood within
   four steps at turn 1, 1 tree by turn 50, none by turn 75. **The resource an orchard would
   replace is worth 13 wood — 52 points — and it lasts 75 turns.**
3. At turn 108 there are about **4 non-wild trees** on our reachable side (10 standing against 6
   wild), which is the champion's own 9.8 plantings and the opponent's, partly felled already.

**Put beside section 2's distance table, this is the read's answer to question 2.** A third troll
arriving at turn 108 into the wild forest is walking 13 steps for its wood — **about 0.5 points a
worker-turn**. The same troll with a banana orchard at the door earns **1.7 points a turn including
the planting cost and the raid loss, and 3.2 on a tree that is already grown.** That is roughly a
**three-fold** difference, and it is the first positive number this line has produced.

## 4. Question 4 — what it costs

`orchard_budget` in `value.py`, 20 map-seats, plant k from turn 2 with the starter, fell all from
turn 200 with one chop-3 carry-4 worker, raids at the measured rate:

| species | k | plant turns | fell turns | points | trees that survive to be felled | per worker-turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BANANA | 5 | 22 | 26 | 26.5 | 1.7 | 0.56 |
| BANANA | 10 | 58 | 62 | 53.5 | 3.3 | 0.45 |
| BANANA | 20 | 166 | 106 | 77.0 | 4.8 | 0.28 |
| BANANA | 30 | 314 | 106 | 77.0 | 4.8 | 0.18 |
| PLUM | 10 | 58 | 82 | 50.7 | 3.2 | 0.36 |
| APPLE | 10 | 58 | 108 | 45.6 | 2.9 | 0.28 |

**Every row of that table is a bad trade, and the reason is in the "trees that survive" column: a
big orchard felled late is an orchard the opponent harvests for you.** k=30 delivers the same 77
points as k=20 for 148 more planting turns. **The cost is not the planting, it is the standing.**

**So the orchard's schedule is forced, and this is question 3's answer in one line: plant small,
plant near, plant bananas, and fell on maturity rather than banking the trees for the endgame.**
A banana at the door is full at +18 inland, +12 by water, and its 5-turn fell cycle at chop 3 means
one chopper can run a plant-and-fell rotation of about 9 worker-turns per 16 points — **1.78 points
a turn, sustained**, against the 0.5 the distant wild forest offers after turn 100.

**The champion's baseline on the same scale.** Its unaided 9.8 trees are 157 points of standing
wood if every one were felled and about 68 after raids to turn 200; the top four's ~29 trees are
464 and 202. But the live read measured that we take **0.03 fruit a game** from our own trees while
the opponent takes 23.5 from theirs, and the census above shows the near ground empty from turn 75.
**We are already planting a third of a useful orchard and then not using it.**

## 5. What is not yet answered, and what I intend to do next

- **The paired comparison the amendment requires** — best turn-300 value with `PLANT` and `TRAIN`
  against the same action space with `TRAIN` disabled, under identical opponent scenarios — is not
  done. Everything above is a rate comparison, not a paired score. **No build claim can rest on it
  yet, and I make none.**
- The rates in section 2 are one worker's cycles in isolation. A near orchard competes for the
  *same near cells* the champion's own planting uses, and the census's 4 non-wild trees at turn 108
  say some of that ground is already taken. The right next measurement is the **near-cell budget**:
  how many door-adjacent cells are free through the game, given what both sides plant.
- The raid hazard is applied per tree-turn from the record's table. At door-distance 1–2 after
  turn 150 it is 0.007–0.011, so a tree left standing 50 turns loses a third of its value. **A
  rotation that fells on maturity barely pays it; a banked orchard pays it in full.** The rotation
  claim above should be replayed end to end before anything is built on it.
- The chop loop's `"last wood can duplicate"` quirk is excluded here because every cycle measured
  is a single chopper. A multi-chopper schedule must not exclude it.
