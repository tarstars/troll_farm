## What the population is, and why it has two parts

The primary population is the **12 orchard-eligible games of the published 240-game panel** —
those are the "identical map" the control asks for, since the candidate was already measured on
them. One of the twelve fires. **One is thin**, and one is also exactly the number a control gets
when its population is too small rather than when its subject is inert, so the enlargement was
declared *before* the primary run: `cure2-c16-extension-config.json` — 48 maps of class
`orchard_eligible` only, everything else (referee, seeds, turn budget, opponent mix, liveness
window) copied unchanged from the panel config — with the pre-committed rule that it is used if
the primary produces no exchange, and that either outcome is published. It ran, and the file's
existence in the same commit is the pre-commitment.

48 maps × 2 seats produced **48 eligible seat views** — every one of them seat 0, none seat 1,
which is `fuzz_panel`'s own asymmetry: the generator's eligibility retry checks `specs[0]` only.
Combined with the panel's 12 that is 60 graded views, of which
`results/c16-population-dedup.json` shows **55 are distinct maps**: five map indices are shared
between the two configs (`m004`, `m014`, `m025`, `m035`, `m045` and their `x` twins) because the
class and the seed derivation coincide there. **All five doubled views agree field for field**,
including the one that fires — an unplanned reproduction of the firing case on a separately
generated instance of the same map.

Both halves are reported over all 60 views; `results/c16-scoping-control-primary.json` holds the
12-game primary run on its own for anyone who wants the panel population alone.

## The gates — each one aborts the run rather than degrading the number

| gate | requirement | result |
|---|---|---|
| **G-1L** one-line arms | each scoping-off arm differs from its source arm in exactly one line, and that line is the scoping flag — re-checked here from the bytes, not trusted from the manifest | **PASS** |
| **G-E** population identity | the graded games are exactly the census's `orchard_eligible` rows, and the regenerated specs agree game for game | **PASS** — 12/12 |
| **G-B** green-half identity | on the scoping-on arm every graded game reproduces its recorded census exchange count (0) *and* is byte-identical to the parent | **PASS** — 60/60 |
| **G-I** red-arm in-play identity | the narrate-on attributing arm and the narrate-off graded arm are identical in play (MSG stripped) on every graded view | **PASS** — 60/60 |
| **G-A** attribution | on every firing view the first P3 divergence turn **equals** the first turn the wire granted an exchange | **PASS** — 9/9 |
| **G-N** off-class inertness | on the non-eligible games that carry exchanges, the scoping-off arm is byte-identical to the scoping-on arm | **PASS** — 28/28 |

G-A and G-N are the two that make this a control rather than a coincidence. Without **G-A** the
red half would say only "P3 fires when the flag is off", which a flag that changed anything at all
would satisfy; with it, each fire starts on the exchange's own turn, and the exchange is visible
on the wire of an arm proven to be the same game. Without **G-N** the flag could have been
changing behaviour everywhere and the eligible class merely the place it was noticed; 28
exchange-bearing non-eligible games byte-identical between the two arms says the flag is confined
to the predicate's class.

The grading function is `fuzz_panel.eval_p3` — the panel's own, imported and called, not restated
here — with `orchard_eligible=True` supplied from the panel's own spec.

Re-running the whole thing writes a **byte-identical** `c16-scoping-control.json`.

## What this does not prove

- **Not that P3 would fire on every orchard-eligible map.** It fires on 8 of 55 distinct views.
  The other 47 produce no exchange at all with the scoping off — on those maps the scoping costs
  nothing because there was nothing to scope. Five near-misses are on the wire across the whole
  red half (`sn=4` non-adjacent landings, `so=1` teammate-on-the-goal, `sf=0`), so "no exchange"
  there is the predicate refusing, not the rule never being consulted.
- **Not that the scoped arm is P3-neutral by any argument other than whole-game inertness.** The
  green half is 0 by construction: an inert rule cannot change a command, so it cannot violate a
  property defined as a command-stream difference. Reporting it is a check that the inertness is
  real on the wire, not an independent neutrality result. §3.6 stands as written: **this is a
  scoping cost, not a neutrality claim.**
- **Not that the scoping is free.** The opposite, and it is now measured on both sides. The
  scoped arm gives up the exchange on eligible maps — the stated cost, dances there are untouched
  by Candidate 2 — and the red half's margin column shows the scoped arm also gives up **+39 net
  margin** across the nine firing views (up to +18 on `xm006:0`). That is a number for the G-1
  cost table, not an argument for switching the scoping off: switching it off buys those points
  by producing nine P3 violations, and P3 is a hard bar.
- **Nothing about the candidate arm's P3 status on non-eligible maps.** That is the still-open
  item 2 of the card — the P3 read on the candidate arm — and **P3 remains UNMEASURED, not
  passed**, in every table I publish until it is read. C-16 measures only the scoping.
