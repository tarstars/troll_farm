## 1. The accepted gate cannot read this candidate's wire

I turned `--p4b` ON on both arms. Both came back **`GATE_UNREADY` with 172 364 evaluator errors**,
which is an instrument failure and not a verdict — and emphatically not a 0.0 % pass.

Two independent causes, both measured rather than argued:

1. **Version refusal.** `p4b_gate` reads the resolver branch with `import narrate4`
   (`claude_1/pipeline/p4b_gate.py:387` for the CLI, `claude_1/pipeline/fuzz_panel.py:2443-2444`
   for the `--p4b` wiring). The candidate-2 arms narrate **v5**. `narrate4.decode` refuses any
   payload whose version token is not `v4`, so every telemetry row of every game is a decode error.
   Positive controls, run on a real payload taken off this corpus (`m000:0`, turn 1):
   - **G-A1** — `narrate4.decode` raises `GateError: unsupported NARRATE version 'v5', this
     decoder reads v4 only`. The refusal names the version, so it is a version refusal.
   - **G-A2** — `narrate5.decode` reads *the same bytes* cleanly. The gate cannot read this wire;
     the wire is not at fault.
2. **A dead numerator.** The accepted definition counts `branch in {H, W}`. **v5 retires `H`**:
   it is off-grammar in `narrate5.BRANCH_CODES` (`"PLRWNSX"`), a v5 payload carrying one is a
   decode error, and control C-9 pre-committed "no `H`". **G-H** measured it: **0 `H` turns on
   either arm** across 76 748 telemetry turns. So half of the accepted definition names a branch
   that cannot occur on this candidate. A version-agnostic decoder fixes cause 1 and not cause 2.

`p4b_gate.evaluate_rows` already takes its narrator as a **parameter**; only the two call sites
hardcode `narrate4`. Whether to make that a parameter of the wiring is a gate amendment and
codex_1's to make, not mine — I name the locus and change nothing.

`W` alone, and **G-X** requires that to equal my independent per-unit tally on every one of the 768
unit lives, and my tallies to equal `narrate5`'s own census. Both hold.

### The 16 episodes do not travel alone

**277 of the 384 unit lives on the candidate arm have no stall-evaluable window at all**

window), so the episode counter looked at **107** unit lives, not 384. 268 are blind on the
rule-off arm. That is the same shape as the P3 read's orchard guard: a number that is small partly

carry at least one forced `W`, against **49** on the rule-off arm — the rule leaves slightly *more*
trolls with a forced wait somewhere in their life while cutting the total from 562 turns to 293 and

Longest-run distribution is identical on both arms
(min 0 / q1 8 / median 14 / q3 22 / max 199), which is what says the difference between 27 and 16
is in the tail and not a shift of the whole population.

## 3. Two readings I measured and am NOT offering against the bar

Published because they are the obvious things to try, and because they are wrong in ways worth
recording once so nobody re-derives them:

- **R2, non-moving (`W` ∪ `N`): 87.34 % on the candidate arm.** `N` is "no MOVE this turn", and a
  troll issuing `CHOP`/`PICK`/`DROP` is an `N`. R2 is an upper bound on non-movement and **is not
  idleness**. Quoted as an idle share it is simply false.
- **R3, non-moving with a concrete target and no progress event: 10.51 % (candidate) vs 11.64 %
  (rule-off).** Conditioning on progress does not rescue it: a troll spending eleven turns felling
  a tree is an `N` with the tree concrete and available, and `progress_event` sees nothing until
  the tree falls, so productive work is counted as idle. **This is exactly why the accepted
  definition counts 60-turn episodes rather than a per-turn share** — the failure of R3 is an
  argument *for* P4b's shape, and I record it as one. R3 moves in the candidate's favour too
  (−1.13 pp, 292 → 294 above-bar unit lives — the one figure of the whole run that moves against
  the rule, by two unit lives, on a metric I have just argued is invalid).
