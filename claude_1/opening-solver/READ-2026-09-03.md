- **The free roster does better still**: a chop-3 third troll (2/3/0/3, harvest kept, the one the orchard

## 1. What was built and how it is checked

A planner for one player on a real map: the second troll bought from the starting draw, seeds planted
next door or next to water, wild trees harvested, iron mined, the third troll trained the turn its bill
clears. It is a fast copy of the referee (`world.py`) driven by a dispatcher with a handful of knobs,
swept per map; **every schedule it keeps is replayed through `sim/engine.py` and the two worlds agree
turn by turn** (inventory, positions, carries, every tree). The turn reported is the referee's TRAIN
turn, never the model's. No opponent is on the board in this version; the enemy enters through the raid
rate of §5 only, and the only enemy act modelled there is the one the owner named — it takes a tree.
Nothing is blocked by an opponent anywhere in the model.

Four variants per map-seat, all with the solver's own second troll except the last: a third troll of
chop 3 (`free`, 2/3/0/3), chop 2 (2/3/0/2), chop 1 (2/3/0/1); and `same` — orchard 6's own second and
third troll on that map-seat, so the comparison to orchard 6 is roster-for-roster.

## 2. The objective — the design round's first question

The owner's words, this morning: *the roster's chopping power online as early as possible, and a farm
sized so that we fell and bank it faster than the enemy raids it; the whole time-versus-power curve,
not one number.* That is what this page reports (§3, §5). My recommendation for the round, beside it:

- Keep the completion turn as the primary key but **make the target a roster, not a chop sum**: "a
  third troll of chop c" (c = 1, 2, 3) with the second troll free. A chop sum blurs a chop-3 second
  troll on turn 1 with a chop-3 third troll on turn 90, and the second troll's chop is a turn-1 draw
  question (what the draw affords), not a scheduling question.
- The tie-break should be **fruit points banked plus the trees planted** (their standing wood and their
  fruit rate): two schedules with the same completion turn differ almost only in the farm that will feed
  the fourth troll and the endgame.
- For stage 2 the objective needs a third term the offline read cannot fix: the value of the third
  troll's chop from its arrival to turn 300. A chop-3 troll at 58 versus chop-1 at 43 (§3): which is worth
  more depends on how many trees stand to be felled, so the choice of c belongs to the game-level
  valuation, not to the planner.

## 3. The optimal order in words, and the time-versus-power curve

The 400 free schedules share one shape (medians of the first turn each verb appears):

| turn | what |
|---|---|
| 1 | the starting troll MOVEs off the shack and the shack TRAINs the second troll from the draw (314 of 400 map-seats; on the other 86 the draw affords no troll and the second comes from the first harvests at turns 4–74, the middle half at 16–38) |
| 2 | PICK a seed (328 of 400 schedules plant at all) |
| 5 | PLANT it — next to water when a water cell is within reach (a lemon there bears in 12 turns instead of 32), next door otherwise |
| 9 | the first HARVEST of a full wild tree, the first DROP at the door |
| 10 | the first MINE (iron maps), against the third troll's iron bill only |
| … | both trolls gather, 14 harvests and 6 mines in the median schedule, nobody idle (WAIT 2 % of commands, only on a fast tree about to bear) |
| 58 | the third troll TRAINed the turn the pre-turn stock clears the bill (chop 3); 46 with chop 2; 43 with chop 1 |

The second troll the solver buys: harvest-2 chop-2 (2/2/1/1) on 95 map-seats, 1/2/1/1 on 60, 2/2/2/1
on 54 — always the most harvest the draw affords, never below 1/1/1/0; for a chop-2 third troll it
mostly settles for a cheap 1/1/1/0 (152 of 400), because the cheaper third-troll bill is met faster by
two cheap gatherers than by one dear one. Seeds: one lemon (next to water on 105 map-seats, next door on
70), none on 64, two or three on the rest. Walking is 64 % of all commands even in the optimum.

**The curve** (medians over all 400 map-seats; the third troll's chop is the power axis):

| third troll | completion turn (median, p25–p75) | bank at completion (points + own standing wood) |
|---|---|---|
| chop 1 (2/3/0/1) | **43** (36–53) | 25 |
| chop 2 (2/3/0/2) | **46** (38–63) | 25 |
| chop 3 (2/3/0/3) | **58** (47–77) | 25 |
| orchard 6's own roster, solver | 70 (59–79) | — |
| orchard 6's real games | 88.5 | — |

Chop 3 costs twelve turns over chop 2 at the median and thirty at the third quartile: the bill's iron is
what stretches it, on the maps where the iron is far. Each map-seat's own four numbers are in
`panel-summary.json`; the bank at completion is the same 25 for every chop because the schedule spends
everything on the bill and the seeds are still standing.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **one item a trip** (every troll brings home one unit) | **7** | 9.0 | 43 of 51 | 27 |
| **the late second troll** (not before the turn orchard 6 bought it) | 0 overall; **7** on the 30 map-seats where orchard 6 bought late | 5.2 | 23 of 51 | 32 |
| seeds next door only, never next to water | 0 | 2.1 | 18 of 51 | 18 |
| no wild tree beyond four steps | 0 | 0.9 | 14 of 48 | 26 |
| orchard 6's real games minus the solver, same roster, these 51 map-seats | **18** | — | p25–p75: 8–33 | — |

The four habits' mean costs sum to 17 turns against a **mean** gap of 21.7 on these 51 map-seats (the 18 in
the table is the median), so the habits account for most of the gap, not all of it; the remaining five
turns or so are search misses and habits the ablation does not name. **The one-item trip is the largest single cause** and the only one that costs on nearly every map;
the late second troll is the second (orchard 6 bought on turn 1 on 21 of these 51 map-seats, and where it
did not, the delay costs seven turns). The seeds' cells and the far wild trees cost little on their own
because the solver routes around them: the walk to a far tree is worth it only when the tree is full and
the troll carries the whole harvest home, which is exactly the trip orchard 6 never makes.

## 5. The farm/chop balance

From 6,200 planted trees in 175 collected games (orchard 6's 159 ladder games, both sides' trees, plus
16 top-player games), a tree planted within two steps of its owner's shack is felled by the enemy at:

| turns | raids per 100 tree-turns |
|---|---|
| 1–50 | 0.19 |
| 51–100 | 0.18 |
| 101–150 | 0.73 |
| 151–200 | 0.99 |
| 201–300 | 0.62 |

Of trees planted at distance ≤ 2 by turn 30: 8 % are raided by turn 60, 12.6 % by turn 100, 18.8 % by
turn 150. Orchard 6's own trees: 1,706 planted, 114 raided outright (6.68 %), 1,587 felled by itself (201 of
those with the enemy chopping too). Raids are
rare until turn 100 and four to five times more frequent after.

What a chop power P can convert (a derivation from the hazard, not a measurement): a mature plum or
lemon has 12 health, so a roster of total chop P fells about P/12 trees a turn while chopping; F mature
trees take ≈ 12F/P turns to fell, during which the expected raids are ≈ h·F·(12F/P)/2. Fewer than one
tree lost means F < √(P/(6h)):

| when | h | P = 2 | P = 3 | P = 4 | P = 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before turn 100 | 0.0019 | 13 | 16 | 19 | 23 |
| after turn 100 | 0.008 | 6 | 8 | 9 | 11 |

So before turn 100 the raid rate does not bound the farm at all (the seeds and the trolls' walking
do); after turn 100 a farm of about 8 near trees per 3 points of chop is what can be felled before the
enemy takes one. Walking to and from the trees is not in this figure; it makes the bound smaller. The
enemy in this model does one thing, fell a tree we planted; it never blocks a cell.

## 6. Speed

Python, one laptop core: the model runs **~64 rollouts a second** (a rollout = one plan played to the
third troll, ~60–90 turns); the free variant spends a median **30 s** per map-seat (1,800 rollouts),
all four variants **78 s**. That is 30–80× over the bot's first second in Python. A single plan
evaluated deterministically (stage A of the driver) costs ~15 ms, so **one second buys the deterministic
sweep of all ~60 plans plus ~5 randomised refinements** — in Python. In Rust the same model runs
30–100× faster, comfortably inside the first second with the full sweep; the 50 ms re-check is one
deterministic replay of the remaining plan, ~1 ms.

## 7. Recommendation

**Rules first, the planner second.** Three rules carry most of the 21 turns and fit in the champion's
source as they stand: (1) turn 1 — MOVE off the shack and TRAIN the best second troll the draw affords;
(2) every trip home carries what the troll can carry, a full wild tree's three fruits or the whole
mining haul, never one item; (3) a seed goes to the nearest free cell next to water if one is within a
few steps, else next door, and the picking troll plants it on the way to its next tree. The planner
itself (stage 2) is what decides *which* wild tree and *when* to mine on each map — that is the
remaining turns beyond the three rules (which tree is full when, where the iron is) and it wants the map. I recommend stage 2 as a
Rust port of `world.py` + the dispatcher, the deterministic sweep in the first second, the plan
followed as a script and re-checked each turn against the board.
