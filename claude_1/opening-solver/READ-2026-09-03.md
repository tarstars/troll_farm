## 1. What was built and how it is checked

A planner for one player on a real map: the second troll bought from the starting draw, seeds
planted next door or next to water, wild trees harvested, iron mined, the third troll trained the
turn its bill clears. It is a fast copy of the referee (`world.py`) driven by a dispatcher with a
handful of knobs, swept per map; **every schedule it keeps is replayed through `sim/engine.py` and
the two worlds agree turn by turn** (inventory, positions, carries, every tree). The turn reported
is the referee's TRAIN turn, never the model's.

## 2. The objective — the design round's first question

The coordinator's frame: (1) the turn at which the roster's chop power reaches P, then (2) the bank
in points plus the standing wood of our own trees at that turn; the whole curve time-versus-P.
This is what stage 1 reports. My recommendation beside it, for the round:

- Keep (1) as the primary key but **make P a roster, not a sum**: "a third troll of chop c"
  (c = 1, 2, 3) with the second troll free. A chop sum blurs a chop-3 second troll on turn 1 with
  a chop-3 third troll on turn 90, and the second troll's chop is a turn-1 draw question, not a
  scheduling question.
- The tie-break (2) should be **fruit points banked plus the trees planted (their standing wood
  and their fruit rate)**, because at completion the difference between two schedules with the same
  completion turn is almost entirely the farm that will feed the fourth troll and the endgame.
- For the bot (stage 2) the objective needs a third term the offline read cannot fix: the value of
  the third troll's chop from its arrival to turn 300. A troll of chop 3 at turn 95 versus chop 2 at
  turn 70: which is worth more depends on how many trees stand to be felled, so the P sweep should
  be handed to the game-level valuation, not decided in the planner.

## 5. The farm/chop balance

From 6,200 planted trees in 175 collected games (orchard 6's 159 ladder games, both sides' trees,
plus 16 top-player games; `raid_rate.py`, `raid-rate.json`), a tree planted within two steps of its
owner's shack is felled by the enemy at:

| turns | raids per 100 tree-turns |
|---|---|
| 1–50 | 0.19 |
| 51–100 | 0.18 |
| 101–150 | 0.73 |
| 151–200 | 0.99 |
| 201–300 | 0.62 |

Of trees planted at distance ≤ 2 by turn 30: 8 % are raided by turn 60, 12.6 % by turn 100, 18.8 %
by turn 150. Orchard 6's own trees: 1,706 planted (all but nine at distance 1), 109 raided
(6.4 %), 1,583 felled by itself. Raids are rare until turn 100 and four to five times more frequent
after.

What a chop power P can convert (a derivation from the hazard, not a measurement): a mature plum or
lemon has 12 health, so a roster of total chop P fells about P/12 trees a turn while chopping; F
mature trees take ≈ 12F/P turns to fell, during which the expected raids are ≈ h·F·(12F/P)/2. Fewer
than one tree lost means F < √(P/(6h)):

| when | h | P = 2 | P = 3 | P = 4 | P = 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before turn 100 | 0.0019 | 13 | 16 | 19 | 23 |
| after turn 100 | 0.008 | 6 | 8 | 9 | 11 |

So before turn 100 the raid rate does not bound the farm at all (the seeds and the trolls' walking
do); after turn 100 a farm of about 8 near trees per 3 points of chop is what can be felled before
the enemy takes one. Walking to and from the trees is not in this figure; it makes the bound smaller.
