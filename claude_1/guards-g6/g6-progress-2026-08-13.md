## D-8 (b) cannot be pinned, and that is the finding — not a gap in the fixture

`detect_d8` only evaluates `p.kind == "BANANA"` (`:1115`) inside
`if c in tr.diag and c in alive_per_turn[t]`. But `own_banana_history` builds `alive_per_turn[t]`
from **the same `state(t)`**, keeping only cells where `plant_at(c).kind == "BANANA"`. So the kind
test is **true whenever it is evaluated**: D8-M8, which deletes it, is an **equivalent mutant** and
no fixture can kill it.

Established two ways rather than argued once:

- **By construction** — a trace whose own-planted DIAG banana becomes a `WOOD` plant at t3: the
  cell leaves the alive set at exactly the turn its kind changes, so the CHOP at t3 never reaches
  the guard. No episode either way.
- **By differential** — the mutant applied to a copy of the module and run against the whole
  seeded probe corpus: **0 of 416 traces differ** in D-8 output.

**Its survival is therefore not evidence of weak coverage, and pinning it is not possible.** I have
left D8-M8 **counted** in the totals and the row at `NO_FIXTURE`, so this work cannot flatter its
own numbers. The disposition — exclude it as `D3-M4-RETIRED` is excluded, or add an `EQUIVALENT`
label — changes the denominator of the headline kill rate, which makes it a measurement-semantics
call. **Referred to the coordinator; not taken unilaterally.**

What I did add is `test_alive_set_already_guarantees_the_plant_kind`, which pins the *coupling*
rather than pretending to pin the branch: if `own_banana_history` ever stops filtering on kind, the
guard becomes load-bearing and that test fails to say so.

ownership clause instead of the clause it names. Every scenario above flips ownership first.

## The water branch is where the leverage was

`(f)` selects the planting cooldown: `CD_wet = 4` next to water, `CD_dry = 6` otherwise. That
number sets the orthogonal-slot planting deadline `T_late = 300 - (2*CD + ceil(4/chop) + 2)` —
**282 dry, 286 wet**. The audit had recorded this branch as unfixturable because *"the fixture map
has no water"*. That framing was the obstacle: the discriminating window is turn **283–286**, where
a dry slot is already illegal and a wet one is still legal, and the *dry* half needs no water at
all — the mutant collapses the choice to always-wet, so a dry cell planted at 284 stops being
flagged.

So the pair is one turn, one cell, two maps differing by a single `~` tile: **dry at 284 fires,
wet at 284 is silent.** The PASS on the wet map is itself the evidence that `near_water` was true,
because a dry cell at that turn fails the other test. I added `MAP_ROWS_WATER` and a
`make_trace_on` helper rather than changing the default map, so no existing fixture moved.

**Pinning an exact turn on both sides of a deadline turned out to pin the deadline's arithmetic
too.** D5-M2 (`2*CD` → `1*CD`, deadline 282 → 288), D5-M3 (slack `+2` → `+20`, 282 → 264, which
makes the *wet* case fire) and D5-M7 (global slack `+1` → `+40`, 296 → 257, which also makes the
wet case fire) are all now caught. Those are branches (d) and (e), which had been `UNPINNED` with
all three mutants surviving since the audit opened. I extended their `owner_test_classes` and
report them as incidental: **three of the six new catches this round were not the ones I set out
to make**, and `caught_by_expected` read 36 of 39 until I named the owners.

The general lesson, worth keeping: **a fixture that asserts an exact boundary value constrains
every term in the formula that produces it.** The earlier D-5 fixture planted at turn 299 — far
past every deadline — so it could not tell a correct deadline from one 30 turns out. Distance from
the boundary is what made it weak, not absence of a test.

## Finding for the coordinator: clause (a1) is close to inert, by construction

The clause is `opp_h <= min_own`, where `min_own` is the minimum ETA over **all** own units at
the planting turn. **The planting unit is by definition standing on the cell it plants**, so its
ETA is 0 and `min_own` is *always* 0 at a PLANT event. The clause can therefore only fire when
`opp_h == 0` — an opponent harvester sharing that exact square.

That is why every pre-G6 fixture left D6-M3, D6-M4 and D6-M6 alive: the existing D-6 traces put
the opponent one or more steps away, where the clause cannot fire whatever it says. It is not
strictly unreachable — unlike D-8 (b) it *can* be triggered — so it is pinnable, and I have pinned
it with a same-cell opponent. But **in ordinary play the branch is close to dead**, and a reader
of the spec text ("`eta_opp_h(c,t) <= min_u eta_u(c,t)`") would not guess that.

**Referred, not repaired.** Whether the intended reading is "minimum over own units *other than*
the planter", or ETA measured from the turn *after* the plant, is a detector-semantics question and
G6 changes no predicate. Flagging it because a clause that can only fire in a degenerate geometry
is exactly the "mechanism that cannot fail" shape this task exists to find — here it is a mechanism
that can *barely* fire.

## Speed is what made (a3) observable

`eta = ceil(bfs_distance / speed)`. **At speed 1 the ETA and the raw distance are the same
number**, so every pre-G6 fixture — all speed-1 units — could not distinguish the formula from the
bare distance, and D6-M7 sailed through. Both new cases use a speed-2 chopper: distance 4 → ETA 2
is inside the `<= 2` bound and flagged; distance 5 → ETA 3 is outside it and silent.

That silent half also killed **D6-M2** (`<= 6`) and **D6-M8** (`<= 5`) incidentally, because an ETA
of 3 falls inside both widened bounds — which promotes clause (a2) from `PARTIAL` to `PINNED`. Same
pattern as the D-5 deadline pair: **an assertion that a value sits just OUTSIDE a bound constrains
how far that bound may be moved.** Owners extended and reported; `caught_by_expected` read 44 of 46
until I named them.

## The D-1 clauses suppress episodes, so their fixtures assert SILENCE

`progress(t)` decides whether a turn counted as real work; an oscillation is only reported when
no progress occurs throughout the run. Deleting a progress source therefore makes the detector
flag **more**, not less, and the fixture for such a clause is an innocent trace that must stay
quiet.

That is a weaker kind of evidence unless it is set up carefully — silence proves nothing if the
trace would have been silent anyway. So `TestD1Uncovered` opens with
`test_a_plain_pacing_run_is_an_oscillation`: the same pacing with **no** progress event does fire.
Only against that baseline does the silence in the other two tests mean the clause did the
suppressing. **A test asserting absence needs a companion proving the presence was available.**

The pair also caught **D1-M7** (`k>=3` → `k>=2`) incidentally: breaking a 10-turn pace in the
middle leaves two shorter runs, and a lowered threshold re-reports one of them. Row (b) stays
`PARTIAL` — D1-M2 and D1-M8 are still alive — but its evidence is stronger.

## D-4 (e) is the second proven-impossible branch

have an observable consequence:

3. The only residue is `nd_run = 0` — and every commitment start sets `nd_run = 0` anyway, so

The interval is born and closed within a single turn having emitted nothing. **Differential:
0 of 416 probe-corpus traces differ under D4-M6.** As with D-8 (b), I left the mutant **counted**

pins the two facts the equivalence rests on, so if DROP ever becomes a banned verb, or the
same-turn clear is removed, the branch becomes load-bearing and the test fails to say so.
